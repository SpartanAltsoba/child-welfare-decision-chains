## v1.1.1 — 2026-09-21

### Case law added

- **Iowa, court-ordered referral (INP-04):** seven authorities, including *In the Interest of G.R.*, 348 N.W.2d 627 (Iowa 1984), *In re the Marriage of Reschly*, 334 N.W.2d 720 (Iowa 1983) and *Webster v. ### Fields

## v1.1.0 — 2026-09-21

Feature release (MINOR): the case-law layer was rebuilt for every jurisdiction and added to the federal baseline. No jurisdictions or node types were added or removed.

### Case-law layer rebuilt

- **What was wrong.** In v1.0.x, `layers.case_law.key_cases` held recent Justia opinion listings selected by title keywords, with synthetic citations, no holdings, and the same list pasted into every node of a jurisdiction regardless of topic. Many entries were unrelated to child welfare.
- **What it is now.** Each decision point carries authorities selected for that point. Every opinion was retrieved from CourtListener and read; `holding` and `quote` come from the majority opinion text; `citation` is the official reporter citation in Bluebook form; `relevance` says why the authority matters at that node; `url` and `courtlistener_cluster_id` point to the opinion. The layer records `method` and `verified_date`. A node with no verified on-point authority says so in `_note` rather than carrying a placeholder.
- **How it was verified.** An independent adversarial pass refetched every cluster and opinion and attempted to refute each entry (citation match, exact-quote check against the majority opinion, holding not inverted or overstated, on-point for the node); rejected entries were removed. A supplement pass retrieved the leading authorities the verifier named as missing and re-verified them. Method and tooling: `data/tools/qa/caselaw/README.md`; the reconciled briefs and per-node digests are committed under `data/tools/qa/caselaw/briefs/` for provenance.
- **New fields** (additive, within case entries): `holding`, `quote`, `relevance`, `date_decided`, `url`, `courtlistener_cluster_id`; on the layer: `method`, `verified_date`, `_note`. These follow the schema's `case_law_reference` shape.
- **Federal baseline** nodes, which had no case-law layer, now carry the U.S. Supreme Court and circuit authorities that govern each decision point nationally.

### Per-jurisdiction corrections

### AK

- Mandatory reporters were rebuilt from the statute. The generic seven-item list is replaced by the ten occupational categories actually enumerated in Alaska Stat. § 47.17.020(a), each carrying its own subsection cite. Foster parents were removed because they do not appear in the statute, and six real categories were added: peace and corrections officers, administrative officers of institutions, domestic violence and sexual assault program employees, child fatality and multidisciplinary team members, school volunteers serving over four hours a week, and juvenile probation and facility staff.

- The athletic-coach entry now carries the statute's carve-out: an unpaid volunteer coach is not required to report unless the coach volunteers more than four hours a week for four consecutive weeks or more than 20 hours a week in a one-month period, has received Alaska Stat. § 47.17.022 training, and has signed a form acknowledging the duty (Alaska Stat. § 47.17.020(j)).

- Reporting details in the twelve intake nodes replaced "Not applicable" with Alaska's real posture: an enumerated-profession state, not a universal-reporter state. Reports run to the nearest office of the Department of Family and Community Services, the department in which the Office of Children's Services sits (Alaska Stat. § 47.17.290(6)); "immediately" means no later than 24 hours (§ 47.17.290(7)); a peace-officer fallback applies under § 47.17.020(c); a parallel immediate duty to law enforcement attaches when the harm appears to result from a suspected sex offense; and reporting by everyone else is permissive under § 47.17.020(b). Contact channels are the hotline at 1-800-478-4444 or 907-269-6510 (24 hours a day), ReportChildAbuse@alaska.gov, and fax 907-269-3939.

- Timeline requirements gained real statutory numbers. The emergency removal hearing is now 48 hours from the court being notified (Alaska Stat. § 47.10.142(d)), replacing a 24-72 hour estimate measured from removal. Newly added: the 24-hour parental notice of emergency custody and the 24-hour child-in-need-of-aid petition deadline (§ 47.10.142(c)), and the 120-day adjudication hearing clock running from entry of probable cause (§ 47.10.080(a)).

- The appeal deadline in the six failure nodes was an unsourced national template of 30 days from "Final order." It is now 15 days, running from the date shown in the clerk's certificate of distribution on the final judgment in the child-in-need-of-aid case (Alaska R. App. P. 218(a)(3), (d)). Rule 204(a)(1)'s general 30 days expressly yields to the shorter window. Rule 218 states no consequence for a miss, so none is asserted; the repealed 7 AAC 54.215 and 7 AAC 54.220 and the surviving 7 AAC 54.255(b)(7) supply no deadline either.

- Extended foster care in the six outcome nodes was previously phrased as an automatic agency benefit. It now states what the statute does: state custody ends at the 19th birthday unless the court grants one-year extensions past 19, which require the person's own consent and a best-interests finding, custody may run to the 21st birthday by court order only, and a person already released from care may ask the court to resume custody before the 21st birthday (Alaska Stat. § 47.10.080(c)(1)(B), § 47.10.080(v), § 47.10.100(a)).

- Three dead legis.state.ak.us folio URLs were retired across all 42 nodes and repointed to live akleg.gov sections. The mislabeled "Title 18 - Child Abuse Reporting" entry now cites Alaska Stat. § 18.85.100, Title 25 is narrowed to Chapter 23 (Adoption), and Title 47 drops an extraction artifact in favor of chapters 10 and 17. All verification dates on the five shared citations in every node were refreshed to 2026-09-19.

- The Alaska Stat. § 18.85.100 citation text was truncated. It now carries the full catchline, "Right to representation, services, and facilities," plus the subsection (e) mechanics: the Public Defender Agency may represent a parent at the Alaska Stat. § 47.10.142(d) temporary custody hearing at public expense without a court order, and if the parent is not later found eligible for court-appointed counsel the court shall assess the cost of that representation against the parent. The clawback now travels with the citation in every node.

- The state hotline field now reads "1-800-478-4444 or 907-269-6510 (24 hours a day, every day)" in all 42 nodes; the agency name, acronym and website are unchanged.

Stat. § 48.981 itself, rather than the whole of Chapter 48, and the second points at the Chapter 48 Children's Code page, rather than the bare legis.wisconsin.gov home page, and is now flagged as a deep link. Both were re-verified 19 SEP 2026.

Mandatory reporters in the twelve intake nodes replaced eight generic uncited categories ("Teachers/School Personnel," "Medical/Dental Professionals," and the like) with 16 statute-keyed entries drawn from Wis. Stat. § 48.981(2): medical and mental health professionals ((2)(a)1., 1m., 4.–9.); coroners and medical examiners ((2)(a)2., 3.); social workers, marriage and family therapists and professional counselors ((2)(a)10.–12.); public assistance workers including financial and employment planners ((2)(a)13.); school teachers, administrators, counselors and other school employees ((2)(a)14.–16., 16m.); mediators under § 767.405 ((2)(a)17.); child care workers and providers ((2)(a)18., 19.); AODA and genetic counselors ((2)(a)20., 20m.); treatment staff under §§ 46.23, 51.42 or 51.437 or a residential care center ((2)(a)21.); physical therapists, physical therapist assistants, occupational therapists and dietitians ((2)(a)22., 22m., 23., 24.); speech-language pathologists and audiologists ((2)(a)25., 26.); EMS practitioners and emergency medical responders ((2)(a)27., 28.); police and law enforcement officers and juvenile correctional officers ((2)(a)29., 30.); court-appointed special advocates as to children seen under § 48.236(3) ((2)(b)); clergy with the confessional exception ((2)(bm)1.–3.); and any other person, including an attorney, on a permissive basis only ((2)(c)).

Four template-estimate timelines were replaced with sourced Wisconsin deadlines. The twelve intake nodes carry a firm "Within 24 hours" initial response running from receipt of the report (§ 48.981(3)(c)1.a.) and a 60-day investigation completion clock that runs from report receipt rather than from when the investigation opened (§ 48.981(3)(c)4.). The six decision nodes take the same 60-day clock for the disposition finding, plus a same-day safety response for reports suggesting immediate danger, drawn from the Department of Children and Families' published CPS process standard.

The six action nodes replaced a guessed 24-to-72-hour emergency removal hearing with the statute's 48 hours excluding weekends and legal holidays, measured from the decision to hold the child, and carry the statute's own consequence: the child shall be released (§ 48.21(1)(a)).

Extended foster care in the six outcome nodes no longer promises age 21 to every youth. Wis. Stat. § 48.366 applies only to a full-time student with an IEP in effect and ends at the diploma or the 21st birthday, whichever comes first. The extended foster care flag stays true, because narrow care past 18 does exist.

The law-enforcement referral intake node gained one timeline row, with its two existing rows untouched: referral to the child welfare agency within 12 hours, exclusive of Saturdays, Sundays or legal holidays, running from the report received by the sheriff or police department (§ 48.981(3)(a)2.), corroborated by the department's published CPS process page. The subdivision states no penalty, so none was invented.

The six failure nodes gained four substantiation-track rows, with their two existing rows untouched: written notice of a final substantiation determination within 5 days of the final determination (§ 48.981(3)(c)5m.); a written ch. 227 contested case hearing request within 10 days of that notice; hearing commencement within 90 days of the department's receipt of the request; and a final decision within 60 days of the close of the hearing (all § 48.981(3)(c)5p.). Neither subdivision states a penalty, so all four record the consequence as not specified. The twelve intake nodes gained a tribal notice entry alongside the existing parent and legal guardian entry: notice to the tribe within 24 hours, limited by the statute's own geographic and knowledge conditions — a county containing a federally recognized reservation or the Ho-Chunk BIA service area, and knowledge or reason to know that the child is an Indian child residing in the county or an Indian unborn child whose expectant mother resides there. Required content tracks the statute's "shall consist only of" clause: the name and address of the Indian child or expectant mother, and the fact that a report has been received. The ICWA additional notice flag is true, and the consequence is recorded as not specified in Wis. Stat. § 48.981(3)(bm).

### WV

West Virginia, all 42 nodes, corrected against the West Virginia Code and the Bureau for Social Services' published intake guidance.

All four state statutory citations now point at first-party deep links on the official code site (code.wvlegislature.gov) instead of a bare host and two third-party links, each confirmed to render the exact chapter, article or section named in its citation text. One replaced link mattered more than a dead one would have: the old ChapterEntire.cfm link for Chapter 49, Article 4 silently redirected to Chapter 1, Article 4 (Seat of Government of Political Subdivisions), serving the wrong law without an error.

Citation labels were corrected to West Virginia's own nomenclature — the code is organized in chapters, not titles — and the child welfare citation now names the Department of Human Services, the agency name carried in the statute itself since the 2024 amendments, in place of the retired DHHR.

Mandatory reporters were rebuilt from W. Va. Code § 49-2-803(a) across the twelve intake nodes: a generic 10-entry template became the statute's 14 actual categories, adding social service workers, humane officers, Division of Juvenile Services employees, youth camp staff, organized-activity volunteers and commercial film processors, and dropping "Shelter Staff," which appears nowhere in the statute. West Virginia is confirmed a non-universal reporting state.

Two timeline requirements moved off template guesswork onto statute. CPS initial response in the twelve intake nodes now states both statutory tracks from § 49-2-802(c) — an immediate response with a face-to-face interview within 72 hours for imminent danger or serious physical abuse, and within 14 days otherwise — where the template's flat "24-72 hours" had understated the non-emergency track by nearly two weeks. The emergency removal hearing in the six action nodes now reflects § 49-4-601(c): a preliminary hearing within 10 days of the custody order with at least 5 days' actual notice, against a template value of "24-72 hours" that contradicted three separate provisions.

Extended foster care in the six outcome nodes is now stated with its conditions per § 49-1-202 — 18 to under 21, requiring agency custody or an abuse or neglect adjudication at 18, plus a contract to continue a program begun before the eighteenth birthday — replacing a flat "up to age 21" that read as automatic.

The reporting detail field in the twelve intake nodes, previously the placeholder "Not applicable," now carries a statutory summary. Mandated reporters listed in W. Va. Code § 49-2-803(a) must report immediately, and not more than 24 hours after suspecting the abuse or neglect, to the Department of Human Services; reporting is not universal, since any other person may report but is not required to (§ 49-2-803(c)). Reports go to a centralized intake maintained by the Bureau for Social Services, reachable through two channels — a 24-hour, seven-day-a-week hotline, or web-based reporting by email, electronic fax, fillable form or other electronic form that sends the report to a live person to handle it immediately — and both systems give the reporter a specific case identifier immediately upon making a report (§ 49-2-809(a)). A reporter who believes the child suffered serious physical abuse, sexual abuse or sexual assault shall also immediately report to the State Police and any law-enforcement agency having jurisdiction (§ 49-2-803(a)). Knowing failure to report is a misdemeanor punishable by not more than 90 days in jail or a fine of not more than $5,000 (§ 49-2-812(a)), rising to not more than six months or not more than $10,000 where the child is known or suspected to be sexually assaulted or sexually abused, or a student is known or suspected to have been the victim of non-consensual sexual contact, sexual intercourse or sexual intrusion on school premises (§ 49-2-812(b)).

The hotline in all 42 nodes changed from "1-800-352-6513" to "1-800-352-6513, option 2," per the Bureau for Social Services centralized intake page, whose only phone-reporting instruction reads "report immediately by phone to 1-800-352-6513, option 2" in a sentence about a child in imminent danger — confirming that option 2 is the child branch rather than the adult protective services branch the same page also covers. Without the menu option a caller lands in a phone tree. The legacy DHHR abuse-and-neglect page no longer resolves, so the Bureau is the only live publisher and no competing dialing instruction exists. The agency name, acronym and website are unchanged.

### WY

Wyoming, all 42 nodes, corrected against Title 14 of the Wyoming Statutes and the Wyoming Rules of Appellate Procedure. Statutory citations were relabeled. The Article 2 citation now reads "Wyo. Stat. §§ 14-3-201 through 14-3-216 (Article 2, Child Protective Services – § 14-3-205(a) duty to report, which applies to any person)," dropping the nonexistent "Child Protective Services Act" short title, and the Article 4 citation now names the real "Child Protection Act" and its juvenile court process. Both verification dates were advanced to 2026-09-19 against the official Wyoming Legislature Title 14 publication.

Mandatory reporters in all twelve intake nodes replaced a 13-profession template list with Wyoming's actual universal-reporter rule: any person under § 14-3-205(a), plus the § 14-3-205(b) institutional duty to notify the person in charge, who must also report.

Two timelines moved onto statute. Initial response time in the intake nodes went from a 24-to-72-hour template estimate to the statutory 24 hours from notification (§ 14-3-204(a)(iii)), and the emergency removal hearing in the action nodes went to 48 hours excluding weekends and legal holidays from the taking of temporary protective custody (§ 14-3-409(a)). Both are now sourced as state statute, and the invented consequences ("case dismissal," "CFSR deficiency") were replaced with what the statute actually says.

The disposition finding in the six decision nodes replaced a template estimate (30-60 days from when the investigation opened, consequence "Procedural violation") with the statutory rule: "Immediately after the decree, or at a postponed hearing within 60 days," running from the point at which the court or jury finds the child neglected after an adjudicatory hearing, a valid admission or a confession (Wyo. Stat. § 14-3-426(c)). The consequence records that § 14-3-426 specifies none, and notes that subsection (e) lets the court continue a disposition hearing for a reasonable time not to exceed sixty days.

The appeal deadline in the six failure nodes keeps its 30-day figure, now with sourced provenance and consequence: it runs from entry of the appealable juvenile court order, judgment or decree, since Wyo. Stat. § 14-3-432(a) applies the Wyoming Rules of Appellate Procedure and W.R.A.P. 2.01(a) sets the 30 days, and timely filing of a notice of appeal is jurisdictional (W.R.A.P. 1.03(a)). The row is classed as a court rule rather than a state statute, because the deadline lives in a Supreme Court rule.

Extended foster care in the outcome nodes no longer claims a flat "up to age 21." It now states that IV-E eligibility ends at 18, or 19 only if the youth is graduating, per the Department of Family Services IV-E manual, while the court may order care or transitional services to 21 under § 14-3-431(b).

Door scenarios were corrected and made consistent statewide. The action nodes' generic "hearing within 24-72 hours" right became the accurate 48-hour shelter care hearing. The intake caseworker scenarios gained two Wyoming-specific rights: to be told the specific complaints or allegations at the initial time of contact (§ 14-3-204(a)(iii)), and the fact that a Department of Family Services caseworker cannot take a child absent a law enforcement officer, a treating physician, physician's assistant or nurse practitioner, or a court order (§ 14-3-405). Both entries were then carried, in identical wording, to the decision nodes, which previously held no Wyoming-specific content at all, and the § 14-3-405 limit was added to the action nodes, where the question of who may lawfully take a child matters most. The § 14-3-204(a)(iii) advisement was deliberately not added to the action nodes, since that duty attaches at the initial contact of an investigation or assessment, not at placement; § 14-3-208(a) independently corroborates that the department receives transferred custody rather than taking it.

Three intake summaries were corrected: one no longer says "designated professionals," another notes that a citizen report is mandatory in Wyoming rather than voluntary, and a third records that Wyoming operates no statewide hotline (§ 14-3-206(b) is permissive) and directs reporters to the county Department of Family Services field office or 911.

The deep-link flag on the first two citations in all 42 nodes was withdrawn, from true to false. The source link is unchanged and remains the correct first-party citation; only the claim that it is a section-level deep link is dropped, because the Title 14 publication carries no URL fragment and exposes no named destinations or bookmarks across its full coverage of Title 14, so a section-level anchor is not constructible, and wyoleg.gov serves no per-section page to link to. The other rows in the same citation list are unchanged.

One item is not in this release: the expanded "Right to attorney" wording across 24 intake, action and decision nodes. Those entries remain the original bare "Right to attorney."

### Known issues carried to v1.1.0 (not corrected in this release)

- **Case-law layer relevance.** `layers.case_law.key_cases` was keyword-matched at build time and attaches off-topic opinions in many jurisdictions (verified examples in AR, DE, GA, HI, IA, IN, KS, KY). A dedicated case-law pass with human-readable relevance checks is planned.
- **Schema shape mismatch (pre-existing since v1.0.0).** `layers.constitutional.*` and `layers.federal.*` entries are strings where `extended_decision_chain.schema.json` expects objects, and `notice_requirements[].who_must_be_notified` carries values outside the schema's enum. Files did not validate at v1.0.0 and still do not; the schema and data will be reconciled in v1.1.0.
- **Backfill poster URLs.** `<ST>_backfill.json` files carry NCMEC poster links that change over time; they are not maintained as citations.
- **Administrative-rule citations.** Several jurisdictions cite regulation index pages or a superseded agency; deep links to the current rule sections are a v1.1.0 item.
- **Justia links.** law.justia.com and regulations.justia.com return HTTP 403 to automated fetches (Cloudflare challenge) but load in a browser. They are live and were left in place; they are not verifiable by script.
- **Full per-jurisdiction list.** Every item the verifiers judged out of scope for a correction release, with the reason, is in `data/tools/qa/carried_to_v1.1.0.json`.

### How this release was produced

Every jurisdiction was re-researched by an AI writer against first-party sources (state code, agency pages, agency policy manuals), then challenged by an independent adversarial verifier that refetched every cited URL and attempted to refute each claim; changes that failed verification were reverted. Dataset-wide corrections were applied by `data/tools/qa/apply_verified_v1_0_1.py` from `verified_agency.json` and `url_fixes.json` (both committed, with provenance). The manifest is rebuilt by the committed `data/tools/qa/build_manifest.py`, which documents the root-hash formula. Project Milk Carton reviewed and released.
