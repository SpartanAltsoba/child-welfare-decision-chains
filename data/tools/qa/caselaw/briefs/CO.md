# CO — 40 authorities, verified 2026-09-20

## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **L.J. v. Carricato**, 2018 COA 3, 413 P.3d 1280
  Holding: The division held that the Child Protection Act and the Colorado Governmental Immunity Act can be read together, so that a public-employee mandatory reporter is immune from liability for reporting or failing to report child abuse unless the conduct was willful and wanton, and remanded for a Trinity hearing on that question.
  Why here: This is the controlling Colorado statement of the scope and limits of mandatory-reporter immunity under C.R.S. § 19-3-309, which is what makes the reporting duty at this node enforceable without exposing the reporter to suit.
  Quote: “persons who report child abuse under section 19-3-304 are immune from liability unless their conduct is willful, wanton, and malicious.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4461396/lj-v-carricato/
- **Berges v. County Court of Douglas County**, 2016 COA 146, 409 P.3d 592
  Holding: The division held that section 19-3-206's grant of exclusive authority to county attorneys over article 3 proceedings does not strip district attorneys of their authority to criminally prosecute mandatory reporters who willfully fail to report under section 19-3-304.
  Why here: This case establishes that a mandatory reporter's willful failure to report is a criminal misdemeanor prosecutable by the district attorney, which is the enforcement mechanism behind the reporting duty at this node.
  Quote: “section 19-3-304 does not set forth a proceeding under article 3; it simply defines an offense.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4311747/berges-v-county-court-of-douglas-county/
- **Trinity Broadcasting of Denver, Inc. v. City of Westminster**, 848 P.2d 916 (Colo. 1993)
  Holding: The court reversed the summary judgment on the Governmental Immunity Act notice issue and remanded, holding that a pretrial governmental-immunity challenge is a jurisdictional question for the trial court under C.R.C.P. 12(b)(1), on which the court may permit limited discovery and must hold an evidentiary hearing when the jurisdictional facts are disputed, rather than resolving it on summary judgment or reserving it for the jury.
  Why here: This is the decision that supplies the procedure — the 'Trinity hearing' — a trial court must use to resolve a mandatory reporter's or agency employee's Governmental Immunity Act defence, which is exactly what L.J. v. Carricato ordered on remand.
  Quote: “When a public entity claims before trial that timely notice was not given to it under the Governmental Immunity Act, the trial court is the finder of fact.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1376376/trinity-broadcasting-of-denver-inc-v-city-of-westminster/
- **Martinez v. Estate of Bleck**, 2016 CO 58, 379 P.3d 315
  Holding: The court reversed and remanded, holding that a public employee who asserts that his conduct was not willful and wanton under section 24-10-118(2)(a) is claiming sovereign immunity and may appeal its denial, that the trial court must resolve all issues bearing on that immunity claim including factual ones at a Trinity hearing rather than merely test the pleadings, and that willful and wanton conduct requires conscious disregard of the danger rather than negligence.
  Why here: It supplies the substantive standard and the procedure for the willful-and-wanton exception that strips a public-employee mandatory reporter of Governmental Immunity Act immunity under the rule stated in L.J. v. Carricato.
  Quote: “willful and wanton conduct is not merely negligent; instead, it must exhibit a conscious disregard for the danger.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4255994/martinez-v-estate-of-bleck-ex-rel-churchill/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Jones v. Hunt**, 410 F.3d 1221 (10th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The Tenth Circuit reversed a grant of qualified immunity, holding that the alleged coercive detention of a teenager at school by a social services official and a deputy, to compel her to change her residence, stated a violation of her clearly established Fourth Amendment right to be free from unreasonable seizure.
  Why here: The seizure was carried out jointly by a child welfare official and a deputy sheriff, the classic joint-response posture at this node.
  Quote: “the prohibition against unreasonable seizures extends to civil, as well as criminal, investigations by the government.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/165973/jones-v-hunt/
- **Malik v. Arapahoe County Department of Social Services**, 191 F.3d 1306 (10th Cir. 1999)
  Holding: The Tenth Circuit affirmed the denial of qualified immunity, holding that procuring an ex parte child custody order through distortion, misrepresentation and omission, and in retaliation for a parent's retaining counsel, violated clearly established Fourth, First and Fourteenth Amendment rights.
  Why here: The opinion holds that law enforcement and caseworkers may seek protective custody where they genuinely believe the child is in danger, but not to circumvent a lawyer's attempt to negotiate interview conditions.
  Quote: “it was clearly established law that, except in extraordinary circumstances, a parent has a liberty interest in familial association and privacy that cannot be violated without adequate pre-deprivation procedures.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/158631/malik-v-arapahoe-county-department-of-social-services/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **S.L. v. District Court**, 676 P.2d 12 (Colo. 1984)
  Holding: The court held that a juvenile court exceeds its jurisdiction if it transfers legal custody of a child from a parent to the county department at a pre-adjudicatory shelter hearing; at that stage the court may only continue shelter or release the child, and a temporary protective custody order reaches physical custody only.
  Why here: It defines exactly what a juvenile court may and may not order before adjudication when a case is opened on referral.
  Quote: “We will not infer from statutory silence a jurisdictional grant to a juvenile court to sever legal custody from a natural parent at a pre-adjudicatory shelter hearing.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5329228/sl-v-district-court-ex-rel-tenth-judicial-district/
- **Lovato v. District Court**, 198 Colo. 419, 601 P.2d 1072 (1979)
  Holding: The court held that the juvenile court had jurisdiction under the Children's Code to take the case at a shelter hearing and to issue temporary orders providing for the medical treatment of a gravely injured child whose parents were unable and unavailable to act, and that its order was proper.
  Why here: The case shows the juvenile court exercising jurisdiction and ordering protective and medical measures at the outset of a child-protection matter.
  Quote: “We hold, therefore, that the court had jurisdiction and its action was proper.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1372246/lovato-v-district-court-in-for-tenth-jud/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **People in Interest of E.M.**, 2016 COA 38
  Holding: The division held that a county department may not seek involuntary termination through an article 5 relinquishment proceeding while an article 3 dependency and neglect case is open, because the dependency court retains continuing exclusive jurisdiction over the child's status until majority or until that jurisdiction is otherwise terminated.
  Why here: A parent's voluntary relinquishment route cannot be used to bypass the open dependency case, which is where a guardian's request for help is handled.
  Quote: “the dependency and neglect court maintains continuing, exclusive jurisdiction over any child who has been adjudicated dependent and neglected.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4247699/people-in-the-interest-of-em/
- **S.L. v. District Court**, 676 P.2d 12 (Colo. 1984)
  Holding: The court held that a juvenile court exceeds its jurisdiction if it transfers legal custody of a child from a parent to the county department at a pre-adjudicatory shelter hearing; at that stage the court may only continue shelter or release the child, and a temporary protective custody order reaches physical custody only.
  Why here: A parent who seeks help voluntarily is protected by the rule that legal custody cannot be taken without adjudication and a dispositional decree.
  Quote: “We will not infer from statutory silence a jurisdictional grant to a juvenile court to sever legal custody from a natural parent at a pre-adjudicatory shelter hearing.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5329228/sl-v-district-court-ex-rel-tenth-judicial-district/
- **Troxel v. Granville**, 530 U.S. 57 (2000)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the invalidation of a nonparental visitation order, holding that the Washington statute as applied violated the mother's due process right to make decisions concerning the care, custody and control of her children because the trial court gave no special weight to the presumption that a fit parent acts in her children's best interests.
  Why here: Where a parent or guardian asks the agency for help and has never been found unfit, this presumption limits what the state may impose in exchange for that help.
  Quote: “there is a presumption that fit parents act in the best interests of their children”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1087668/troxel-v-granville/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Brubaker v. Colorado Sun**, 2026 CO 18
  Holding: The court held that the Department of Human Services failed to carry its burden of showing that the Children's Records Act confidentiality provision barred release, and ordered disclosure of the aggregate number of child-abuse reports and screened-in reports from three named residential child care facilities.
  Why here: This is the current Colorado authority letting press and public obtain the intake and screening data that oversight of a facility or county depends on.
  Quote: “we conclude that CDHS must disclose the six cardinal numbers requested by the Media Organizations.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10830785/amanda-brubaker-in-her-official-capacity-as-the-records-custodian-for-the/
- **Denver Publishing Co. v. Dreyfus**, 184 Colo. 288, 520 P.2d 104 (1974)
  Holding: The court held that coroners' autopsy reports are public records open to inspection under the Colorado Open Records Act, that a requester need not show a special interest to obtain them, and that a custodian who denies access must state the legal ground and may seek a protective order from the district court.
  Why here: Public access to death-investigation records without any showing of special interest is the evidentiary basis on which outside oversight of a fatality begins.
  Quote: “This statement of policy clearly eliminates any requirement that a person seeking access to public records show a special interest in those records in order to be permitted access thereto.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1191310/denver-publishing-company-v-dreyfus/
- **Denver Post Corp. v. Ritter**, 255 P.3d 1083 (Colo. 2011)
  Holding: The court affirmed dismissal of the CORA complaint, holding that where the custodian is an individual who is also a public official the requester must first allege facts showing the record was made, maintained or kept in an official capacity before the burden shifts to the official, and that the Governor's personal cell phone bills, kept only to pay them, were not shown to be likely public records.
  Why here: Outside oversight that begins with a records request must carry this threshold showing before a custodian is put to the burden of justifying a withholding.
  Quote: “When the custodian of a requested record is an individual who is also a public official, the plaintiff must show that the record is likely a public record.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5326417/denver-post-corp-v-ritter/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **People in Interest of My.K.M.**, 2022 CO 35
  Holding: The court held that ICWA's 'active efforts' standard is heightened and more demanding than 'reasonable efforts,' requiring affirmative, active, thorough and timely remedial services tailored to the case, though the agency retains discretion to prioritize services, and it overruled People ex rel. K.D. to the extent inconsistent.
  Why here: This is the controlling Colorado authority on what ICWA requires of a county department once a child is identified as an Indian child.
  Quote: “the active efforts standard under ICWA is a more demanding standard than the reasonable efforts standard applied in non-ICWA cases.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10018079/the-people-of-the-state-of-colorado-in-the-interest-of-minor-children-my/
- **People in Interest of D.B.**, 414 P.3d 46 (Colo. App. 2017)
  Holding: The division held that 25 U.S.C. § 1912(f) requires a beyond-a-reasonable-doubt determination of likely serious emotional or physical damage supported by qualified expert testimony, but that the expert need not recite the statutory language, and affirmed the termination.
  Why here: This decision fixes the qualified-expert-witness requirement that attaches once a case is identified as involving an Indian child.
  Quote: “the trial court's determination must be supported by evidence that includes testimony from qualified expert witnesses.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6656148/people-ex-rel-db/
- **People in Interest of A.D.**, 2017 COA 61, 413 P.3d 290
  Holding: The division held that section 19-1-126(1)(a) required the department to make new ICWA inquiries in this case, and that findings and orders from a sibling's earlier case could not alone support a determination that ICWA did not apply; the judgment was reversed and the case remanded.
  Why here: This is the Colorado authority on the continuing duty of inquiry that triggers ICWA at the front end of every new case, even for a sibling of a child already screened.
  Quote: “we conclude that the findings and orders from the prior case alone are insufficient to support the trial court's conclusion that ICWA does not apply in this case.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4390089/people-in-re-ad-and-trd/
