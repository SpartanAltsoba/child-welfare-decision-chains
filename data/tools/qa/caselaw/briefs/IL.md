# IL — 53 authorities, verified 2026-09-20

## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Doe-3 v. McLean County Unit District No. 5 Board of Directors**, 2012 IL 112479, 973 N.E.2d 880
  Holding: The court held school officials owed no affirmative common-law duty to warn or report, but that plaintiffs stated a willful and wanton claim based on defendants' alleged misrepresentation of a teacher's record to a subsequent employer, and remanded for further proceedings.
  Why here: Establishes that in Illinois the reporting duty of school personnel is statutory under ANCRA, not a freestanding common-law duty.
  Quote: “the common law does not recognize an affirmative duty to act for the protection of another in the absence of a special relationship between the parties”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3135854/doe-3-v-mclean-county-unit-district-no-5-board-of-directors/
- **Lyon v. Department of Children & Family Services**, 209 Ill. 2d 264, 807 N.E.2d 423 (2004)
  Holding: The court held that DCFS violated Lyon's due process rights by combining the low credible-evidence standard for indicating a report and deciding the first-stage appeal with delays beyond its own regulatory deadlines, and affirmed expungement from the State Central Register.
  Why here: Mandated-reporter allegations against a school employee drove the register listing at issue.
  Quote: “we require strict statutory and regulatory compliance only if the Department uses the credible-evidence standard to indicate a report and to consider a first-stage appeal”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1991243/lyon-v-department-of-children-family-services/
- **Dupuy v. Samuels**, 397 F.3d 493 (7th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The court largely upheld injunctive relief requiring DCFS to weigh exculpatory evidence under the credible-evidence standard and to give child-care workers a pre-deprivation conference and expedited appeal, while holding foster parents lack a protected liberty or property interest.
  Why here: Mandated-reporter allegations feed the register system whose accuracy safeguards this case imposed.
  Quote: “It also requires that the investigator take into account all of the available evidence that tends to show that abuse or neglect did or did not occur.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/789169/jeff-dupuy-belinda-dupuy-pilar-berman-v-bryan-samuels-director-illinois/
- **Iseberg v. Gross**, 227 Ill. 2d 78, 879 N.E.2d 278 (2007)
  Holding: The court affirmed dismissal of a failure-to-warn claim, holding that Illinois adheres to the rule that absent one of the four recognized special relationships a private person owes no affirmative duty to warn or protect another from a third party's criminal conduct.
  Why here: The affirmative-duty framework Doe-3 applies to school staff: because the common law imposes no duty to warn, a reporting obligation in Illinois must come from ANCRA.
  Quote: “a private person has no duty to act affirmatively to protect another from criminal attack by a third person absent a "special relationship" between the parties”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2050890/iseberg-v-gross/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **In re D.C.**, 209 Ill. 2d 287, 807 N.E.2d 472 (2004)
  Holding: The court affirmed reversal of the unfitness finding as to a child born during the nine-month reasonable-progress period, holding the statute's literal language, not convenience, controls which children a reasonable-progress finding can reach.
  Why here: The case entered the system through a Peoria police response to a domestic dispute that generated a hotline call to DCFS.
  Quote: “when conducting such proceedings, it is not simple logic, but the literal language of the statute that must control”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3135155/people-v-tontorya-c/
- **Brokaw v. Mercer County**, 235 F.3d 1000 (7th Cir. 2000)
  Holding: The court reversed dismissal of a former foster child's section 1983 claims, holding that a child removed from his home on allegedly fabricated neglect allegations stated Fourth Amendment and substantive due process claims that survived the defendants' immunity defenses in part.
  Why here: The removal followed a coordinated law-enforcement and agency response to relatives' neglect allegations in Mercer County, Illinois.
  Quote: “a seizure is reasonable if it is pursuant to a court order, if it is supported by probable cause, or if it is justified by exigent circumstances”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/771526/ca-brokaw-v-mercer-county-james-brokaw-weir-brokaw/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **In re K.C.**, 325 Ill. App. 3d 771, 759 N.E.2d 15 (2001)
  Holding: The court affirmed a circuit court order directing DCFS to remove and reassign a caseworker team that had failed its statutory reporting duty, holding the order neither exceeded the Juvenile Court Act nor violated separation of powers.
  Why here: Illustrates judicial direction to the agency mid-case as a trigger for renewed agency action.
  Quote: “The ultimate determination of which caseworkers to assign to the matter, therefore, remained within the discretion of DCFS.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3142009/in-re-kc/
- **In re Austin W.**, 214 Ill. 2d 31, 823 N.E.2d 572 (2005)
  Holding: The court held that the circuit court could not disregard a DCFS administrative abuse finding by conducting its own de novo review, reversed the change of custody as against the manifest weight of the evidence, and reinstated DCFS as guardian with power to consent to adoption.
  Why here: Illustrates a guardian ad litem motion in juvenile court triggering renewed agency and judicial scrutiny of a placement.
  Quote: “The court, without authority, rejected an administrative finding that the Wards abused Austin after conducting a de novo review of the matter.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3135277/berkley-v-illinois-department-of-children-family-services/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **In re J.M.**, 170 Ill. App. 3d 552, 524 N.E.2d 1241 (1988)
  Holding: The court upheld the constitutionality of the minor-requiring-authoritative-intervention statute and affirmed the adjudication as supported by a preponderance of the evidence, but reversed the dispositional order because the father was not given the notice the Act requires, and remanded.
  Why here: The Illinois pathway that opens when a youth or family seeks help: jurisdiction attaches only after interim crisis services are offered and the minor and parent cannot agree on a voluntary placement.
  Quote: “The welfare of the minor is in jeopardy as a consequence of the minor's own behavior, not that of the parents.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2063502/in-re-jm/

## INP-07 · Medical Disclosure
- **In re A.P.**, 2012 IL 113875, 981 N.E.2d 336
  Holding: The court affirmed the appellate court's reversal of a neglect adjudication, holding that a mother who left her children with a boyfriend she had no reason to suspect, and who took the injured child straight to hospital, did not place them in an injurious environment.
  Why here: The neglect finding rested in part on Pediatric Resource Center medical records generated after a hospital presentation.
  Quote: “If the State fails to prove the allegations of abuse, neglect, or dependence by a preponderance of the evidence, the court must dismiss the petition.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3135789/in-re-ap/
- **In re Arthur H.**, 212 Ill. 2d 441, 819 N.E.2d 734 (2004)
  Holding: The court held that at an adjudicatory hearing the circuit court must focus solely on whether the child was neglected rather than apportion blame among parents, and, finding the State had failed to prove neglect as to Arthur Jr., reversed the circuit court and dismissed the amended wardship petition.
  Why here: The neglect allegations rested on medical-neglect findings reported by a DCFS investigator after a health-care contact, and the court held that evidence insufficient.
  Quote: “It is the burden of the State to prove allegations of neglect by a preponderance of the evidence.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1988960/people-v-arthur-h/
- **In re D.S.**, 217 Ill. 2d 306, 840 N.E.2d 1216 (2005)
  Holding: The court affirmed the neglect adjudication and wardship of a newborn, holding Illinois had jurisdiction under the UCCJEA even though the child was delivered in Indiana, that the ex parte shelter-care notice complaint was moot, and that the injurious-environment finding was not against the manifest weight of the evidence.
  Why here: The case arose from the mother's flight from Illinois against her obstetrician's instructions and the out-of-state hospital delivery that followed.
  Quote: “On review, a trial court's ruling of neglect will not be reversed unless it is against the manifest weight of the evidence.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1981261/in-re-ds/
- **In re E.G.**, 133 Ill. 2d 98, 549 N.E.2d 322 (1989)
  Holding: The court held that a minor shown by clear and convincing evidence to be mature has a common-law right to consent to or refuse medical treatment, subject to countervailing state interests, and remanded with directions to expunge the neglect finding entered against the minor's mother.
  Why here: The case reached the juvenile court through the hospital treating a seventeen-year-old leukemia patient who refused blood transfusions.
  Quote: “Because we find that a mature minor may exercise a common law right to consent to or refuse medical care, we decline to address the constitutional issue.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2230619/in-re-eg/

  Holding: The court affirmed denial of qualified immunity, holding that a caseworker's under-the-clothes examination of children at a private school exceeded the principal's consent to interview them and violated their clearly established Fourth Amendment rights.
  Why here: Governs child-protection interviews and examinations conducted on private school premises in this circuit.

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1190279/michael-c-v-gresbach/

  Holding: The court affirmed summary judgment for the caseworker defendants, holding the parents had not shown racial animus and that their equal protection and section 1985(3) conspiracy claims arising from a disabled child's removal therefore failed.
  Why here: The investigation began with a referral from school personnel who observed the child's condition.
  Quote: “To establish "purpose" under prong two, a plaintiff must demonstrate racial, ethnic, or other class-based "invidiously discriminatory animus behind the conspirators' actions."”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/810539/lia-xiong-v-michael-wagner/
- **Doe v. Heck**, 327 F.3d 492 (7th Cir. 2003)
  Holding: The court held that caseworkers who searched a private religious school and seized a student for a child-abuse interview without a warrant, court order, probable cause, consent or exigent circumstances violated the Fourth Amendment and the family's right to familial relations, but affirmed qualified immunity because those rights were not yet clearly established.
  Why here: The foundational Seventh Circuit decision on child-abuse investigations conducted on private school premises without parental notice or consent.
  Quote: “although child welfare caseworkers may investigate allegations of child abuse without violating parents' constitutional right to familial relations, they may not do so arbitrarily”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/781752/doe-v-heck/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **In re Z.L.**, 2021 IL 126931
  Holding: The court reinstated the abuse and neglect findings and the dispositional order, reaffirming that the adjudicatory hearing asks only whether the child was abused or neglected, and remanded for proof that the Indian Child Welfare Act's notice requirements had been satisfied.
  Why here: The leading Illinois Supreme Court statement that unresolved Indian-ancestry information triggers ICWA compliance before a case can proceed.
  Quote: “We agree with the GAL that this case must be remanded for proof of compliance with the Indian Child Welfare Act.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5302327/in-re-zl/
- **In re A.M.**, 2025 IL App (1st) 242325
  Holding: The court reversed a dispositional order placing an Indian child in foster care entered without qualified expert witness testimony, holding ICWA section 1912(e) must be satisfied before any foster care placement, and remanded for an ICWA-compliant dispositional hearing.
  Why here: The most recent published Illinois application of ICWA's qualified expert witness and active efforts requirements.
  Quote: “ICWA rules for foster care placement applied at the time that A.M., a child of Indigenous standing, was removed from her father's home”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10677091/in-re-am/

## INP-12 · Emergency / Protective Custody
- **Hernandez ex rel. Hernandez v. Foster**, 657 F.3d 463 (7th Cir. 2011)
  Holding: The court affirmed qualified immunity on the initial removal of an Illinois child but vacated as to continuing to hold him after probable cause dissipated and as to the coerced safety plan, remanding those claims for further proceedings.
  Why here: Holds that the Juvenile Court Act's 48-hour window does not license continued custody once probable cause has dissipated.
  Quote: “Probable cause is determined on the basis of the facts and circumstances known to the defendants at the time of the conduct in issue”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/612665/hernandez-ex-rel-hernandez-v-foster/
- **Brokaw v. Mercer County**, 235 F.3d 1000 (7th Cir. 2000)
  Holding: The court reversed dismissal of a former foster child's section 1983 claims, holding that a child removed from his home on allegedly fabricated neglect allegations stated Fourth Amendment and substantive due process claims that survived the defendants' immunity defenses in part.
  Why here: Defines the constitutional ceiling on emergency protective custody in Illinois.
  Quote: “a seizure is reasonable if it is pursuant to a court order, if it is supported by probable cause, or if it is justified by exigent circumstances”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/771526/ca-brokaw-v-mercer-county-james-brokaw-weir-brokaw/
- **Stanley v. Illinois**, 405 U.S. 645 (1972)
  Holding: The Court held that Illinois violated due process and equal protection by declaring the children of an unwed father dependent without any hearing on his fitness, and reversed the Illinois Supreme Court.
  Why here: Emergency custody cannot ripen into a change of custody without the hearing due process requires.
  Quote: “We have concluded that all Illinois parents are constitutionally entitled to a hearing on their fitness before their children are removed from their custody.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/108497/stanley-v-illinois/
- **Siliven v. Indiana Department of Child Services**, 635 F.3d 921 (7th Cir. 2011)
  Holding: The court affirmed judgment for the caseworkers, holding that a removal made without a court order is reasonable only if supported by probable cause or exigent circumstances and that a physician's opinion of inflicted injury plus a prior abuse allegation supplied probable cause here.
  Why here: Supplies the objective probable-cause framework for protective custody, judged on the facts known to the caseworker when the decision was made.
  Quote: “The pertinent inquiry, then, is whether defendants had probable cause to believe that C.S.'s physical health would be seriously endangered if he was not immediately removed from his father's custody.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/212473/siliven-v-indiana-department-of-child-services/

## PMC-04 · Fatality Review Commission
- **Harris v. One Hope United, Inc.**, 2015 IL 117200
  Holding: The court declined to recognize a self-critical analysis privilege in Illinois and affirmed the order compelling a DCFS contractor to produce its internal priority review report on an infant's death during intact family services.
  Why here: Confirms that fatality-review material is not shielded from litigants by a common-law privilege.
  Quote: “the type of information sought in discovery here is not subject to a "self-critical analysis privilege" that would protect it from disclosure”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2796291/harris-v-one-hope-united-inc/
- **K.H. ex rel. Murphy v. Morgan**, 914 F.2d 846 (7th Cir. 1990)
  Holding: The court held that an Illinois foster child has a clearly established right not to be handed to a caretaker the state's caseworkers know or suspect is likely to abuse or neglect her, while rejecting any right to a stable foster-home environment, and affirmed denial of qualified immunity in part.
  Why here: Frames the constitutional duty whose breach fatality and serious-injury reviews are meant to detect.
  Quote: “a prima facie right not to be placed with a foster parent who the state's caseworkers and supervisors know or suspect is likely to abuse or neglect the foster child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/548401/murphy-v-morgan/
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services**, 489 U.S. 189 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held that the Due Process Clause imposed no affirmative constitutional duty on county social workers to protect a child from his father's violence while he remained in his father's custody, and affirmed judgment for the county.
  Why here: Explains why fatality review and legislative reform, rather than constitutional tort, are the main accountability routes for children harmed at home.
  Quote: “nothing in the language of the Due Process Clause itself requires the State to protect the life, liberty, and property of its citizens against invasion by private actors”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112202/deshaney-v-winnebago-cty-dss/

## PMC-05 · FOIA / Transparency
- **Harris v. One Hope United, Inc.**, 2015 IL 117200
  Holding: The court declined to recognize a self-critical analysis privilege in Illinois and affirmed the order compelling a DCFS contractor to produce its internal priority review report on an infant's death during intact family services.
  Why here: Illinois' highest court leaves any new confidentiality privilege to the legislature, favoring disclosure in its absence.
  Quote: “the type of information sought in discovery here is not subject to a "self-critical analysis privilege" that would protect it from disclosure”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2796291/harris-v-one-hope-united-inc/
- **B.H. v. McDonald**, 49 F.3d 294 (7th Cir. 1995)
  Holding: The court held that non-parties, including the Cook County Public Guardian, had no right to intervene in or attend in-chambers status conferences implementing the B.H. consent decree governing the Illinois DCFS, and that the public had no First Amendment right of access to those conferences.
  Why here: Defines what decree-implementation material is publicly accessible and what remains in chambers.
  Quote: “we hold that non-parties have no right to be present at these in-chambers conferences”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/691072/bh-ch-je-v-jess-mcdonald-director-of-the-illinois-department-of/

## PMC-06 · Project Milk Carton AI Analysis
- **Dupuy v. Samuels**, 397 F.3d 493 (7th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The court largely upheld injunctive relief requiring DCFS to weigh exculpatory evidence under the credible-evidence standard and to give child-care workers a pre-deprivation conference and expedited appeal, while holding foster parents lack a protected liberty or property interest.

  Quote: “It also requires that the investigator take into account all of the available evidence that tends to show that abuse or neglect did or did not occur.”
