## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Arbaugh v. Board of Education**, 214 W. Va. 677, 591 S.E.2d 235 (2003)
  Holding: Answering a certified question from federal court, the Court held that West Virginia's mandatory child-abuse reporting statute, which carries criminal penalties, does not create an implied private civil cause of action against a mandated reporter who failed to report.
  Why here: It is the controlling WV answer to what a failure by a mandated reporter does and does not give the child in civil court.
  Quote: “We conclude that relevant West Virginia law does not give rise to a private cause of action in this regard”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1418417/arbaugh-v-board-of-education/
- **In re Randy H.**, 220 W. Va. 122, 640 S.E.2d 185 (2006)
  Holding: Reversing a one-page order dismissing an abuse and neglect petition, the Court held that a circuit court that sees reasonable cause to believe additional abuse or neglect exists may compel DHHR to investigate and amend the petition, and must enter findings of fact and conclusions of law on the allegations.
  Why here: The opinion sets out the reporting duties that attach when judicial officers have reasonable cause to suspect abuse or neglect.
  Quote: “the circuit court has the inherent authority to compel the Department to amend its petition to encompass the evidence or allegations”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8219169/in-re-randy-h/
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services**, 489 U.S. 189 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held that the Due Process Clause imposes no affirmative duty on a State to protect a child from private violence, so the agency's failure to remove Joshua from his abusive father was not a constitutional violation.
  Why here: Reports received and not acted on do not, without more, create federal constitutional liability for the agency.
  Quote: “a State's failure to protect an individual against private violence simply does not constitute a violation of the Due Process Clause”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112202/deshaney-v-winnebago-county-department-of-social-services/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request

  Holding: Affirming denial of a non-parent guardianship petition, the Court held that a court appointing a guardian under W. Va. Code § 44-10-3 must give priority to the child's mother or father, and that a family court presented with abuse and neglect allegations in a guardianship petition must refer the matter under Rule 48a(a).
  Why here: A relative's guardianship petition is a common self-referral route into the system, and this case governs its handling.
  Quote: “In appointing a guardian, the court shall give priority to the minor's mother or father”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1259062/in-re-abbigail-faye-b/
- **In re Antonio R.A.**, 228 W. Va. 380, 719 S.E.2d 850 (2011)
  Holding: The Court held that a minor over fourteen who nominates a relative as guardian does not thereby permit a court to divest his fit, non-offending biological mother of custody, although the child may be entitled to visitation with the grandmother.
  Why here: A child's or relative's own petition for guardianship cannot substitute for proof that the parent is unfit.
  Quote: “A court may not divest Gina H., Antonio's biological mother, of her custodial rights simply because Carol G. would be a "fit" guardian”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2506624/in-re-antonio-ra/
- **In re Charity H.**, 215 W. Va. 208, 599 S.E.2d 631 (2004)
  Holding: Affirming denial of post-adjudicatory and dispositional improvement periods and the termination of parental rights, the Court held that an improvement period is a carefully limited, discretionary opportunity that must be denied where granting it would jeopardize the child's best interests.
  Why here: A parent's own request for treatment-based services does not entitle her to the delay an improvement period would impose.
  Quote: “Where an improvement period would jeopardize the best interests of the child, for instance, an improvement period will not be granted”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8219054/in-re-charity-h/

## INP-07 · Medical Disclosure

  Why here: It sustains medical examination of children suspected of being maltreated without criminal-law warrant standards.
  Quote: “investigative home visits by social workers are not subject to the same scrutiny as searches in the criminal context”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9017727/wildauer-v-frederick-county/

  Holding: Reversing the return of an abused infant to his mother, the Court held that parental rights may be terminated where clear and convincing evidence shows extensive physical abuse in the parents' custody and the perpetrator remains unidentified, and it set out the duties of guardians ad litem.
  Why here: Medical findings of extensive, unexplained injury to an infant are the evidentiary core of this kind of case.
  Quote: “There is no reasonable likelihood that the conditions of abuse can be substantially corrected because the perpetrator of Jeffrey R.L.'s physical abuse has not been identified”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1225293/in-re-jeffrey-rl/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Farley v. Worley**, 215 W. Va. 412, 599 S.E.2d 835 (2004)
  Holding: The Court held that a public body answering a FOIA request must segregate or redact exempt material and disclose the rest, so the City had to produce redacted records even though the requester had not initially asked for redacted material, and it prescribed the Vaughn index and affidavit a body must supply when it withholds.
  Why here: It is the practical tool by which oversight bodies and citizens force production of agency records.
  Quote: “we conclude that the City was required to provide Mr. Farley this redacted material in response to his original FOIA request”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1328152/farley-v-worley/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's requirement that States make 'reasonable efforts' to prevent removal and reunify families does not create a right enforceable by beneficiaries through § 1983 or an implied right of action.
  Why here: Enforcement of federal child-welfare standards runs through federal administrative oversight, not private suits.
  Quote: “We hold that the Act does not create an enforceable right on behalf of respondents”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **In the Interest of Carlita B.**, 185 W. Va. 613, 408 S.E.2d 365 (1991)
  Holding: Affirming termination of parental rights, the Court held the improvement periods and family case plans afforded the mother were more than adequate, and directed that abuse and neglect cases be given the highest priority with systems developed to monitor their progress and prevent delay.
  Why here: The Court ordered the Administrative Director to build systems monitoring the status and progress of abuse and neglect cases, the state-court analogue of systemic oversight triggers.
  Quote: “Unjustified procedural delays wreak havoc on a child's development, stability and security.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1314674/in-the-interest-of-carlita-b/
- **Daily Gazette Co. v. West Virginia Development Office**, 198 W. Va. 563, 482 S.E.2d 180 (1996)
  Holding: Remanding with directions, the Court held that a public body withholding records under the internal-memoranda exemption must produce a Vaughn index justifying each document and a supporting affidavit, and that the exemption covers only deliberative advice, opinions and recommendations, not ordinary communications with private parties.
  Why here: Oversight of an agency begins with its records, and this decision sets out precisely what the agency must establish, document by document, before it may withhold them.
  Quote: “The Vaughn index must provide a relatively detailed justification as to why each document is exempt, specifically identifying the reasons why W. Va.Code, 29B-1-4(8) [1977] is relevant”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1343706/daily-gazette-co-v-west-virginia-development-office/

## DEC-01 · Screen-Out / Unfounded at Intake
- **In re Randy H.**, 220 W. Va. 122, 640 S.E.2d 185 (2006)
  Holding: Reversing a one-page order dismissing an abuse and neglect petition, the Court held that a circuit court that sees reasonable cause to believe additional abuse or neglect exists may compel DHHR to investigate and amend the petition, and must enter findings of fact and conclusions of law on the allegations.
  Why here: A court may not let the agency screen out or abandon allegations without findings.
  Quote: “the circuit court has the inherent authority to compel the Department to amend its petition to encompass the evidence or allegations”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8219169/in-re-randy-h/
- **In the Interest of Tiffany Marie S.**, 196 W. Va. 223, 470 S.E.2d 177 (1996)
  Holding: Affirming a finding of abuse and neglect, the Court held that a circuit court's findings of fact in an abuse and neglect case may be disturbed only if clearly erroneous, while its conclusions of law are reviewed de novo, and that the Department must prove the conditions existing when the petition was filed by clear and convincing proof.
  Why here: A screen-out or unfounded conclusion challenged on appeal is reviewed under this deferential standard for factual determinations.
  Quote: “absent a mistake of law, an appellate tribunal should disturb a circuit court's determination only if it is clearly erroneous”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1201720/in-interest-of-tiffany-marie-s/
- **In re Z.S.-1 and Z.S.-2**, Nos. 22-0478 & 22-602 (W. Va. Nov. 9, 2023)
  Holding: Vacating the adjudicatory and dispositional orders, the Court held that a Rule 26(a) stipulated adjudication must contain agreed facts supporting court involvement and a statement of the respondent's problems, and that without a proper adjudication the circuit court had no jurisdiction to proceed to disposition.
  Why here: An informal or undocumented resolution at the front of the case cannot substitute for adjudicatory findings.
  Quote: “Without properly made findings of abuse and/or neglect at adjudication, a case cannot proceed to disposition”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9438430/in-re-zs-1-and-zs-2-and-in-re-zs-1-and-zs-2/
- **Martin v. Saint Mary's Department of Social Services**, 346 F.3d 502 (4th Cir. 2003)
  Holding: Affirming judgment for the social workers, the court held that conduct that was at most negligent in bringing about the children's removal cannot support a § 1983 claim, so the defendants were entitled to qualified immunity.
  Why here: Errors and miscommunications in agency handling are judged by more than negligence before liability attaches.
  Quote: “Because a § 1983 claim cannot be sustained, it follows that the defendants are entitled to qualified immunity”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2967846/martin-v-saint-marys-department-of-social-services/
- **In re A.P.-1, A.P.-2, A.P.-3**, 825 S.E.2d 324 (W. Va. 2019)
  Holding: Vacating the termination order and remanding, the Court held that a circuit court may not terminate parental rights without first adjudicating the parent as having abused or neglected the child, where the same court had expressly concluded three months earlier that the father had not abused or neglected them.
  Why here: An allegation the court has rejected cannot be carried forward into disposition; where the agency's theory of abuse or neglect fails, the case must end at adjudication rather than proceed on the agency's preference.
  Quote: “a circuit court may not terminate a parent's rights to his child without first finding that the parent abused or neglected his child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4599903/in-re-ap-1-ap-2-ap-3/
- **In re C.S. and B.S.**, No. 21-0694 (W. Va. June 15, 2022)
  Holding: Vacating both dispositional orders, the Court held that the fifteen-of-twenty-two-months filing trigger does not relieve the Department of presenting, or the circuit court of finding, affirmative evidence supporting termination, and that the court lacked subject matter jurisdiction over a child who did not meet the statutory definition of an abused or neglected child.
  Why here: It is the recent WV authority, relied on in In re Z.S., on what an adjudication must actually establish: a child safely placed with non-abusing legal guardians before the petition does not meet the statutory definition, and the case as to that child should never have gone forward.
  Quote: “Because B.S. was not an abused or neglected child, the circuit court lacked subject matter jurisdiction to address, and ultimately terminate, the petitioner's parental rights to this child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6478070/in-re-cs-and-bs/

## OUT-01 · Reunification
- **In re Jeffrey R.L.**, 190 W. Va. 24, 435 S.E.2d 162 (1993)
  Holding: Reversing the return of an abused infant to his mother, the Court held that parental rights may be terminated where clear and convincing evidence shows extensive physical abuse in the parents' custody and the perpetrator remains unidentified, and it set out the duties of guardians ad litem.
  Why here: Reunification is not permissible where the abuser has never been identified and the parents will not identify him.
  Quote: “There is no reasonable likelihood that the conditions of abuse can be substantially corrected because the perpetrator of Jeffrey R.L.'s physical abuse has not been identified”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1225293/in-re-jeffrey-rl/
- **In re Charity H.**, 215 W. Va. 208, 599 S.E.2d 631 (2004)
  Holding: Affirming denial of post-adjudicatory and dispositional improvement periods and the termination of parental rights, the Court held that an improvement period is a carefully limited, discretionary opportunity that must be denied where granting it would jeopardize the child's best interests.
  Why here: Reunification services may be withheld where further delay for services would harm the child.
  Quote: “Where an improvement period would jeopardize the best interests of the child, for instance, an improvement period will not be granted”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8219054/in-re-charity-h/
- **In the Interest of Carlita B.**, 185 W. Va. 613, 408 S.E.2d 365 (1991)
  Holding: Affirming termination of parental rights, the Court held the improvement periods and family case plans afforded the mother were more than adequate, and directed that abuse and neglect cases be given the highest priority with systems developed to monitor their progress and prevent delay.
  Why here: Reunification efforts are adequate when improvement periods and case plans give the parent a real, monitored opportunity to correct conditions.
  Quote: “Unjustified procedural delays wreak havoc on a child's development, stability and security.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1314674/in-the-interest-of-carlita-b/
- **In re Cecil T.**, 228 W. Va. 89, 717 S.E.2d 873 (2011)
  Holding: Reversing the circuit court, the Court held that incarceration alone does not automatically terminate parental rights but the rights of an incarcerated parent may be terminated on a best-interests analysis, and that Rule 43's eighteen-month permanency deadline must be strictly followed.
  Why here: An incarcerated parent's ability to correct conditions 'in the near future' is measured against the child's need for permanency, not the release date.
  Quote: “Thus while the mere fact that someone is incarcerated will not result in automatic termination of parental rights, the parental rights of an incarcerated person may be terminated”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2506080/in-re-cecil-t/
- **In re R.J.M.**, 164 W. Va. 496, 266 S.E.2d 114 (1980)
  Holding: Affirming termination of parental rights where an infant was allowed to reach near-starvation, the Court held that compelling circumstances justified denying an improvement period and that immediate termination, without first employing a less drastic intervening alternative, is more reasonable where the child is under the age of three.
  Why here: It fixes the point at which continued effort toward reunification becomes futile, holding that insisting on an improvement period here would have been a vain act because return of the child might have threatened her life.
  Quote: “where a child is under the age of three immediate termination without an intervening period employing a less drastic alternative is more reasonable than in other cases”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8217537/in-re-rjm/
- **In re Erica C.**, 214 W. Va. 375, 589 S.E.2d 517 (2003)
  Holding: Affirming termination of parental rights and the denial of post-termination visitation, the Court held that parents who made no serious effort during a granted improvement period and refused to cooperate with the Department's repeated attempts to help could not show a reasonable likelihood that the conditions of abuse and neglect would be corrected.
  Why here: Cited alongside R.J.M. in Charity H., it measures reunification by what the parents actually did with the improvement period they were given, against the Department's repeated attempts to help them regain custody.
  Quote: “Although they were granted an improvement period, the appellants made no serious effort to change their behavior or provide a stable home for their children.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1310960/in-re-erica-c/

## OUT-03 · Guardianship / Kinship Permanency
- **In re Antonio R.A.**, 228 W. Va. 380, 719 S.E.2d 850 (2011)
  Holding: The Court held that a minor over fourteen who nominates a relative as guardian does not thereby permit a court to divest his fit, non-offending biological mother of custody, although the child may be entitled to visitation with the grandmother.
  Why here: It is the leading WV case on contested guardianship as permanency against a biological parent.
  Quote: “A court may not divest Gina H., Antonio's biological mother, of her custodial rights simply because Carol G. would be a "fit" guardian”

  Why here: It is the governing framework for guardianship as a permanency route in West Virginia.
  Quote: “In appointing a guardian, the court shall give priority to the minor's mother or father”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1259062/in-re-abbigail-faye-b/
- **In re A.F.**, No. 23-698 (W. Va. June 6, 2025)
  Holding: Vacating and remanding, the Court held that a circuit court denying placement with a grandparent must enter detailed findings showing it considered the statutory grandparent preference and determined, on the whole record, that the preference was overcome by the child's best interests.
  Why here: Permanency through kinship cannot be rejected in a conclusory order.
  Quote: “in denying grandparent placement the circuit court must make detailed findings of fact and conclusions of law demonstrating that it considered the grandparent preference”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10599981/in-re-af/
- **In re Cesar L.**, 221 W. Va. 249, 654 S.E.2d 373 (2007)

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1268026/in-re-cesar-l/
- **Napoleon S. v. Walker**, 217 W. Va. 254, 617 S.E.2d 801 (2005)
  Holding: Holding that the lower tribunals abused their discretion by failing to apply the statutory grandparent preference, the Court remanded with directions that the child be placed with his paternal grandparents for adoption following a gradual transition of custody.
  Why here: It governs when kinship placement must be chosen over a non-relative foster home as the child's permanent home, and requires the agency to actually assist relatives in developing a permanency plan.

  Quote: “Thus, the non-parent whose parental rights have been terminated could not then be deemed a "noncustodial parent."”

  Holding: Remanding with directions, the Court held that a public body withholding records under the internal-memoranda exemption must produce a Vaughn index justifying each document and a supporting affidavit, and that the exemption covers only deliberative advice, opinions and recommendations, not ordinary communications with private parties.
  Why here: Systematic analysis of agency conduct depends on records that cannot be withheld on unexplained assertions of deliberative privilege.
  Quote: “The Vaughn index must provide a relatively detailed justification as to why each document is exempt, specifically identifying the reasons why W. Va.Code, 29B-1-4(8) [1977] is relevant”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1343706/daily-gazette-co-v-west-virginia-development-office/
