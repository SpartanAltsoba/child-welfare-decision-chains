# NY — 43 authorities, verified 2026-09-21

## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Matter of Catherine G. v. County of Essex**, 3 N.Y.3d 175, 818 N.E.2d 1110, 785 N.Y.S.2d 369 (2004)
  Holding: The Court held that a fourteen-year-old sibling is not a 'person legally responsible' under Family Court Act § 1012, so mandated reporters had no statutory duty to report his abuse of his sisters and the mother's late notice of claim against county and school officials was patently meritless.
  Why here: This decision fixes the outer boundary of the mandated-reporter duty by defining who can be the subject of a reportable allegation.
  Quote: “When in doubt about whether a case must be reported under Social Services Law § 413, they ought to err on the side of caution and make a report”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5638733/catherine-g-v-county-of-essex/
- **Kimberly S.M. v. Bradford Central School**, 226 A.D.2d 85, 649 N.Y.S.2d 588 (4th Dep't 1996)
  Holding: The Fourth Department reversed summary judgment and reinstated the complaint, holding that a mandated reporter's duty under Social Services Law § 413 is measured by the facts known when abuse is suspected and that liability under § 420(2) is not defeated merely because the alleged abuser later proves not to have been a person legally responsible for the child.
  Why here: This is the principal New York decision imposing civil liability on a mandated reporter for a willful failure to report.
  Quote: “a mandated reporter is obligated to report suspected cases of child sexual abuse based upon facts and circumstances within the knowledge of the reporter”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6134938/kimberly-s-m-v-bradford-central-school/
- **Dolgas v. Wales**, 215 A.D.3d 51, 187 N.Y.S.3d 829 (3d Dep't 2023)
  Holding: The Third Department modified to dismiss the failure-to-report claims, holding that because a school employee who abused students was not a 'person legally responsible' for their care, the children were not 'abused children' for Social Services Law § 413 purposes and the school district could not be liable under § 420(2).
  Why here: It states the § 413 duty and the § 420(2) liability standard while confining both to abuse by a parent or person legally responsible.
  Quote: “Civil liability may be imposed upon these individuals who knowingly and willfully fail to make the requisite report”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9389799/dolgas-v-wales/
- **Kempster v. Child Protective Services**, 130 A.D.2d 623, 515 N.Y.S.2d 807 (2d Dep't 1987)
  Holding: The Second Department held that Social Services Law § 419 immunity required dismissal of the complaint against the hospital that reported suspected child abuse, since a person or institution participating in good faith in making a report is immune from civil and criminal liability and good faith is presumed for mandated reporters.
  Why here: Section 419 immunity and its presumption of good faith are the protection that makes the mandatory reporting duty workable.
  Quote: “We find, contrary to Special Term, that, by virtue of Social Services Law § 419, the foregoing complaint must be dismissed insofar as it is asserted against the defendant hospital”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6032808/kempster-v-child-protective-services/

## INP-02 · Voluntary / Third-Party Report
- **Matter of Catherine G. v. County of Essex**, 3 N.Y.3d 175, 818 N.E.2d 1110, 785 N.Y.S.2d 369 (2004)
  Holding: The Court held that a fourteen-year-old sibling is not a 'person legally responsible' under Family Court Act § 1012, so mandated reporters had no statutory duty to report his abuse of his sisters and the mother's late notice of claim against county and school officials was patently meritless.
  Why here: It confirms that anyone may report, while limiting who may be named as a subject of a Central Register report.
  Quote: “When in doubt about whether a case must be reported under Social Services Law § 413, they ought to err on the side of caution and make a report”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5638733/catherine-g-v-county-of-essex/
- **Kempster v. Child Protective Services**, 130 A.D.2d 623, 515 N.Y.S.2d 807 (2d Dep't 1987)
  Holding: The Second Department held that Social Services Law § 419 immunity required dismissal of the complaint against the hospital that reported suspected child abuse, since a person or institution participating in good faith in making a report is immune from civil and criminal liability and good faith is presumed for mandated reporters.
  Why here: The same statutory immunity extends to any person or institution reporting in good faith, not only mandated reporters.
  Quote: “We find, contrary to Special Term, that, by virtue of Social Services Law § 419, the foregoing complaint must be dismissed insofar as it is asserted against the defendant hospital”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6032808/kempster-v-child-protective-services/
- **Matter of Lee TT. v. Dowling**, 87 N.Y.2d 699, 664 N.E.2d 1243, 642 N.Y.S.2d 181 (1996)
  Holding: The Court of Appeals held that the 'some credible evidence' standard for retaining an indicated report in the Statewide Central Register violates federal due process to the extent the report is disseminated to provider and licensing agencies, and that a subject is entitled to a pre-deprivation hearing at which the agency must prove the abuse or maltreatment by a fair preponderance of the evidence.
  Why here: It confirms that permissive reports from any person, identified or anonymous, enter the same register and trigger the same consequences.
  Quote: “subjects are entitled to a predeprivation hearing to determine by a fair preponderance of the evidence whether reports of abuse can be substantiated and relevant to future employment or licensure”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2130803/matter-of-lee-tt-v-dowling/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Matter of Jeter v. Poole**, 2024 NY Slip Op 05868 (N.Y. Nov. 25, 2024)
  Holding: The Court affirmed confirmation of an indicated Central Register report, holding that there is no constitutional right to assigned counsel at a Statewide Central Register administrative fair hearing and that the 2020 amendments to Social Services Law § 422 - including the fair-preponderance standard and the irrebuttable presumption from an article 10 dismissal - do not apply retroactively to determinations rendered before their January 1, 2022 effective date.
  Why here: The report at issue originated from a police officer's referral to the Statewide Central Register after interviewing the child.
  Quote: “Inclusion on the SCR—unlike Family Court article 10 proceedings—does not impact rights that we have concluded warrant recognition of a constitutional right to assigned counsel in civil proceedings”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10283167/matter-of-jeter-v-poole/
- **Southerland v. City of New York**, 681 F.3d 122 (2d Cir. 2012)
  Holding: The Second Circuit vacated summary judgment for the caseworker on the father's Fourth Amendment unlawful-search claim and the children's unlawful-seizure and procedural due process claims, holding that a Family Court order obtained on a materially misleading affidavit cannot supply the warrant equivalent and that fact disputes remained over whether emergency circumstances justified the removal.
  Why here: The case involves police officers accompanying a caseworker on a home entry and addresses the City's failure to train them.
  Quote: “In child-abuse investigations, a Family Court order is equivalent to a search warrant for Fourth Amendment purposes”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/799761/southerland-ex-rel-southerland-v-city-of-new-york/
- **Cornejo v. Bell**, 592 F.3d 121 (2d Cir. 2010)
  Holding: In an action arising from an infant's death and the ensuing child-protective proceeding, the Second Circuit held that agency lawyers who initiate and prosecute child-protective petitions have absolute immunity, that caseworkers have only qualified rather than absolute immunity, and that qualified immunity nonetheless barred the § 1983 claims against the caseworkers here.
  Why here: Hospital and law-enforcement referrals following a child's death initiated the proceeding the court reviews.
  Quote: “The lawyer defendants are entitled to absolute immunity on plaintiffs § 1983 claims because they were performing functions analogous to those of a prosecutor”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1356186/cornejo-v-bell/
- **Nicholson v. Scoppetta**, 3 N.Y.3d 357, 820 N.E.2d 840, 787 N.Y.S.2d 196 (2004)
  Holding: Answering certified questions from the Second Circuit, the Court of Appeals held that a child's exposure to domestic violence does not alone establish neglect or justify removal, that neglect requires a preponderance showing of actual or imminent impairment caused by the parent's failure to exercise a minimum degree of care, and that emergency removal without a court order under Family Court Act § 1024 is permissible only where the danger is so urgent that there is no time to obtain an ex parte order.
  Why here: Police and agency referrals arising from domestic-violence calls cannot, standing alone, be treated as establishing danger to the child under this decision.
  Quote: “emergency removal is appropriate where the danger is so immediate, so urgent that the child's life or safety will be at risk before an ex parte order can be obtained”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2227102/nicholson-v-scoppetta/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **Matter of Jamie J.**, No. 118 (N.Y. Nov. 20, 2017)
  Holding: The Court held that Family Court Act article 10-A confers no independent grant of continuing jurisdiction, so once an article 10 neglect petition is dismissed for failure to prove neglect the court loses subject matter jurisdiction and may not conduct further permanency hearings.
  Why here: A court may not keep a child-welfare case open by judicial referral once the underlying petition has been dismissed.
  Quote: “the dismissal of a neglect petition terminates Family Court's jurisdiction”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4444621/the-matter-of-jamie-j-wayne-county-department-of-social-services/
- **Nicholson v. Scoppetta**, 3 N.Y.3d 357, 820 N.E.2d 840, 787 N.Y.S.2d 196 (2004)
  Holding: Answering certified questions from the Second Circuit, the Court of Appeals held that a child's exposure to domestic violence does not alone establish neglect or justify removal, that neglect requires a preponderance showing of actual or imminent impairment caused by the parent's failure to exercise a minimum degree of care, and that emergency removal without a court order under Family Court Act § 1024 is permissible only where the danger is so urgent that there is no time to obtain an ex parte order.
  Why here: When a court directs or reviews child-protective intervention, Nicholson supplies the statutory neglect elements the court must find.
  Quote: “emergency removal is appropriate where the danger is so immediate, so urgent that the child's life or safety will be at risk before an ex parte order can be obtained”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2227102/nicholson-v-scoppetta/
- **Matter of Ella B.**, 30 N.Y.2d 352, 285 N.E.2d 288, 334 N.Y.S.2d 133 (1972)
  Holding: The Court held that an indigent parent charged with child neglect is entitled to assigned counsel and must be affirmatively advised of that right, so that a mother told only that she could hire and pay for a lawyer herself could not validly waive counsel or admit the petition, and it remitted the matter to Family Court for a new adjudicatory and dispositional hearing with counsel.
  Why here: When a neglect proceeding is opened against a parent, the court itself must advise the parent of the right to assigned counsel before taking any admission or proceeding to adjudication.
  Quote: “to be relinquished to the State without the opportunity for a hearing, with assigned counsel if the parent lacks the means to retain a lawyer”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5679136/in-re-ella-b/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **Matter of Catherine G. v. County of Essex**, 3 N.Y.3d 175, 818 N.E.2d 1110, 785 N.Y.S.2d 369 (2004)
  Holding: The Court held that a fourteen-year-old sibling is not a 'person legally responsible' under Family Court Act § 1012, so mandated reporters had no statutory duty to report his abuse of his sisters and the mother's late notice of claim against county and school officials was patently meritless.
  Why here: The mother's own calls to the hotline and disclosures to a pediatrician and school psychologist are the paradigm guardian self-report.
  Quote: “When in doubt about whether a case must be reported under Social Services Law § 413, they ought to err on the side of caution and make a report”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5638733/catherine-g-v-county-of-essex/
- **Matter of Lawyers for Children v. New York State Office of Children & Family Services**, 2025 NY Slip Op 02115 (N.Y. App. Div. 3d Dep't Apr. 10, 2025)
  Holding: Over a dissent, the Third Department affirmed dismissal of an article 78 challenge to OCFS's Host Family Home regulations, holding that OCFS did not exceed its authority because the program is a preventive service in which the parent, not the State, decides whether and with whom to place the child.
  Why here: The program exists precisely for parents who ask for help before any allegation, and the parent retains the placement decision.
  Quote: “Petitioners' argument that OCFS exceeded its authority when it created the Host Family Home program is unpersuasive”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10579936/matter-of-lawyers-for-children-v-new-york-state-off-of-children-family/
- **Matter of Michael B.**, 80 N.Y.2d 299, 604 N.E.2d 122, 590 N.Y.S.2d 60 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that awarding custody to foster parents under Social Services Law § 392(6) was impermissible and that the best-interests inquiry for a child in temporary foster care compares continued foster care against discharge to the biological parent or other relative or suitable person, rather than measuring parent against foster parent.
  Why here: The Court emphasizes that parents in temporary crisis are encouraged to place children voluntarily under § 384-a without forfeiting their rights.
  Quote: “analysis of the child's "best interest" must begin not by measuring biological parent against foster parent but by weighing past and continued foster care against discharge to the biological parent”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5690914/in-re-michael-b/
- **Duchesne v. Sugarman**, 566 F.2d 817 (2d Cir. 1977)
  Holding: The Second Circuit reversed directed verdicts for city child-welfare officials and the placement agencies and remanded for a new trial, holding that a mother and her children were deprived of their constitutionally protected right to family integrity without due process where the Bureau of Child Welfare took and then kept the children for years without her consent, without a court order, and without any hearing, and that § 1983 damages liability was for the jury.
  Why here: The mother placed her children informally only because she sought hospital treatment for herself, and this case holds that a parent who asks for help does not thereby forfeit custody.
  Quote: “the right of the family to remain together without the coercive interference of the awesome power of the state”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/350998/josephina-duchesne-as-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-pauline-perez-v-jule/

## INP-07 · Medical Disclosure
- **Kempster v. Child Protective Services**, 130 A.D.2d 623, 515 N.Y.S.2d 807 (2d Dep't 1987)
  Holding: The Second Department held that Social Services Law § 419 immunity required dismissal of the complaint against the hospital that reported suspected child abuse, since a person or institution participating in good faith in making a report is immune from civil and criminal liability and good faith is presumed for mandated reporters.
  Why here: This is the New York authority shielding hospitals and treating clinicians who report suspected abuse from retaliatory suit.
  Quote: “We find, contrary to Special Term, that, by virtue of Social Services Law § 419, the foregoing complaint must be dismissed insofar as it is asserted against the defendant hospital”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6032808/kempster-v-child-protective-services/
- **Matter of Philip M.**, 82 N.Y.2d 238, 624 N.E.2d 168, 604 N.Y.S.2d 40 (1993)
  Holding: Affirming findings of abuse and an order of supervision, the Court held that Family Court Act § 1046(a)(ii) creates only a rebuttable presumption shifting the burden of going forward to the respondents, while the burden of proving abuse by a preponderance always remains with the petitioning agency.
  Why here: An unexplained sexually transmitted infection diagnosed in a medical setting is treated as evidence of abuse under § 1046(a)(ii).
  Quote: “The establishment of a prima facie case does not require the court to find that the parents were culpable; it merely establishes a rebuttable presumption of parental culpability”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2065121/matter-of-philip-m/
- **Matter of Hofbauer**, 47 N.Y.2d 648, 393 N.E.2d 1009, 419 N.Y.S.2d 936 (1979)
  Holding: The Court affirmed dismissal of neglect petitions, holding that parents who sought accredited medical assistance and chose a treatment recommended by a duly licensed physician and not totally rejected by all responsible medical authority have not failed to supply adequate medical care.
  Why here: It sets the limit on when a treating physician's disagreement with parental choices converts a medical encounter into medical neglect.
  Quote: “have provided for their child a treatment which is recommended by their physician and which has not been totally rejected by all responsible medical authority”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5683552/in-re-hofbauer/
- **Matter of Nassau County Dep't of Social Servs. ex rel. Dante M. v. Denise J.**, 87 N.Y.2d 73, 661 N.E.2d 138, 637 N.Y.S.2d 666 (1995)
  Holding: The Court held that a newborn's positive toxicology for a controlled substance cannot by itself support a neglect finding because it does not establish the causative connection to actual or imminent impairment that Family Court Act § 1012(f)(i)(B) requires, but affirmed the findings and the one-year order of supervision because other evidence - prematurity, low birth weight, intensive-care and high-risk follow-up needs, and the mother's cocaine use during pregnancy - showed imminent danger of impairment to both children.
  Why here: A hospital toxicology screen at birth is the classic medical disclosure, and this decision fixes how far that single laboratory result can carry an agency.
  Quote: “We conclude that more than a positive toxicology is generally required for a neglect determination.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2092873/nassau-county-department-of-social-services-ex-rel-dante-m-v-denise-j/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Marisol A. v. Giuliani**, 126 F.3d 372 (2d Cir. 1997)
  Holding: The Second Circuit affirmed certification of a Rule 23(b)(2) class of children in or at risk of entering New York City's child welfare system challenging systemic failures, while directing the district court to refine the class into subclasses before trial.
  Why here: Systemic, class-wide litigation is the recognized vehicle in this circuit for forcing review of a child welfare system's performance.
  Quote: “we hold that the district court did not abuse its discretion by certifying the class at this point in the litigation”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/746860/marisol-v-giuliani/
- **Elisa W. v. City of New York**, 82 F.4th 115 (2d Cir. 2023)
  Holding: The Second Circuit vacated the denial of class certification to children in New York City foster care alleging systemic deficiencies, holding that the district court applied an incorrect legal framework to commonality and typicality, and remanded for reconsideration of the class and subclasses.
  Why here: The decision revives systemic oversight litigation as a route to scrutinize New York City's foster care administration.
  Quote: “The district court erred in its analysis of commonality and typicality under Rule 23”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9426937/elisa-w-v-city-of-new-york/
- **Mark G. v. Sabol**, 93 N.Y.2d 710, 717 N.E.2d 1067, 695 N.Y.S.2d 730 (1999)
  Holding: The Court held that no private right of action for money damages may be implied from title 4 or title 6 of article 6 of the Social Services Law for a child-welfare agency's failure to provide statutorily mandated services, because the Legislature enacted a comprehensive enforcement scheme centered on monitoring and loss of State reimbursement.
  Why here: Enforcement of New York's child-welfare service mandates runs through monitoring and fiscal penalties, not private suits.
  Quote: “Recognition of such a private right of action under title 4 would not be consistent with the legislative scheme”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2165417/mark-g-v-sabol/
- **Matter of Lawyers for Children v. New York State Office of Children & Family Services**, 2025 NY Slip Op 02115 (N.Y. App. Div. 3d Dep't Apr. 10, 2025)
  Holding: Over a dissent, the Third Department affirmed dismissal of an article 78 challenge to OCFS's Host Family Home regulations, holding that OCFS did not exceed its authority because the program is a preventive service in which the parent, not the State, decides whether and with whom to place the child.
  Why here: The case is the current test of whether an administrative child-welfare program can be created without legislation, litigated by an oversight organization.
  Quote: “Petitioners' argument that OCFS exceeded its authority when it created the Host Family Home program is unpersuasive”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10579936/matter-of-lawyers-for-children-v-new-york-state-off-of-children-family/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield**, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)
  Holding: The Supreme Court reversed, holding that 'domicile' under the Indian Child Welfare Act is a matter of uniform federal law, that children born off-reservation to reservation-domiciled parents were domiciled on the reservation, and that the tribal court therefore had exclusive jurisdiction under 25 U.S.C. § 1911(a), voiding the state adoption decree.
  Why here: This is the controlling federal authority on when an Indian child's case must go to tribal court and when state jurisdiction is merely presumptively transferable, the standard New York courts must apply because no published New York appellate decision reaches ICWA's merits.
  Quote: “In enacting the ICWA Congress confirmed that, in child custody proceedings involving Indian children domiciled on the reservation, tribal jurisdiction was exclusive as to the States”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112241/mississippi-band-of-choctaw-indians-v-holyfield/
- **Matter of Baby Boy C.**, 27 A.D.3d 34, 805 N.Y.S.2d 313 (1st Dep't 2005)
  Holding: Reversing Family Court and granting the Tohono O'odham Nation's motion to intervene, the First Department held that the judicially created 'existing Indian family' exception cannot be engrafted onto the Indian Child Welfare Act, because the Act's only threshold requirements are that the matter be a child custody proceeding involving an Indian child, and remanded for a hearing on the 25 U.S.C. § 1915 placement preferences and any good cause to depart from them.
  Why here: This is the published New York appellate decision reaching ICWA's merits: it holds the Act applies whenever the child is a tribal member or eligible for membership regardless of the family's cultural ties, recognizes the tribe's independent right to intervene, and requires a hearing on the statutory placement preferences.
  Quote: “judicial insertion of an additional criterion for applicability is plainly beyond the intent of Congress and must be rejected”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5839166/in-re-baby-boy-c/

## INP-12 · Emergency / Protective Custody
- **Nicholson v. Scoppetta**, 3 N.Y.3d 357, 820 N.E.2d 840, 787 N.Y.S.2d 196 (2004)
  Holding: Answering certified questions from the Second Circuit, the Court of Appeals held that a child's exposure to domestic violence does not alone establish neglect or justify removal, that neglect requires a preponderance showing of actual or imminent impairment caused by the parent's failure to exercise a minimum degree of care, and that emergency removal without a court order under Family Court Act § 1024 is permissible only where the danger is so urgent that there is no time to obtain an ex parte order.
  Why here: Nicholson sets the governing New York standard for taking a child into protective custody without prior judicial authorization.
  Quote: “emergency removal is appropriate where the danger is so immediate, so urgent that the child's life or safety will be at risk before an ex parte order can be obtained”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2227102/nicholson-v-scoppetta/
- **Southerland v. City of New York**, 681 F.3d 122 (2d Cir. 2012)
  Holding: The Second Circuit vacated summary judgment for the caseworker on the father's Fourth Amendment unlawful-search claim and the children's unlawful-seizure and procedural due process claims, holding that a Family Court order obtained on a materially misleading affidavit cannot supply the warrant equivalent and that fact disputes remained over whether emergency circumstances justified the removal.
  Why here: Absent consent, a court order, or emergency circumstances, officials may not take children from a parent's custody.
  Quote: “In child-abuse investigations, a Family Court order is equivalent to a search warrant for Fourth Amendment purposes”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/799761/southerland-ex-rel-southerland-v-city-of-new-york/
- **Matter of Elizabeth C. (Omar C.)**, 156 A.D.3d 193, 66 N.Y.S.3d 300 (2d Dep't 2017)
  Holding: Reaching the merits under the mootness exception and reversing, the Second Department held that a parent excluded from the home by a full stay-away order of protection in an article 10 proceeding is entitled to a prompt Family Court Act § 1028 hearing applying the imminent-risk standard, because such an order is functionally equivalent to removal of the children from his care.
  Why here: Separating parent and child by excluding the parent triggers the same protective-custody safeguards as physical removal.
  Quote: “it must be governed by the same legal standards as are provided for removal of a child in Family Court Act §§ 1027 and 1028”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4447266/matter-of-elizabeth-c-omar-c/
- **Duchesne v. Sugarman**, 566 F.2d 817 (2d Cir. 1977)
  Holding: The Second Circuit reversed directed verdicts for city child-welfare officials and the placement agencies and remanded for a new trial, holding that a mother and her children were deprived of their constitutionally protected right to family integrity without due process where the Bureau of Child Welfare took and then kept the children for years without her consent, without a court order, and without any hearing, and that § 1983 damages liability was for the jury.
  Why here: Taking and retaining custody of a child without parental consent and without court authorization violates the reciprocal due process rights of parent and child.
  Quote: “the right of the family to remain together without the coercive interference of the awesome power of the state”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/350998/josephina-duchesne-as-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-pauline-perez-v-jule/
- **Nicholson v. Scoppetta**, 344 F.3d 154 (2d Cir. 2003)
  Holding: Reviewing the preliminary injunction against New York City's practice of removing children from mothers who had been battered, the Second Circuit held that the removals may raise serious federal constitutional questions but that antecedent, unsettled questions about the Family Court Act had to be answered first, and it therefore certified three questions to the New York Court of Appeals - on the definition of neglect, on whether witnessing domestic abuse is 'danger' or 'risk' to life or health, and on whether removal requires additional particularized evidence - retaining jurisdiction over the appeal.
  Why here: The certified questions frame the federal constraints on taking a child into protective custody in a domestic-violence household and identify the state-law predicates that must be settled before those constraints are applied.
  Quote: “We agree that in some circumstances the removals may raise serious questions of federal constitutional law. We conclude, however, that uncertain issues of state law precede our own constitutional inquiry.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/783602/nicholson-v-scoppetta/

## DEC-01 · Screen-Out / Unfounded at Intake
- **Matter of Catherine G. v. County of Essex**, 3 N.Y.3d 175, 818 N.E.2d 1110, 785 N.Y.S.2d 369 (2004)
  Holding: The Court held that a fourteen-year-old sibling is not a 'person legally responsible' under Family Court Act § 1012, so mandated reporters had no statutory duty to report his abuse of his sisters and the mother's late notice of claim against county and school officials was patently meritless.
  Why here: It validates screening out reports that do not allege conduct by a parent or person legally responsible.
  Quote: “When in doubt about whether a case must be reported under Social Services Law § 413, they ought to err on the side of caution and make a report”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5638733/catherine-g-v-county-of-essex/
- **Matter of Lee TT. v. Dowling**, 87 N.Y.2d 699, 664 N.E.2d 1243, 642 N.Y.S.2d 181 (1996)
  Holding: The Court of Appeals held that the 'some credible evidence' standard for retaining an indicated report in the Statewide Central Register violates federal due process to the extent the report is disseminated to provider and licensing agencies, and that a subject is entitled to a pre-deprivation hearing at which the agency must prove the abuse or maltreatment by a fair preponderance of the evidence.
  Why here: Unfounded reports must be deleted and destroyed under the scheme the Court construes here.
  Quote: “subjects are entitled to a predeprivation hearing to determine by a fair preponderance of the evidence whether reports of abuse can be substantiated and relevant to future employment or licensure”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2130803/matter-of-lee-tt-v-dowling/
- **Matter of Elizabeth B. v. New York State Office of Children & Family Services**, 149 A.D.3d 8, 47 N.Y.S.3d 515 (3d Dep't 2017)
  Holding: The Third Department annulled the portion of the OCFS determination that maintained an indicated report of inadequate guardianship against a mother who was herself the victim of domestic violence, ordering the report unfounded, expunged and sealed, because the minimum-degree-of-care inquiry must account for the risks and constraints she actually faced.
  Why here: A report that cannot survive this analysis must be amended to unfounded, expunged and sealed.
  Quote: “Respondent misconstrues this standard as one that cannot be "modified or excused because a parent is under stress or fear."”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4370544/matter-of-elizabeth-b-v-new-york-state-office-of-children-and-family/
- **Matter of Jeter v. Poole**, 2024 NY Slip Op 05868 (N.Y. Nov. 25, 2024)
  Holding: The Court affirmed confirmation of an indicated Central Register report, holding that there is no constitutional right to assigned counsel at a Statewide Central Register administrative fair hearing and that the 2020 amendments to Social Services Law § 422 - including the fair-preponderance standard and the irrebuttable presumption from an article 10 dismissal - do not apply retroactively to determinations rendered before their January 1, 2022 effective date.
  Why here: It explains that under prior law a Family Court dismissal created no presumption that the register record should be unfounded.
  Quote: “Inclusion on the SCR—unlike Family Court article 10 proceedings—does not impact rights that we have concluded warrant recognition of a constitutional right to assigned counsel in civil proceedings”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10283167/matter-of-jeter-v-poole/

## DEC-03 · Substantiated / Founded Finding

  Why here: This is the current Court of Appeals decision on the procedural protections and evidentiary standard governing a substantiated finding.
  Quote: “Inclusion on the SCR—unlike Family Court article 10 proceedings—does not impact rights that we have concluded warrant recognition of a constitutional right to assigned counsel in civil proceedings”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10283167/matter-of-jeter-v-poole/

  Why here: It applies the fair-preponderance maltreatment standard and Nicholson's minimum-degree-of-care test to an administrative substantiation.
  Quote: “Respondent misconstrues this standard as one that cannot be "modified or excused because a parent is under stress or fear."”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4370544/matter-of-elizabeth-b-v-new-york-state-office-of-children-and-family/
- **Matter of Philip M.**, 82 N.Y.2d 238, 624 N.E.2d 168, 604 N.Y.S.2d 40 (1993)
  Holding: Affirming findings of abuse and an order of supervision, the Court held that Family Court Act § 1046(a)(ii) creates only a rebuttable presumption shifting the burden of going forward to the respondents, while the burden of proving abuse by a preponderance always remains with the petitioning agency.
  Why here: It fixes the burden of proof and the evidentiary weight of a res ipsa-style prima facie case behind a founded finding.
  Quote: “The establishment of a prima facie case does not require the court to find that the parents were culpable; it merely establishes a rebuttable presumption of parental culpability”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2065121/matter-of-philip-m/
- **Matter of Nicole V.**, 71 N.Y.2d 112, 518 N.E.2d 914, 524 N.Y.S.2d 19 (1987)
  Holding: The Court affirmed abuse findings in two proceedings, holding that as amended Family Court Act § 1046(a)(vi) permits a child's out-of-court statements to be corroborated by any other evidence tending to support their reliability, including validation testimony by a qualified expert and the cross-corroborating statements of other child victims.
  Why here: This is the controlling New York rule on the corroboration required before a child's hearsay disclosure can support a founded finding.
  Quote: “As amended, section 1046 (a) (vi) states a broad flexible rule providing that out-of-court statements may be corroborated by "[a]ny other evidence tending to support" their reliability”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5689110/in-re-nicole-v/
- **Matter of Nassau County Dep't of Social Servs. ex rel. Dante M. v. Denise J.**, 87 N.Y.2d 73, 661 N.E.2d 138, 637 N.Y.S.2d 666 (1995)
  Holding: The Court held that a newborn's positive toxicology for a controlled substance cannot by itself support a neglect finding because it does not establish the causative connection to actual or imminent impairment that Family Court Act § 1012(f)(i)(B) requires, but affirmed the findings and the one-year order of supervision because other evidence - prematurity, low birth weight, intensive-care and high-risk follow-up needs, and the mother's cocaine use during pregnancy - showed imminent danger of impairment to both children.
  Why here: This is the leading New York causation authority for a founded finding: the agency must link the medical result to actual or imminent impairment rather than treat the result itself as the finding.
  Quote: “We conclude that more than a positive toxicology is generally required for a neglect determination.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2092873/nassau-county-department-of-social-services-ex-rel-dante-m-v-denise-j/
