## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **State v. Polashek**, 2002 WI 74, 253 Wis. 2d 527, 646 N.W.2d 330
  Holding: The strict-liability prohibition in Wis. Stat. sec. 48.981(7) on disclosing child-abuse report information reaches only communications of information the recipient did not already know.
  Why here: It defines the confidentiality duty that attaches to a mandated reporter's report and to those who receive it.
  Quote: “we hold, for the purposes of § 48.981(7), that "disclose" is defined to require that the information communicated must have been previously unknown by the recipient”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2013929/state-v-polashek/
- **Phillips v. Behnke**, 192 Wis. 2d 552, 531 N.W.2d 619 (Ct. App. 1995)
  Holding: Wis. Stat. sec. 48.981(4) immunity extends to persons who in good faith relay suspected abuse information to someone expected to report it, not only to the individual who contacts authorities; good faith is presumed and conclusory allegations do not rebut it.
  Why here: It is the Wisconsin construction of the mandated-reporter immunity a reporter relies on.
  Quote: “Because we conclude that the trial court properly determined that the respondents were statutorily immune from liability, the judgment is affirmed.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1347651/phillips-v-behnke/
- **Doe v. Heck**, 327 F.3d 492 (7th Cir. 2003)
  Holding: Wisconsin caseworkers violated the Fourth Amendment and the plaintiffs' right to familial relations by entering a private school and seizing and interviewing a child without a warrant, court order, probable cause, exigent circumstances, or parental or school consent.
  Why here: It holds that a report from a mandated reporter does not by itself authorize a warrantless custodial interview of a child.
  Quote: “Thus, although child welfare caseworkers may investigate allegations of child abuse without violating parents' constitutional right to familial relations, they may not do so arbitrarily.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/781752/doe-v-heck/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **W.A.B. v. W.G.B.**, 2019 WI App 5, 385 Wis. 2d 514, 925 N.W.2d 783
  Holding: A prior JIPS disposition as to the children does not preclude the county from obtaining a CHIPS disposition against the parents on the same facts; JIPS and CHIPS are distinct proceedings with different parties and purposes.
  Why here: A juvenile-justice referral does not exhaust the court's ability to order child-protection proceedings against the parents.
  Quote: “a JIPS disposition and a CHIPS disposition are two distinct areas of law, and a disposition in the JIPS case did not preclude a disposition in the CHIPS cases”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8287224/wab-v-wgb-in-re-wab/
- **Joni B. v. State**, 202 Wis. 2d 1, 549 N.W.2d 411 (1996)
  Holding: The amendment to Wis. Stat. sec. 48.23(3) barring courts from appointing counsel for anyone but the child in CHIPS cases violates the separation of powers and the Due Process Clause; courts must retain discretion to appoint counsel for parents case by case.
  Why here: It governs a judge's authority over counsel and process once a child-protection matter is before the court.
  Quote: “We conclude that fundamental fairness requires that a circuit judge be given the discretion to make the determination of what due process requires on a case-by-case basis.”

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **In re Guardianship of James D.K.**, 2006 WI 68, 291 Wis. 2d 333, 718 N.W.2d 38
  Holding: Over a parent's objection a court may appoint a nonparent guardian of a minor only on a showing of extraordinary circumstances affecting the child's health or safety, and then only if the appointment is in the child's best interests.
  Why here: It is the standard applied when a relative or caregiver asks a court to take responsibility for a child without a CPS petition.
  Quote: “a court is authorized under chapter 880 to appoint a nonparent as guardian of a minor if there exist extraordinary circumstances affecting the health or safety of the minor”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1233152/in-matter-of-the-guardianship-of-james-dk/
- **Stephen R. v. Ilana C.**, 2011 WI App 13, 331 Wis. 2d 108, 794 N.W.2d 533
  Holding: Wisconsin had initial child-custody jurisdiction under the UCCJEA and the circuit court properly exercised its discretion in awarding guardianship of the child to her maternal grandmother and step-grandfather on compelling reasons.
  Why here: It is the path a relative uses to seek legal responsibility for a child without a CPS petition.
  Quote: “Here, we conclude that the circuit court properly exercised its discretion in awarding guardianship of Alexis to Stephen and Ilya.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8238376/stephen-r-v-ilana-c/
- **Barstad v. Frazier**, 118 Wis. 2d 549, 348 N.W.2d 479 (1984)
  Holding: In a custody dispute between a parent and a third party the best-interests test does not govern; a parent is entitled to custody unless the parent is unfit or unable to care for the child or compelling reasons exist, and only then may the court award custody to a third party on best interests.
  Why here: It is the source rule a relative or caregiver must satisfy when asking a court for responsibility for a child over a parent's objection, without any CPS petition.
  Quote: “Compelling reasons include abandonment, persistent neglect of parental responsibilities, extended disruption of parental custody, or other similar extraordinary circumstances that would drastically affect the welfare of the child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2177290/barstad-v-frazier/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Monroe County DHS v. Luis R.**, 2009 WI App 109, 320 Wis. 2d 652, 770 N.W.2d 795
  Holding: ICWA sec. 1912(f) applies in a TPR even though the Indian child was already placed outside the parental home, and a county social worker without expertise beyond ordinary social-work qualifications is not a 'qualified expert witness'; sec. 1912(d) carries no beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.
  Why here: It is Wisconsin's leading published decision on how ICWA attaches once a case involves an Indian child.
  Quote: “We conclude that 25 U.S.C. § 1912 (f) applies even though the child has been placed outside the parental home before the TPR proceeding is filed.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2197577/monroe-county-department-of-human-services-v-luis-r/
- **R.A.C.P. v. State**, 157 Wis. 2d 106, 458 N.W.2d 823 (Ct. App. 1990)
  Holding: In a TPR involving an Indian child the trial court correctly imposed a dual burden: proof beyond a reasonable doubt of ICWA's active-efforts and serious-damage elements, alongside the Children's Code elements.
  Why here: It establishes that ICWA supersedes the Children's Code in any covered custody proceeding in Wisconsin.
  Quote: “We conclude that the trial court appropriately distributed the burden of proof according to the dictates of both the ICWA and the Wisconsin children's code.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8237509/racp-v-state/
- **Brown County v. Marcella G.**, 2001 WI App 194, 247 Wis. 2d 158, 634 N.W.2d 140
  Holding: Before denying a parent's ICWA request to transfer a case to tribal court, the circuit court must first ascertain whether the tribal court will accept jurisdiction and then decide whether good cause exists to deny transfer.
  Why here: It is the Wisconsin procedure for an ICWA transfer-to-tribal-court request.
  Quote: “We conclude that reversal is appropriate and, on remand, direct the circuit court to first ascertain whether the tribal court will accept jurisdiction.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2089554/brown-county-v-marcella-g/
- **Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl**, 570 U.S. 637 (2013)
  Holding: ICWA sec. 1912(f)'s heightened showing does not apply where the parent never had legal or physical custody of the Indian child, and sec. 1912(d)'s remedial-and-rehabilitative-efforts requirement applies only where terminating the parent's rights would precipitate the breakup of an Indian family.
  Why here: It limits when ICWA's heightened protections attach once a case involves an Indian child, and it postdates and narrows Wisconsin's Luis R. line.
  Quote: “we hold that §1912(d) applies only in cases where an Indian family's "breakup" would be precipitated by the termination of the parent's rights.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/931616/adoptive-couple-v-baby-girl/
- **Haaland v. Brackeen**, 599 U.S. 255 (2023)
  Holding: The Court rejected every challenge to the Indian Child Welfare Act - upholding Congress's Article I authority to enact it, rejecting the anticommandeering claims on the merits, and finding no standing for the equal protection and nondelegation claims - leaving ICWA's placement preferences and procedural requirements intact.
  Why here: It confirms that ICWA remains binding on Wisconsin counties and state courts, so the tribal-jurisdiction and notice machinery in the Wisconsin ICWA line is current law.
  Quote: “we reject all of petitioners' challenges to the statute, some on the merits and others for lack of standing.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406660/haaland-v-brackeen/

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1281091/jones-v-dane-county/

## FAIL-05 · Discrimination / Retaliation
- **Kenosha County DHS v. Jodie W.**, 2006 WI 93, 293 Wis. 2d 530, 716 N.W.2d 845
  Holding: Terminating an incarcerated parent's rights under the continuing-CHIPS ground because she failed to meet a condition of return she could not possibly satisfy while imprisoned, without regard to her actual parenting or the child's condition, violates substantive due process.
  Why here: It is Wisconsin's leading authority that treating a parent's status - incarceration - as a proxy for unfitness is unconstitutional.
  Quote: “the circuit court improperly deemed Jodie unfit solely by virtue of her status as an incarcerated person without regard for her actual parenting activities or the condition of her child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8238187/kenosha-county-department-of-human-services-v-jodie-w/
- **Monroe County DHS v. Kelli B.**, 2004 WI 48, 271 Wis. 2d 51, 678 N.W.2d 831
  Holding: The incestuous-parenthood ground for termination, applied to a mother who was herself the child victim of the incest, is not narrowly tailored to a compelling state interest and violates her substantive due process right to parent.
  Why here: It is the Wisconsin precedent for challenging a termination ground that penalizes a parent for a status or circumstance rather than conduct.
  Quote: “Because we determine that the statute, as applied, is not narrowly tailored to advance a compelling state interest, we conclude that it violates Kelli's right to substantive due process.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1787950/monroe-county-department-of-human-services-v-kelli-b/
- **Monroe County DHS v. Luis R.**, 2009 WI App 109, 320 Wis. 2d 652, 770 N.W.2d 795
  Holding: ICWA sec. 1912(f) applies in a TPR even though the Indian child was already placed outside the parental home, and a county social worker without expertise beyond ordinary social-work qualifications is not a 'qualified expert witness'; sec. 1912(d) carries no beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.
  Why here: Failure to apply ICWA's protections to an Indian family is a reversible, rights-based failure in Wisconsin.
  Quote: “We conclude that 25 U.S.C. § 1912 (f) applies even though the child has been placed outside the parental home before the TPR proceeding is filed.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2197577/monroe-county-department-of-human-services-v-luis-r/
- **Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl**, 570 U.S. 637 (2013)
  Holding: ICWA sec. 1912(f)'s heightened showing does not apply where the parent never had legal or physical custody of the Indian child, and sec. 1912(d)'s remedial-and-rehabilitative-efforts requirement applies only where terminating the parent's rights would precipitate the breakup of an Indian family.
  Why here: Any claim that ICWA's protections were denied to an Indian family must now be framed within the custody and family-breakup limits this decision imposes.
  Quote: “we hold that §1912(d) applies only in cases where an Indian family's "breakup" would be precipitated by the termination of the parent's rights.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/931616/adoptive-couple-v-baby-girl/
