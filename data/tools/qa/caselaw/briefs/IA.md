## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Nelson v. Lindaman**, 867 N.W.2d 1 (Iowa 2015)
  Holding: A physician who gave the DHS child-abuse investigator his honest opinion that the father's explanation of an infant's spiral fracture was plausible was immune from civil liability under Iowa Code section 232.73, because that immunity turns on the subjective good faith of the person reporting or assisting in the assessment, not on whether he was negligent.
  Why here: Sets Iowa's controlling standard for reporter and participant immunity under Iowa Code section 232.73 — subjective good faith, not reasonableness — which is the liability rule every mandatory reporter operates under.
  Quote: “To avoid summary judgment, the plaintiff must have evidence the defendant acted dishonestly, not merely carelessly, in assisting the DHS.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4472092/shannon-and-danny-nelson-individually-and-on-behalf-of-en-fka-en-a/
- **McCracken v. Iowa Department of Human Services**, 595 N.W.2d 779 (Iowa 1999)
  Holding: DHS had statutory authority to investigate the same alleged incident a second time and to enter a founded report even though an earlier investigation had produced an unfounded report that was expunged; the second investigator lawfully accessed the expunged unfounded information under Iowa Code section 235A.15(3), and the father's constitutional due-process objections to the ALJ hearing were not preserved.
  Why here: Holds that a department social worker who learns of suspected abuse is herself a mandatory reporter bound by Iowa Code section 232.69 and exposed to civil and criminal penalties for silence, so a new disclosure restarts the reporting duty.
  Quote: “the department had statutory authority to conduct an investigation involving the alleged sexual abuse, even though a previous investigation of the same allegation resulted in an unfounded report”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1990166/mccracken-v-iowa-department-of-human-services/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Webster v. Westlake**, 41 F.4th 1004 (8th Cir. 2022)
  Holding: Des Moines detectives conducting a child welfare check were not entitled to qualified immunity for arresting a mother on her porch after she refused their demands: without a warrant, an exigency, a removal order, or her consent, they could neither enter the home nor compel her to produce the child.
  Why here: An Iowa case decided by Iowa's federal circuit, defining the lawful limits of a law-enforcement child welfare check that precedes a CPS referral.
  Quote: “they could not lawfully enter the home without Webster's consent, nor force her to take any specific action with respect to L.B.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7441117/trenisha-webster-v-jennifer-westlake/
- **Riehm v. Engelking**, 538 F.3d 952 (8th Cir. 2008)
  Holding: A county social worker who obtained an ex parte order removing a high school student for a seventy-two-hour psychiatric evaluation after his teacher reported a violent murder-suicide essay was entitled to qualified immunity, and the mother's familial-association claim failed because there was no underlying Fourth Amendment violation.
  Why here: Illustrates the coordinated school-to-law-enforcement-to-child-protection referral chain and the reasonable-suspicion standard that validates the resulting order.
  Quote: “Diercks had a "reasonable suspicion" of a threat to David's welfare that justified the ex parte order removing David from his home”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1443178/riehm-v-engelking/
- **Lennette v. State**, No. 20-1148 (Iowa June 10, 2022)

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **In re Guardianship of L.Y.**, No. 20-1034 (Iowa Jan. 14, 2022)
  Holding: Under the 2020 Guardianship Act, when a parent revokes consent and the basis for a minor's guardianship no longer exists, Iowa Code section 232D.503(2) places the burden on the guardian to justify continuing it, and the constitutionally grounded parental preference survived the repeal of the old statutory preference; termination of the guardianship was affirmed.
  Why here: Governs the consensual guardianship route parents use to arrange temporary care without agency involvement, and their right to unwind it.
  Quote: “The constitutionally based parental preference predated section 633.559 and, in our view, survives its repeal.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6238585/in-the-matter-of-the-guardianship-of-ly/
- **In re J.C.**, 857 N.W.2d 495 (Iowa 2014)
  Holding: An 'established' legal father who is neither the biological nor the adoptive father is not a 'parent' under chapter 232 and therefore is not a necessary party to CINA or termination proceedings; his remedy is to petition to intervene to seek guardianship or custody.
  Why here: Identifies who may come forward to seek custody or guardianship of a child already in the system, and by what mechanism.
  Quote: “Daniel is neither J.C.'s biological father nor her adoptive father. Although Daniel is J.C.'s established father, he is not her parent under chapter 232.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2764892/in-the-interest-of-jc-minor-child-dc-father/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Geringer v. Iowa Department of Human Services**, 521 N.W.2d 730 (Iowa 1994)
  Holding: In a contested case brought to expunge a child-abuse report from the central registry, the DHS director was not disqualified from reviewing the ALJ's proposed decision on the agency's own motion, and the mother received all the process she was due.
  Why here: Shows the limits of challenging agency self-review: internal recommendations that the director revisit a proposed decision are not disqualifying advocacy.
  Quote: “We also find no denial of due process of law in the manner in which Ellen's claim was adjudicated by the agency.”

  Holding: Information about the discipline imposed on two school employees who strip-searched five students is personal information in confidential personnel records exempt from disclosure under Iowa Code section 22.7(11); no balancing test applies once information fits a statutory exemption.
  Why here: Establishes that Iowa's open-records law will not itself supply the transparency needed to audit how institutions handled suspected child mistreatment.
  Quote: “we hold that the disciplinary information sought is exempt from disclosure under Iowa Code section 22.7(11)”

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **In re N.N.E.**, 752 N.W.2d 1 (Iowa 2008)
  Holding: The Iowa ICWA placement preferences in chapter 232B are unconstitutional as applied in voluntary termination cases, so the federal ICWA preferences and their 'good cause' exception for a parent's request govern; a tribe may also appear through a non-lawyer authorized representative.
  Why here: The controlling Iowa Supreme Court decision on when state ICWA yields to federal ICWA and on tribal participation rights in Iowa child-custody proceedings.
  Quote: “The Iowa ICWA placement preferences are unconstitutional in voluntary termination cases.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4472771/in-the-interest-of-nne-minor-child-tyme-madiu-tribe-of-the-berry-creek/
- **In re D.S.**, 806 N.W.2d 458 (Iowa Ct. App. 2011)
  Holding: Termination of parental rights to an Indian child was affirmed: the tribe was notified immediately, active efforts to prevent the breakup of the Indian family were made, and the Iowa ICWA requirements including qualified expert testimony and proof beyond a reasonable doubt were satisfied.
  Why here: Published Iowa appellate authority applying the chapter 232B active-efforts, tribal-notice, and qualified-expert-witness requirements once an Indian child is identified.
  Quote: “Even assuming the issue was properly preserved, we conclude active efforts were made to prevent the breakup of the Indian family.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8280390/in-the-interest-of-ds/

## INP-12 · Emergency / Protective Custody
- **F.K. v. Iowa District Court for Polk County**, 630 N.W.2d 801 (Iowa 2001)
  Holding: Iowa Code section 232.78, which permits ex parte temporary removal orders on an application that need not be sworn or written, violates neither the Fourth Amendment nor the Warrant Clause nor federal or state due process, because the removal is limited in duration and a reasonably prompt post-removal hearing follows.
  Why here: The controlling Iowa decision on the constitutionality of emergency protective custody under Iowa Code section 232.78, including the ten-day maximum for an ex parte removal.
  Quote: “On balance, we cannot say that the protections in place in section 232.78 deprive litigants, such as Felicia, of due process.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1253062/fk-v-iowa-district-court-for-polk-county/
- **Webster v. Westlake**, 41 F.4th 1004 (8th Cir. 2022)
  Holding: Des Moines detectives conducting a child welfare check were not entitled to qualified immunity for arresting a mother on her porch after she refused their demands: without a warrant, an exigency, a removal order, or her consent, they could neither enter the home nor compel her to produce the child.
  Why here: Confirms that taking protective custody requires a warrant, court order, consent, or a genuine exigency — the four lawful doors.
  Quote: “they could not lawfully enter the home without Webster's consent, nor force her to take any specific action with respect to L.B.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7441117/trenisha-webster-v-jennifer-westlake/
- **Stanley v. Finnegan**, 899 F.3d 623 (8th Cir. 2018)
  Holding: The Eighth Circuit affirmed the denial of qualified immunity on a motion to dismiss where a child-abuse investigator was alleged to have ordered seven children removed from their home despite contrary evidence and staff recommendations; reasonable suspicion of child abuse is required and is judged on the totality of the circumstances at the time of removal.
  Why here: States the substantive standard — reasonable suspicion of child abuse on the totality of the circumstances — that must exist when protective custody is taken.
  Quote: “whether there was reasonable suspicion of child abuse warranting removal must be determined by analyzing the totality of the circumstances at the time of the removal”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4525560/hal-stanley-v-katherine-finnegan/
- **Heartland Academy Community Church v. Waddle**, 427 F.3d 525 (8th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The Eighth Circuit affirmed injunctive relief and the district court's conclusion that a juvenile officer's mass removal of children from a residential school violated the Fourth Amendment — the ex parte orders were tainted by the manner in which they were obtained, and thirty-five children were taken with no court order, probable cause, or exigent circumstances.
  Why here: The Eighth Circuit's clearest condemnation of mass protective custody taken without individualized court authorization or exigency.
  Quote: “upon de novo review, we see no error in the legal conclusions on the reasonableness of the seizures and the Fourth Amendment violation”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/792293/heartland-academy-community-church-v-michael-waddle/

## DEC-04 · Emergency Removal Decision
- **F.K. v. Iowa District Court for Polk County**, 630 N.W.2d 801 (Iowa 2001)
