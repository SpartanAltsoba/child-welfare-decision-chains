## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **State v. Strauch**, 2015-NMSC-009, 345 P.3d 317
  Holding: The Court held that both privately and publicly employed licensed social workers are mandatory child-abuse reporters under NMSA 1978, Section 32A-4-3(A), so an alleged abuser's statements in private counseling sessions are not protected by the psychotherapist-patient privilege.
  Why here: This is the controlling New Mexico Supreme Court construction of the Section 32A-4-3(A) reporting duty and of who counts as a mandatory reporter.
  Quote: “both privately and publicly employed social workers are mandatory child abuse reporters, in light of the statutory history and the broadly inclusive terms of the Abuse and Neglect Act”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6592364/state-v-strauch/
- **Eckhardt v. Charter Hospital of Albuquerque, Inc.**, 1998-NMCA-017, 124 N.M. 549, 953 P.2d 722
  Holding: The court held that a counseling center director owed the plaintiff a duty to maintain confidentiality and that good faith alone does not defeat a wrongful-disclosure claim, while identifying the child-abuse reporting statute and its immunity provision as a recognized exception to that duty.
  Why here: It states the health-care provider's reporting obligation and cites Section 32A-4-5(B)'s immunity for reports not made in bad faith or with malicious purpose.
  Quote: “Health-care providers must report actual or suspected cases of child neglect or abuse”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1378698/eckhardt-v-charter-hospital-of-albuquerque-inc/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Lowther v. Children Youth and Family Department**, 101 F.4th 742 (10th Cir. 2024)
  Holding: The court affirmed qualified immunity for the CYFD social worker and county officers who entered a New Mexico home without a warrant and removed two children, and clarified that warrantless entry or seizure in the child welfare context requires a threat of imminent harm under both the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments.
  Why here: CYFD and several law-enforcement officers acted jointly within hours of the report, the coordinated response this node describes.
  Quote: “even in the child welfare context, there must be a threat of imminent harm when officials enter a home or seize children without a warrant or judicial authorization”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9500095/lowther-v-children-youth-and-family-department/
- **Jones v. Hunt**, 410 F.3d 1221 (10th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The court held that a New Mexico CYFD social work supervisor who, together with a deputy sheriff, confronted a student at her high school until she agreed to live with her father violated her clearly established Fourth Amendment right to be free of unreasonable seizure, and reversed the dismissal of her Section 1983 suit.
  Why here: The seizure was carried out jointly by a sheriff's deputy and a CYFD supervisor, the paradigm of a coordinated law-enforcement and CPS contact.
  Quote: “we conclude that she has alleged sufficient facts to demonstrate at the 12(B)(6) stage that she was unreasonably seized within the meaning of the Fourth Amendment”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/165973/jones-v-hunt/
- **Malik v. Arapahoe County Department of Social Services**, 191 F.3d 1306 (10th Cir. 1999)
  Holding: The court affirmed denial of qualified immunity to caseworkers, holding it was clearly established that a parent's liberty interest in familial association cannot be taken without adequate pre-deprivation procedures absent extraordinary circumstances, and that an ex parte order procured by misrepresentation and omission is no substitute.
  Why here: The removal was planned jointly by the department and the city police department, the coordinated posture this node describes.
  Quote: “An ex parte hearing based on misrepresentation and omission does not constitute notice and an opportunity to be heard”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/158631/malik-v-arapahoe-county-department-of-social-services/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Calvin T.**, No. S-1-SC-40347 (N.M. July 3, 2025)
  Holding: The Court held that a children's court may join 'any other person' under Rule 10-121(B)(4) NMRA only where that person is a necessary party under the Rule 1-019(A)(1) standard, and remanded for the children's court to apply that standard to the youth's managed care organization.
  Why here: It shows the children's court exercising its own authority to pull additional entities into an abuse and neglect case to obtain services for the child.
  Quote: “the set of parties necessary to afford complete relief sought in such proceedings will include those parties necessary to serve the best interests of the child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10699659/state-ex-rel-cyfd-v-calvin-t/
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Mercer-Smith**, 434 P.3d 930 (N.M. 2018)
  Holding: The Court held that the purpose for which the district court exercised its contempt power against CYFD was not remedial and therefore could not be sustained as civil contempt, and it reversed the contempt order and vacated the nearly four-million-dollar award in its entirety.
  Why here: It arose from a judicial placement order directed at CYFD and shows how far a court may go in enforcing such an order.
  Quote: “we reverse the contempt order and vacate the award in its entirety”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4575657/state-ex-rel-children-youth-families-dept-v-mercer-smith/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Mafin M.**, 2003-NMSC-015, 133 N.M. 827, 70 P.3d 1266
  Holding: Applying the Mathews v. Eldridge factors, the Court held that a mother's procedural due process rights were not violated when the district court terminated her parental rights at a hearing she did not attend, and it reinstated the termination.
  Why here: The case began when the mother herself called police and asked the State to take custody, and the Court treats that voluntary request as the lawful entry point into the dependency system.
  Quote: “because the right to raise one's child is a fundamental right protected by the Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution, termination proceedings must be conducted in a constitutional manner”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6597085/state-ex-rel-children-youth-families-department-v-mafin-m/
- **In re Guardianship of Ashleigh R.**, 2002-NMCA-103, 132 N.M. 772, 55 P.3d 984
  Holding: The court held that the trial court had no authority to appoint guardians over a parent's objection absent proof of present unfitness or extraordinary circumstances, and that voluntary relinquishment of custody plus the children's attachment to the grandparents did not supply those circumstances.
  Why here: It arose from a parent's own voluntary handover of her children to relatives without any agency petition.
  Quote: “We hold that Grandparents presented insufficient evidence to prove that Mother was unfit to care for her children at the time of the hearing”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2598640/in-re-guardianship-of-ashleigh-r/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **In re Termination of Parental Rights of Eventyr J.**, 120 N.M. 463, 902 P.2d 1066 (Ct. App. 1995)
  Holding: The court affirmed termination, holding that clear and convincing evidence supported the findings and that admitting the Citizen's Review Board chairman's testimony about the mother's statements to the Board did not deprive her of due process.
  Why here: It is the New Mexico decision describing how the substitute care review board process under Section 32A-8-6 feeds monitoring findings back into the court file.
  Quote: “we conclude that there was clear and convincing evidence to support the district court's order terminating Respondent's parental rights and that Respondent's due process rights were not violated”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2607019/matter-of-termination-of-parental-rights/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' clause of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act, 42 U.S.C. Section 671(a)(15), confers no enforceable private right on its beneficiaries and creates no implied cause of action.
  Why here: It explains why federal child-welfare performance requirements are enforced through the Secretary's plan-approval and review machinery rather than private suits.
  Quote: “We conclude that 42 U. S. C. § 671 (a)(15) neither confers an enforceable private right on its beneficiaries nor creates an implied cause of action on their behalf”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **Yvonne L. v. New Mexico Department of Human Services**, 959 F.2d 883 (10th Cir. 1992)
  Holding: The court held that children in New Mexico state custody had a clearly established Fourteenth Amendment right to reasonable safety measured by the Youngberg professional judgment standard and reversed qualified immunity on that claim, while affirming that 42 U.S.C. Section 671(a)(10) of the AACWA is too vague and amorphous to be enforced under Section 1983.
  Why here: The plaintiffs' theory was that New Mexico never designated an authority or adopted standards for child care institutions, an oversight failure the court examines.
  Quote: “while it does not require actual knowledge the children will be harmed, it implies abdication of the duty to act professionally in making the placements”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9006488/yvonne-l-v-new-mexico-department-of-human-services/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Marlene C. (In re Esther V.)**, 2011-NMSC-005, 149 N.M. 315
  Holding: The Court held that in a contested adjudication governed by ICWA the district court must always make the 25 U.S.C. Section 1912(d) and (e) findings at the adjudicatory stage, and separately held that New Mexico's ex parte and custody hearings are Section 1922 emergency proceedings to which Section 1912 does not apply; it reversed the neglect adjudication and remanded.
  Why here: This is New Mexico's controlling decision on when ICWA's federal standards attach once a child is identified as an Indian child.
  Quote: “in a contested adjudication to which ICWA applies, the district court must always make the findings of fact required under § 1912(d) and (e) of ICWA at the adjudication stage”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/891709/state-ex-rel-children-youth-families-department-v-marlene-c/
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Douglas B.**, 2023-NMSC-028
  Holding: The Court held that a court must analyze a qualified expert witness's qualifications independently for each of ICWA's two required categories of testimony, and that the witness here was qualified as to tribal cultural standards but not as to serious emotional or physical damage, requiring a new adjudicatory hearing.
  Why here: This is New Mexico's most recent Supreme Court decision on the ICWA qualified-expert-witness requirement that conditions removal and foster placement of an Indian child.
  Quote: “courts must independently analyze qualification in the two categories of required QEW testimony under ICWA and that the testimony can come from one or multiple experts”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10701733/state-ex-rel-cyfd-v-douglas-b/
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Yodell B.**, 2016-NMCA-029
  Holding: The court held that ICWA's active efforts requirement under 25 U.S.C. Section 1912(d) must be proven by clear and convincing evidence and that the evidence at trial was insufficient, and it reversed the termination of the father's parental rights.
  Why here: It fixes both the content and the burden of proof of ICWA's active efforts requirement in New Mexico once tribal jurisdiction is triggered.
  Quote: “the proper standard of proof for determinations under 25 U.S.C. § 1912(d) is the clear and convincing standard, which is applicable to the underlying termination of parental rights proceedings”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3165056/state-ex-rel-children-youth-families-department-v-yodell-b/
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Casey J.**, 2015-NMCA-088
  Holding: The court affirmed termination and held that CYFD's placement of the children complied with the Abuse and Neglect Act and ICWA because the record supported the district court's finding of good cause to deviate from the ICWA placement preferences, and that the father received due process.
  Why here: It applies ICWA's placement preferences, the federal rule that governs where an Indian child may be placed once tribal jurisdiction is triggered.
  Quote: “we conclude that the district court did not err in determining that good cause existed to deviate from the ICWA placement preferences at the time of the initial judicial review”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2811407/state-ex-rel-children-youth-families-dept-v-casey-/

  Holding: The court reversed a neglect adjudication, holding that the district court's findings did not establish that a mental disorder or incapacity was the cause of the mother's inability to care for the child as Section 32A-4-2(E)(4) requires.

## PMC-01 · CFSR Monitoring
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' clause of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act, 42 U.S.C. Section 671(a)(15), confers no enforceable private right on its beneficiaries and creates no implied cause of action.
  Why here: CFSR review exists because Suter leaves federal plan compliance to the funding agency rather than to litigants.
  Quote: “We conclude that 42 U. S. C. § 671 (a)(15) neither confers an enforceable private right on its beneficiaries nor creates an implied cause of action on their behalf”

- **Health and Hospital Corporation of Marion Cty. v. Talevski**, 599 U.S. 166 (2023)
  Holding: The Court held that Spending Clause legislation can create rights enforceable under Section 1983 where the statute unambiguously confers individual federal rights, that the two Federal Nursing Home Reform Act provisions at issue did so, and that nothing in the Act's enforcement scheme impliedly precluded Section 1983 suits; it affirmed.
  Why here: It restates that the typical remedy for a State's non-compliance with a Spending Clause statute is termination of funds, which is exactly the CFSR and program-improvement machinery.
  Quote: “In that respect, our precedent sets a demanding bar: Statutory provisions must unambiguously confer individual federal rights”

  Why here: It shows why CFSR and federal plan review, not private litigation, are the operative checks on state-plan compliance.

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9006488/yvonne-l-v-new-mexico-department-of-human-services/
- **State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Keon H. (In re Anhayla H.)**, 421 P.3d 814 (N.M. 2018)
  Holding: The Court held that substantial evidence supported the district court's finding that CYFD made reasonable efforts to assist an incarcerated father under Section 32A-4-28(B)(2), reversed the Court of Appeals, and affirmed the termination of his parental rights.
  Why here: Reasonable efforts is the state-law expression of the Title IV-E requirement CFSR measures, and this case shows how New Mexico courts review it.
  Quote: “In this case, the Department's efforts, although imperfect, were reasonable.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4508176/state-ex-rel-children-youth-families-dept-v-keon-h-in-re-anhayla/

- Added Roth v. Bookert (cluster 6596803, 119 N.M. 638, 894 P.2d 994 (1995)) for OUT-02: it is the New Mexico Supreme Court's foundational decision on presumptive abandonment as a ground for severing parental rights in a contested adoption, and it holds the presumption is rebutted by showing the parent did not cause the relationship's disintegration.
- Added State ex rel. CYFD v. Ruth Anne E. (cluster 1193799, 1999-NMCA-035) for DEC-05: it is New Mexico's leading statement that a parent who cannot attend the termination hearing is owed meaningful participation, not merely counsel's argument or a deposition, and it reverses the termination on that ground.
- Added State ex rel. CYFD v. Steven (cluster 1196979, 1999-NMCA-141, the case commonly cited as Rosa R.) for DEC-05: it imposes an affirmative duty on the children's court to inquire on the record whether an absent parent waived her due process rights before the case proceeds without her.
- Added State ex rel. CYFD v. Vanessa C. (cluster 1195320, 2000-NMCA-025) for DEC-05: it is the standard New Mexico citation for the clear-and-convincing burden and for the Mathews balancing applied to the judicial-review and futility findings made after a dependency case is opened.
- Added State ex rel. CYFD v. Patricia H. (cluster 6597054, 2002-NMCA-061) for ACT-02 and OUT-01: it is the source of New Mexico's reasonable-efforts formulation, holds that the statutory assistance obligation is a prerequisite to termination, and imports ASFA's fifteen-month time-limited reunification period as guidance on duration.
- Added State ex rel. CYFD v. Shawna C. (cluster 2590615, 2005-NMCA-066) for DEC-03 and FAIL-01: it applies the clear-and-convincing standard separately to each parent, reverses the father's adjudication for insufficient evidence, and rejects a substantive-due-process vagueness attack on the Abuse and Neglect Act's definitions.
- Added State ex rel. CYFD v. Amanda H. (cluster 2627007, 2007-NMCA-029) for DEC-03 and OUT-05: it states the burden and narrow standard of review for abuse and neglect adjudications, reverses on inconclusive toxicology evidence, and holds that dismissal of the underlying case does not moot a sufficiency appeal.
- Added In the Matter of the Guardianship of Victoria R. (cluster 892264, 2009-NMCA-007) for ACT-04 and OUT-03: it defines what a kin caregiver must prove — psychological parenthood plus significant depression on abrupt severance — to rebut the parental presumption and establish extraordinary circumstances under the Kinship Guardianship Act.
- Added M.D.R. v. State ex rel. Human Services Department (cluster 1445142, 114 N.M. 187 (Ct. App. 1992)) for ACT-03 and FAIL-04: it is the governing New Mexico holding that the Tort Claims Act waives no immunity for negligent foster placement, because such placement is neither 'health care services' under Section 41-4-10 nor operation of a 'like facility' under Section 41-4-9.
- Added San Juan Agricultural Water Users Ass'n v. KNME-TV (cluster 891702, 2011-NMSC-011) for FAIL-06 and PMC-05: it holds that an IPRA request may be made through an undisclosed agent such as counsel and that either agent or principal may sue to enforce it, removing the standing objection agencies raise against records requests routed through a lawyer.
- Added Currier v. Doran (cluster 160810, 242 F.3d 905 (10th Cir. 2001)) for OUT-06, FAIL-02 and FAIL-04: it is the New Mexico-arising Tenth Circuit decision establishing that DeShaney does not bar danger-creation liability where officials' affirmative conduct placed a child with a fatally abusive parent, and it is the precedent T.D. v. Patton applies.
- Added Gomes v. Wood (cluster 167696, 451 F.3d 1122 (10th Cir. 2006)) for DEC-04 and INP-12: it adopts the reasonable-suspicion-of-immediate-threat standard for warrantless emergency removal and requires a prompt post-removal hearing, and it is the framework Lowther clarifies.
- Added Dubbs ex rel. Dubbs v. Head Start, Inc. (cluster 163684, 336 F.3d 1194 (10th Cir. 2003)) for DEC-02, INP-07 and INP-08: it holds that non-consensual physical and blood examinations of children at a preschool program are Fourth Amendment searches and that the special needs doctrine does not excuse the absence of parental consent.
- Added Health and Hospital Corporation of Marion Cty. v. Talevski (cluster 10049677, 599 U.S. 166 (2023)) for FAIL-03, PMC-01 and PMC-02: it is the Supreme Court's current framework for Section 1983 enforcement of Spending Clause statutes and must be applied before Suter's reasoning is relied on for Title IV-E compliance questions.
- Added Nguyen v. Bui (cluster 10701724, 2023-NMSC-020, 536 P.3d 482) for ACT-05: ACT-05 previously carried a single case. This is the New Mexico Supreme Court's controlling construction of the Family Violence Protection Act, holding that no showing of imminent danger is required for an order of protection, in a case brought by a petitioner alleging childhood sexual assault — the statute under which an abuser is kept away without removing the child.
- CORRECTION to the verifier's description of Roth v. Bookert: Roth does NOT hold that a child's best interests alone cannot justify severing a fit parent's rights. That was the COURT OF APPEALS' reasoning at 117 N.M. 31, 868 P.2d 1256 (cluster 6596721), which the Supreme Court expressly OVERRULED. The Supreme Court held that '(1) no separate showing or finding of unfitness is required for the termination of parental rights under Section 32-1-54(B)(4)'; it affirmed the judgment voiding the adoption only 'on other legal grounds,' namely that the abandonment presumption was rebutted as a matter of law. Cite the Supreme Court cluster 6596803 (opinion 6471877, Frost, J.), not the Court of Appeals cluster and not the Franchini dissent (opinion 6471878).
- CORRECTION to the verifier's description of Rosa R. (cluster 1196979): the opinion is a TERMINATION due-process case about a mother deported to Mexico, not an investigation case. The 'constitutionally adequate procedures' language the verifier quotes appears in the opinion only as the court's quotation of Oldfield and is therefore not the court's own statement of a rule; it is not used as the quote here. The case is mapped to DEC-05, not to DEC-02. CourtListener styles the cluster 'State ex rel. Children, Youth & Families Department v. Steven' (the lead child); the mother is Rosa R.
- CORRECTION to the verifier's description of In the Matter of the Guardianship of Victoria R. (cluster 892264): the opinion contains no ICWA analysis. It is purely a Kinship Guardianship Act 'extraordinary circumstances' / psychological-parent decision. CourtListener stores it under the caption 'Debbie L. v. Galadriel R.'; the court styles it 'In the Matter of the Guardianship of Victoria R., a Child.' The cluster carries three sub-opinions; the quote used here is from the lead opinion at paragraph 16, not from the separate concurrence.
- CORRECTION regarding Dubbs: the phrase 'There is no social worker exception to the Fourth Amendment' does NOT appear in Dubbs, 336 F.3d 1194 — a full-text search of the retrieved opinion returned no hit. That formulation comes from other Tenth Circuit cases (Roska/Franz line). Dubbs's own holding, used here, is that the special needs doctrine would not excuse the failure to obtain parental consent for the examinations.
