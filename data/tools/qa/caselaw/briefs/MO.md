## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Owen v. Missouri Department of Social Services, Children's Division**, 535 S.W.3d 421 (Mo. Ct. App. 2017)
  Holding: The court reversed and remanded, holding that the Children's Division has statutory authority to investigate and to list on the Central Registry a non-resident who abused a Missouri child outside Missouri, and describing the hotline-to-investigation-to-registry sequence set by sections 210.145 and 210.152.
  Why here: Owen lays out the statutory path a report takes from the toll-free hotline through local-office screening to investigation, which is the framework every mandated report enters.
  Quote: “The circuit court erred as a matter of law in concluding that the Children's Division lacked authority to investigate Owen's out-of-state abuse of a Missouri minor child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5449577/owen-v-missouri-department-of-social-services-childrens-division-child/
- **C.D.J., Jr. v. Missouri Department of Social Services, Children's Division**, 507 S.W.3d 605 (Mo. Ct. App. 2016)
  Holding: The court affirmed, holding that a family whose child is recorded as abused has standing to seek review and that the Children's Division lacks statutory authority to substantiate a report of abuse by an 'unknown perpetrator.'
  Why here: The opinion sets out the four statutory priorities — child safety, family preservation, due process for the accused, and an accurate information system — that govern every report received.
  Quote: “the Children's Division does not have the statutory authority to substantiate a report that an "unknown perpetrator" committed child abuse”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4319067/cdj-jr-by-and-through-his-next-friend-cdj-sr-and-cdj-sr/
- **Shurn v. Monteleone**, 769 S.W.2d 188 (Mo. Ct. App. 1989)
  Holding: The court affirmed dismissal of a father's damages petition against the physician who reported him, holding that Chapter 210 — including the 1982 amendment to section 210.135 withdrawing immunity from a person who intentionally files a false report — creates no statutory cause of action for the subject of a child abuse report, whose remedy, if any, lies in ordinary defamation law.
  Why here: This is the on-point published Missouri authority on section 210.135 reporter immunity, and it tells a mandated reporter that the statute's false-report exception creates no new tort against the reporter.
  Quote: “We find that the statute does not create, expressly or by implication, a statutory cause of action for a private person who becomes the subject of a child abuse report.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1624646/shurn-v-monteleone/
- **Pitts v. Williams**, 315 S.W.3d 755 (Mo. Ct. App. 2010)
  Holding: The court affirmed a writ of mandamus against the Children's Division, holding that section 210.150.2(5) entitles an alleged perpetrator to the investigative records substantiating the finding — subject to statutory redaction and the Division's safety determination — and that an open criminal investigation with no charges filed is not the 'pending criminal charges' that withholds them.
  Why here: This is the source decision for the section 210.145.1 statutory priorities — the safety of the child, retention of the family unit, and the due process rights of the alleged perpetrator — that C.D.J. later restates.
  Quote: “Had the legislature intended for the production of investigative documents to be restricted under section 210.150.2(5) when a criminal "investigation" was pending, it could have and would have said so.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2352927/pitts-v-williams/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Heartland Academy Community Church v. Waddle**, 427 F.3d 525 (8th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The Eighth Circuit affirmed injunctive and declaratory relief against a Missouri chief juvenile officer, holding that removals carried out under ex parte orders obtained with stale information, misinformation and material omissions were unreasonable seizures, and that the taking of thirty-five children with no court order at all was unsupported by probable cause or exigent circumstances.
  Why here: The removals were carried out jointly with law enforcement, and the opinion evaluates the reasonableness of the combined state action.
  Quote: “upon de novo review, we see no error in the legal conclusions on the reasonableness of the seizures and the Fourth Amendment violation”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/792293/heartland-academy-community-church-v-michael-waddle/
- **Webster v. Westlake**, 41 F.4th 1004 (8th Cir. 2022)
  Holding: The Eighth Circuit affirmed the denial of qualified immunity, holding that detectives who arrested a mother on her porch during a child-abuse welfare check without a warrant and without probable cause violated her clearly established rights, and noting they had neither a warrant, an exigency, nor a removal order authorizing entry.
  Why here: The case arises from a school-nurse referral routed through the child-welfare agency to police, the classic law-enforcement referral pathway.
  Quote: “The detectives could have pursued a warrant, or looked for an exigency or exception to the warrant requirement authorizing entry into the home.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7441117/trenisha-webster-v-jennifer-westlake/

  Holding: The Eighth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the officials, holding that a state official who disrupts familial integrity is entitled to qualified immunity when the action is properly founded on a reasonable suspicion of child abuse or of danger to the child's health or welfare, and that this rule is not confined to formal abuse or neglect investigations.
  Why here: The removal was carried out by a deputy and a school official acting on a report, the standard law-enforcement referral scenario.
  Quote: “he or she is entitled to qualified immunity if the action is properly founded upon a reasonable suspicion of child abuse”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/792986/kd-a-minor-through-his-mother-michelle-deason-michelle-deason/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Guyer v. City of Kirkwood**, 38 S.W.3d 412 (Mo. banc 2001)
  Holding: The Court reversed and remanded, holding that under the Sunshine Law incident reports are open records and investigative reports become open once the investigation is inactive, subject only to permitted redactions, and that the personnel-record exception did not close the report.
  Why here: Public access to closed investigative files is the mechanism by which outside oversight bodies and citizens can trigger scrutiny of agency conduct.
  Quote: “Under this statute, "incident reports" are open records, and by implication, once the ensuing investigation becomes inactive, "investigative reports" become open records as well.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2104130/guyer-v-city-of-kirkwood/
- **Scroggins v. Missouri Department of Social Services**, 227 S.W.3d 498 (Mo. Ct. App. 2007)
  Holding: The court reversed and remanded, holding that the Department of Social Services is not a 'law enforcement agency' whose investigative reports are closed under section 610.100.2, that section 210.150 governs confidentiality of its child abuse records with an express exception in section 210.150.5 for cases resulting in a child fatality or near fatality, but that law-enforcement-generated reports held in its files remain closed until that investigation is inactive.
  Why here: The requested disclosure was the mechanism by which outside actors sought to examine the agency's handling of a child's death.
  Quote: “Such investigative reports are closed records under the clear language of § 610.100.2 until the law enforcement investigation becomes inactive.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5280234/scroggins-v-missouri-department-of-social-services/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' requirement of 42 U.S.C. section 671(a)(15) of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act creates no privately enforceable right, so it may not be enforced by children or families through an implied right of action or under 42 U.S.C. section 1983.
  Why here: The case explains why systemic non-compliance surfaces through federal review and audit rather than through individual enforcement suits.
  Quote: “We hold that the Act does not create an enforceable right on behalf of respondents.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/

  Why here: S.E. is the Missouri published decision confirming a tribe's right to intervene and appeal under ICWA, and it shows that ICWA objections must be made at the termination hearing or they are forfeited.

## OUT-02 · Termination of Parental Rights / Adoption
- **Santosky v. Kramer**, 455 U.S. 745 (1982)
  Holding: The Court held that because parents have a fundamental liberty interest in the care, custody and management of their children, a preponderance standard in a permanent neglect proceeding violates due process and a State must support its allegations by at least clear and convincing evidence before terminating parental rights.
  Why here: Santosky is the federal constitutional floor for every termination of parental rights in Missouri and the source of the clear, cogent and convincing standard in section 211.447.
  Quote: “due process requires that the State support its allegations by at least clear and convincing evidence”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/110676/santosky-v-kramer/
- **Lassiter v. Department of Social Services of Durham County**, 452 U.S. 18 (1981)
  Holding: The Court held that the Due Process Clause does not require appointment of counsel in every parental termination proceeding, and left the decision whether counsel is required to be made case by case by the trial court subject to appellate review.
  Why here: Lassiter is the federal baseline on counsel in termination cases, and it is the reason Missouri's statutory right under section 211.462.2 matters so much in practice.
  Quote: “leave the decision whether due process calls for the appointment of counsel for indigent parents in termination proceedings to be answered in the first instance by the trial court”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/110506/lassiter-v-department-of-social-servs-of-durham-cty/
- **In the Interest of Q.A.H.**, 426 S.W.3d 7 (Mo. banc 2014)
  Holding: The Court affirmed termination of the mother's parental rights, holding that substantial evidence supported the statutory grounds under section 211.447.5(2) and (3), including that a parent's mental condition must be assessed by evidence of conduct at the time of the termination hearing and not merely when jurisdiction was taken.
  Why here: This is the Missouri Supreme Court's current statement of the evidentiary threshold for terminating parental rights and of the requirement that unfitness be measured at the time of the termination hearing.
  Quote: “To terminate parental rights, the trial court must find clear, cogent and convincing evidence of at least one of the grounds of termination outlined in section 211.447.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2673499/in-the-interest-of-qah-juvenile-officer-cwm-and-cdm-v-mh/
- **In the Interest of B.H.**, 348 S.W.3d 770 (Mo. banc 2011)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the termination and held that Missouri's two-tier scheme — clear, cogent and convincing evidence of a statutory ground, then a preponderance of the evidence on best interests — comports with due process under Santosky v. Kramer.
  Why here: This is the controlling Missouri authority on which burden of proof applies to each half of a termination judgment and on why the lower best-interests standard survives constitutional challenge.
  Quote: “After a ground for termination has been shown, section 211.447 requires that termination be found in the child's best interest by a preponderance of the evidence standard of proof.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5281215/in-the-interest-of-bh/
- **In the Interest of P.L.O.**, 131 S.W.3d 782 (Mo. banc 2004)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the termination, holding that proof of only one statutory ground by clear, cogent and convincing evidence sustains the judgment, that such findings are reviewed under Murphy v. Carron, and that best interests is proved by a preponderance at trial and reviewed for abuse of discretion.
  Why here: P.L.O. is the leading Missouri statement of the standard of proof and standard of review that govern every contested termination and appeal from one.
  Quote: “On that question, the standard of proof at trial is a preponderance of the evidence, and the standard of review on appeal is abuse of discretion.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5277173/in-the-interest-of-plo/
- **In the Interest of J.P.B.**, 509 S.W.3d 84 (Mo. banc 2017)
  Holding: The Court affirmed termination of an incarcerated father's parental rights, rejecting a vagueness challenge to section 211.447.5(6)(a) because the statutory limitation that incarceration alone is not a ground applies only to the subsections it names and not to an unfitness finding under 211.447.5(6)(a).
  Why here: This is the controlling Missouri authority on how an incarcerated parent's conduct may support a finding of unfitness notwithstanding the statutory caution that incarceration alone is not a ground.
  Quote: “By the statute's plain terms, subdivision (6) of § 211.447.7 applies only to termination that is pursuant to certain subsections or subdivisions.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4336322/in-the-interest-of-jpb-mrs-v-greene-county-juvenile-office/
- **In the Interest of K.A.W.**, 133 S.W.3d 1 (Mo. banc 2004)
  Holding: The Court reversed the termination of the mother's parental rights and remanded, holding that past acts support termination only when convincingly linked to predicted future harm, that findings supporting the earlier assumption of jurisdiction must be updated to describe the parent's condition at the time of the termination hearing, and that the trial court's findings here were insufficient on every ground relied on.
  Why here: K.A.W. is the leading modern Missouri Supreme Court decision on what a termination judgment must actually find, and it is the parent-side counterweight to the affirmances that dominate this node.
  Quote: “To that end, a trial court cannot support a termination by merely incorporating earlier findings supporting its assumption of jurisdiction or some other earlier disposition.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5277221/in-the-interest-of-kaw/
- **Murphy v. Carron**, 536 S.W.2d 30 (Mo. banc 1976)
  Holding: The Court construed Rule 73.01 to mean that the judgment in a court-tried case will be sustained unless there is no substantial evidence to support it, it is against the weight of the evidence, it erroneously declares the law, or it erroneously applies the law, and directed that the labels 'de novo' and 'clearly erroneous' no longer be used.
  Why here: Every sufficiency challenge to a Missouri termination judgment is decided under this standard, which P.L.O. and T.W.C. in this brief invoke by name without citing it.
  Quote: “The use of the words de novo and clearly erroneous is no longer appropriate in appellate review of cases under Rule 73.01.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1684319/murphy-v-carron/

## FAIL-02 · Unlawful Removal

- **Pitts v. Williams**, 315 S.W.3d 755 (Mo. Ct. App. 2010)
  Holding: The court affirmed a writ of mandamus against the Children's Division, holding that section 210.150.2(5) entitles an alleged perpetrator to the investigative records substantiating the finding — subject to statutory redaction and the Division's safety determination — and that an open criminal investigation with no charges filed is not the 'pending criminal charges' that withholds them.

## PMC-06 · Project Milk Carton AI Analysis
- **Guyer v. City of Kirkwood**, 38 S.W.3d 412 (Mo. banc 2001)
  Holding: The Court reversed and remanded, holding that under the Sunshine Law incident reports are open records and investigative reports become open once the investigation is inactive, subject only to permitted redactions, and that the personnel-record exception did not close the report.
  Why here: Any cross-jurisdiction analysis of Missouri agency conduct rests on this presumption that investigative records become disclosable once inactive.
  Quote: “Under this statute, "incident reports" are open records, and by implication, once the ensuing investigation becomes inactive, "investigative reports" become open records as well.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2104130/guyer-v-city-of-kirkwood/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' requirement of 42 U.S.C. section 671(a)(15) of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act creates no privately enforceable right, so it may not be enforced by children or families through an implied right of action or under 42 U.S.C. section 1983.
  Why here: Any accountability analysis of state performance against federal standards must start from Suter's holding that those standards are not privately enforceable.
  Quote: “We hold that the Act does not create an enforceable right on behalf of respondents.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **Scroggins v. Missouri Department of Social Services**, 227 S.W.3d 498 (Mo. Ct. App. 2007)
  Holding: The court reversed and remanded, holding that the Department of Social Services is not a 'law enforcement agency' whose investigative reports are closed under section 610.100.2, that section 210.150 governs confidentiality of its child abuse records with an express exception in section 210.150.5 for cases resulting in a child fatality or near fatality, but that law-enforcement-generated reports held in its files remain closed until that investigation is inactive.
  Why here: Any systemic analysis of Missouri child welfare data rests on this case's allocation of what is open and what is closed.
  Quote: “Such investigative reports are closed records under the clear language of § 610.100.2 until the law enforcement investigation becomes inactive.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5280234/scroggins-v-missouri-department-of-social-services/
