## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **B.W. v. Meade County**, 534 N.W.2d 595 (S.D. 1995)
  Holding: The court affirmed summary judgment for a deputy sheriff and a DSS social worker, holding that SDCL 26-8A-14 immunizes public officials involved in child abuse reporting and investigation and that mere negligence in the investigation does not defeat the statute's good-faith requirement.
  Why here: This is South Dakota's leading construction of the SDCL 26-8A-14 immunity that protects mandatory reporters and the officials who act on their reports, and it fixes good faith - not due care - as the operative test.
  Quote: “Within the bounds of our statute, negligence and lack of good faith are not equivalent.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1700429/bw-v-meade-county/
- **Cotton v. Stange**, 1998 S.D. 81, 582 N.W.2d 25
  Holding: The court reversed the denial of summary judgment and ordered judgment for two DSS social workers, holding they were immune under SDCL 26-8A-14 because the children produced only evidence of negligent investigation, not of bad faith.
  Why here: It confirms that the SDCL 26-8A-14 good-faith shield covers the caseworkers who receive and act on referrals, not just the person who makes the report.
  Quote: “These circumstances might amount to negligence but they do not equate with bad faith.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/900289/cotton-v-stange/
- **E.P. v. Riley**, 1999 S.D. 163, 604 N.W.2d 7
  Holding: The court held that DSS employees owed a common law duty to protect a child from a foster child with known dangerous propensities and reversed summary judgment because fact issues remained on whether the employees acted in good faith under SDCL 26-8A-14.
  Why here: The opinion sets out the full text and reach of SDCL 26-8A-14, the immunity provision that anchors South Dakota's mandatory reporting scheme.
  Quote: “it is clear the immunity provisions of SDCL 26-8A-14 apply to the placement activities of DSS employees involving JH”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/900372/ep-v-riley/
- **In re S.A.**, 2005 S.D. 120, 708 N.W.2d 673
  Holding: The court affirmed the adjudication and termination, holding that the State proved abuse and neglect by clear and convincing evidence and that DSS need not exhaust every possible form of assistance before termination becomes the least restrictive alternative.
  Why here: The case began when a school principal observed marks on a child and called DSS, the paradigm mandatory-reporter chain in South Dakota.
  Quote: “The State has the burden of proving that the children were abused or neglected by clear and convincing evidence.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8244988/in-re-sa/

## INP-02 · Voluntary / Third-Party Report
- **B.W. v. Meade County**, 534 N.W.2d 595 (S.D. 1995)
  Holding: The court affirmed summary judgment for a deputy sheriff and a DSS social worker, holding that SDCL 26-8A-14 immunizes public officials involved in child abuse reporting and investigation and that mere negligence in the investigation does not defeat the statute's good-faith requirement.
  Why here: It extends the same good-faith immunity to any person who reports or cooperates in a child abuse investigation, which is the legal backdrop for non-mandated third-party reports.
  Quote: “Within the bounds of our statute, negligence and lack of good faith are not equivalent.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1700429/bw-v-meade-county/
- **E.P. v. Riley**, 1999 S.D. 163, 604 N.W.2d 7

  Why here: It confirms that the statute's immunity extends to any person who in good faith cooperates with DSS, which covers voluntary reporters.
  Quote: “it is clear the immunity provisions of SDCL 26-8A-14 apply to the placement activities of DSS employees involving JH”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/900372/ep-v-riley/
- **Pfuhl v. Pfuhl**, 2014 S.D. 25, 846 N.W.2d 778
  Holding: The court reversed an order directing the county to pay court-appointed counsel in a protection order proceeding, holding SDCL 26-8A-18 authorizes county payment only in proceedings whose particular subject is a child's status or condition as abused or neglected.
  Why here: The protection order petition repeated child abuse allegations that DSS had already investigated and found unsupported, the classic third-party report in a custody dispute.
  Quote: “the circuit court lacked statutory authority in this case to order Minnehaha to pay appointed counsel's costs”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2713901/pfuhl-v-pfuhl/
- **L.S. v. C.T.**, 2009 S.D. 2, 760 N.W.2d 145
  Holding: The court affirmed denial of a protection order and of a request to restrict visitation, holding the circuit court was not clearly erroneous in finding that the mother had failed to prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the father sexually abused their child.
  Why here: Repeated parental and third-party reports routed through law enforcement, CPS in two states, a children's advocacy center and treating clinicians illustrate how a custody dispute generates voluntary reports and how they are weighed.
  Quote: “Under SDCL 25-10-5, a circuit court may grant a protection order if it finds by a preponderance of the evidence that abuse has occurred.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8279753/ls-v-ct/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request

  Holding: The court remanded a termination of parental rights because the circuit court never made the 25 U.S.C. 1912(f) finding, beyond a reasonable doubt, that the mother's continued custody would likely cause serious emotional or physical damage to the Indian child.
  Why here: The case began when the mother herself contacted DSS about suspected abuse of her daughter, the self-report pathway, and the agency's response then turned on her.
  Quote: “The court erred when it terminated Mother's parental rights without conducting this necessary examination utilizing the proper standard of proof.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3204901/people-ex-rel-ab/

  Why here: The referrals here came from within the family's orbit, and the case sets what a family must later prove to hold the responding workers liable.

  Holding: The court affirmed termination, holding the circuit court did not clearly err in finding that further reasonable efforts would be futile and that statutory good cause under SDCL 26-8A-21.1 and 26-8A-26.1 justified terminating the parents' rights.

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2713909/interest-of-ms-ks/

## INP-07 · Medical Disclosure
- **In re M.V.**, 2011 S.D. 81, 808 N.W.2d 916
  Holding: The court affirmed an abuse and neglect adjudication of an infant with non-accidental head trauma, holding the adjudication concerns the child's status so no parent-specific findings were required, and that a parent's remedy for an erroneous central registry listing lies with the Department, not the juvenile court.
  Why here: Treating physicians determined the injuries were non-accidental and reported them as potential child abuse, the medical-disclosure trigger.
  Quote: “Mother's remedy is with the Department rather than the juvenile court that is adjudicating the status of the child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8246039/in-re-mv/
- **Abdouch v. Burger**, 426 F.3d 982 (8th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The Eighth Circuit affirmed qualified immunity for South Dakota social workers who removed an eleven-week-old infant with seventeen broken bones for about seven months, holding their actions rested on reasonable suspicion of abuse and were not disproportionate.
  Why here: The case was triggered by radiographic findings at a cardiology visit, the medical-disclosure entry point into the system.
  Quote: “the officials' actions must have been based on a reasonable suspicion of abuse and must not have been disproportionate under the circumstances”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3038786/melissa-abdouch-v-vicki-burger/
- **People ex rel. D.T., Jr.**, 2003 S.D. 88, 667 N.W.2d 694
  Holding: The court affirmed an adjudication of abuse and neglect and termination of the father's parental rights, holding that the State proved abuse and neglect by clear and convincing evidence on the parents' history with their other children and that termination was the least restrictive alternative.
  Why here: Hospital personnel reported the newborn's skull fractures and brain hemorrhage to DSS, the medical-disclosure entry point that opened the case.
  Quote: “Termination of parental rights is proper only when the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that termination is the least restrictive alternative in keeping with the child's best interest.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/901073/the-people-of-the-state-of-south-dakota-in-the-interest-of-dt-jr/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Puffy's, LLC v. Department of Health**, 2025 S.D. 10
  Holding: The court affirmed a writ of mandamus compelling the Department of Health to issue a registration certificate, holding that the plain text of its own administrative rule obligated the agency to act and that the applicant had no other adequate remedy.
  Why here: It is the current South Dakota statement that a state agency is bound by the plain text of its own administrative rules, the premise of any compliance audit.
  Quote: “We conclude that the text of ARSD 44:90:03:16 obligated the Department to issue the registration certificate to Puffy's, and Puffy's had a clear legal right to receive it.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10803000/puffys-llc-v-dept-of-health/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Supreme Court held that the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's reasonable efforts requirement creates no privately enforceable right, so beneficiaries cannot sue to enforce it under the Act itself or under 42 U.S.C. 1983.
  Why here: It explains why federal child welfare compliance is enforced through federal administrative oversight rather than private litigation.
  Quote: “We hold that the Act does not create an enforceable right on behalf of respondents.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **Mercer v. South Dakota Attorney General Office**, 2015 S.D. 31, 864 N.W.2d 299
  Holding: The court affirmed denial of a public records request for a death investigation file, holding SDCL 1-27-1.5(5) and SDCL 23-5-11 expressly exempt law enforcement investigative reports and confidential criminal justice information, and that no right to redaction or partial release exists.
  Why here: It shows the statutory ceiling on what an outside investigator or journalist can obtain to trigger or support oversight of a state agency.
  Quote: “The records developed or received by the agencies charged with the duty to investigate Benda's death constitute confidential criminal justice information as defined by statute.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2800709/mercer-v-south-dakota-attorney-general-office/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **People in the Interests of M.C.**, 504 N.W.2d 598 (S.D. 1993)
  Holding: The court reversed an order transferring an Indian child's abuse and neglect proceeding to tribal court, holding that although the tribe's motion was timely, the party opposing transfer was entitled to an evidentiary hearing and that the circuit court had to make specific findings on the good-cause factors.
  Why here: It is South Dakota's foundational ICWA transfer decision - presumptively tribal jurisdiction under 25 U.S.C. 1911(b), the burden on the party opposing transfer, and a mandatory evidentiary hearing with specific findings on each good-cause factor - and it is the parent authority expressly relied on in People ex rel. T.I. and In re E.T.
  Quote: “M.C. was entitled to an evidentiary hearing to establish good cause not to transfer jurisdiction to the Tribe.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2220835/people-in-interests-of-mc/
- **In the Interest of D.M.**, 2004 S.D. 90, 685 N.W.2d 768
  Holding: The court affirmed a finding of good cause to deny the tribe's ICWA transfer motions, holding that the tribe had actual notice, that the State substantially complied with ICWA's notice requirement, and that the motions filed eleven and seventeen months after notice came at an advanced stage of the proceedings.
  Why here: It is the South Dakota ICWA transfer decision immediately preceding People ex rel. T.I. and the controlling application of the advanced-stage/untimeliness good-cause factor, while also holding that the State's ICWA notice obligation continues after a tribe intervenes and is not satisfied merely when convenient.
  Quote: “The Tribe had actual notice of the pending dispositional hearing, and by faxing notice of the hearing to the Tribe, the State substantially complied with ICWA.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/901210/in-the-interest-of-dm/
- **People ex rel. T.I.**, 2005 S.D. 125, 707 N.W.2d 826
  Holding: The court raised the burden on a party opposing an ICWA transfer to clear and convincing evidence of good cause (prospectively), affirmed the denial of transfer because the moving tribe was not the Indian child's tribe, and affirmed termination on qualified expert testimony and proof beyond a reasonable doubt.
  Why here: This is South Dakota's governing decision on ICWA transfer: presumptive tribal jurisdiction, the burden on the opponent, and the requirement that a single tribe be designated the Indian child's tribe.
  Quote: “the standard most consistent with the Act requires clear and convincing evidence of good cause for a state court to refuse to transfer to tribal court”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8244984/people-ex-rel-ti/
- **Interest of A.O., V.O. & C.O.**, 2017 S.D. 30, 896 N.W.2d 652
  Holding: The court reversed the final dispositional order and remanded, holding the circuit court abused its discretion by denying the mother's and the tribe's ICWA transfer motions without an evidentiary hearing and without specific factual findings on good cause.
  Why here: It is the controlling South Dakota holding that an ICWA transfer motion cannot be denied on argument alone - a hearing and written findings are required.
  Quote: “The circuit court was required to conduct an evidentiary hearing on the question whether good cause existed to deny Mother's and the Tribe's motions to transfer”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4171620/interest-of-ao-vo-co/
- **In re E.T.**, 2019 S.D. 23, 927 N.W.2d 111
  Holding: The court held the circuit court abused its discretion by granting a tribe's late oral transfer motion without hearing the available testimony of the child's physician, and reversed for a properly developed record on good cause.
  Why here: It is the converse of A.O.: a transfer granted on a thin record is as reversible as a transfer denied on one, so the record requirement cuts both ways.
  Quote: “circuit courts need the benefit of a sufficiently developed record to assist in the good cause determination”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8253742/in-re-et/
- **Matter of Dependency and Neglect of A.L.**, 442 N.W.2d 233 (S.D. 1989)
  Holding: The court affirmed denial of a tribe's petition to transfer a dependency and neglect proceeding, holding that the BIA good-cause guidelines are interpretive rather than binding and that the trial court was not clearly erroneous in finding the tribe's transfer request, made a year after notice, untimely.
  Why here: It is South Dakota's earliest construction of ICWA's undefined 'good cause to the contrary' and the source of the rule, relied on in 2011 S.D. 8 and In the Interest of D.M., that the BIA guidelines inform but do not bind the court and that timeliness is judged case by case.
  Quote: “We agree with State that the BIA guidelines are not binding. They are interpretative rather than legislative in nature.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1237582/matter-of-dependency-and-neglect-of-al/

- Added People in the Interests of M.C., 504 N.W.2d 598 (S.D. 1993) (cluster 2220835), for INP-11 and FAIL-01: it is the foundational South Dakota ICWA transfer decision, holding that the party opposing transfer is entitled to an evidentiary hearing and that the circuit court must make specific findings on the good-cause factors rather than a conclusory 'no good cause to the contrary.' It is the parent authority expressly relied on by People ex rel. T.I. and In re E.T., both already in the brief.
- Added In the Interest of D.M., 2004 S.D. 90, 685 N.W.2d 768 (cluster 901210), for INP-11: it is the ICWA transfer decision immediately preceding T.I. and the controlling South Dakota application of the advanced-stage/untimeliness good-cause factor (motions filed eleven and seventeen months after notice), while also holding that faxed notice plus actual notice constitutes substantial compliance and that the State's duty to keep an intervening tribe informed continues after initial notice. The verifier's characterization of D.M. as the source of the 'mere discretion to override a transfer is inconsistent with congressional intent' proposition was not located in the D.M. opinion text; that phrasing appears in T.I.'s discussion, and the holding stated here is taken from D.M.'s own majority opinion.

D.T., Jr., 2003 S.D. 88, 667 N.W.2d 694 (cluster 901073), for DEC-05, OUT-02, INP-07 and INP-12: it is the customary South Dakota primary citation for the clear-and-convincing burden at adjudication and for the SDCL 26-8A-27 least-restrictive-alternative standard at termination, and the case In re S.A. cites on those points. - Added Matter of Dependency and Neglect of A.L., 442 N.W.2d 233 (S.D. 1989) (cluster 1237582), for INP-11 and ACT-04: it is South Dakota's earliest construction of ICWA's undefined 'good cause to the contrary' and the source of the rule - relied on in 2011 S.D. 8 and In the Interest of D.M. - that the BIA guidelines are interpretive rather than binding and that timeliness is assessed case by case with flexibility.
- Added Argus Leader Media v. Hogstad, 2017 S.D. 57, 902 N.W.2d 778 (cluster 4427727), for PMC-05, PMC-02, PMC-06 and FAIL-06: it is the leading post-Mercer South Dakota Public Records Act decision, holding that the Act's exceptions must be construed narrowly against the government and that a public body cannot defeat disclosure by contracting for confidentiality. Its reliance on SDCL 1-27-1.3's liberal-construction directive for fiscal and expenditure records is the statutory hook for any financial audit of South Dakota child-welfare spending, and it materially qualifies the impression left by Mercer alone.
- Added Rapid City Journal v. Delaney, 2011 S.D. 55, 804 N.W.2d 388 (cluster 902061), for PMC-05, PMC-06 and FAIL-06: it holds that the First Amendment affords the media and public a qualified right of access to civil trials in South Dakota and adopts the Publicker procedure - articulated overriding interest, findings specific enough for appellate review, and narrow tailoring - that a court must follow before closing a proceeding or sealing records. It is the access framework on which Matter of Implicated Individual builds.
- Added Rapid City Journal v. Callahan, 2022 S.D. 38, 977 N.W.2d 742 (cluster 9508040), for PMC-05, PMC-06 and FAIL-06: it is the most recent South Dakota decision on press access to court files, holding that a newspaper denied access has standing to challenge a sealing order and that a court may not give a corrective nunc pro tunc order retroactive effect so as to extinguish a third party's already-pending access request.
- Added L.S. v. C.T., 2009 S.D. 2, 760 N.W.2d 145 (cluster 8279753), for INP-04, ACT-05, DEC-01 and INP-02: INP-04 previously rested on a single case (Pfuhl) in node_map, and this decision supplies a second, fuller illustration of a civil court directing its own fact-finding - court-ordered psychological and psychosexual evaluations, and the results of five separate CPS and law-enforcement investigations - on child abuse allegations raised in a visitation dispute.
