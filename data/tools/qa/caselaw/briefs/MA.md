## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Kyle K. v. Department of Children and Families**, No. 22-P-35 (Mass. App. Ct. Oct. 6, 2023)
  Holding: The Appeals Court upheld a DCF hearing officer's decision affirming a "supported" finding of sexual abuse, holding that judicial review under G. L. c. 30A asks only whether substantial evidence supports the conclusion that there was reasonable cause to believe the abuse occurred, with particular deference to the hearing officer's credibility determinations.
  Why here: The opinion sets out the mandated reporter's § 51A duty to notify DCF on reasonable cause to believe a child is suffering abuse, and traces what follows.
  Quote: “As to facts, where there is substantial evidence to support the findings of the agency, the court will not substitute its own view of the facts.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9433181/kyle-k-v-department-of-children-and-families/
- **Adoption of Virgil**, 93 Mass. App. Ct. 298, 102 N.E.3d 1009 (2018)
  Holding: The Appeals Court affirmed termination, holding that homelessness, poverty and financial instability cannot alone support termination but may properly be considered as part of the unfitness determination where they are tied to the parent's inability to provide minimally adequate care.
  Why here: The case begins with a pediatrician's § 51A report of neglect, a mandated reporter's disclosure in a medical setting.
  Quote: “While homelessness, poverty, and financial instability alone are not sufficient to terminate a person's parental rights, they are proper considerations in an unfitness determination.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4502553/adoption-of-virgil/

## INP-02 · Voluntary / Third-Party Report
- **Custody of Eleanor**, 414 Mass. 795, 610 N.E.2d 938 (1993)
  Holding: The SJC held that a trial judge's subsidiary findings stand unless clearly erroneous but the appellate court independently reviews whether those findings establish parental unfitness by clear and convincing evidence, and it reversed the Juvenile Court judgment placing the children in the department's permanent custody.
  Why here: The case opens with a § 51A report filed after a physician's examination and a mother's disclosure, showing how a third-party report becomes a custody case.
  Quote: “although the judge’s findings were not clearly erroneous, the evidence on which they were based did not prove parental unfitness by clear and convincing evidence”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2110838/custody-of-eleanor/
- **Lindsay v. Department of Social Services**, 439 Mass. 789, 791 N.E.2d 866 (2003)
  Holding: The SJC clarified that on judicial review of a "supported" finding of abuse or neglect, the court asks only whether substantial evidence supports the hearing officer's conclusion that there was reasonable cause to believe the maltreatment occurred, and it upheld the supported finding of neglect.
  Why here: Lindsay arises from a third-party report of neglect at a day care center and traces it through investigation to a supported finding.
  Quote: “there need only be “substantial evidence” supporting the conclusion that there was “reasonable cause to believe” that Lindsay neglected Adrianna”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6578755/lindsay-v-department-of-social-services/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Gallagher v. South Shore Hospital, Inc.**, 101 Mass. App. Ct. 807 (2022)
  Holding: The Appeals Court held that a protective services caseworker and a police officer who entered a home during an abuse investigation without a warrant, consent, court order, or a reasonable belief of an emergency could be found to have violated the Fourth Amendment and art. 14, and that no community caretaking exception permits entry into a home.
  Why here: Gallagher involves a joint entry by a protective services caseworker and a police officer, the standard co-response posture in abuse investigations.
  Quote: “there cannot be a community caretaking exception permitting entry into the home under art. 14 of the Massachusetts Declaration of Rights”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10271857/susan-gallagher-v-south-shore-hospital-inc/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **Care & Protection of Stephen**, 401 Mass. 144, 514 N.E.2d 1087 (1987)
  Holding: The SJC held that parents in a care and protection proceeding are entitled to the effective assistance of appointed counsel, measured by the criminal-law Saferian standard, but affirmed because the department proved unfitness by clear and convincing evidence and the ineffectiveness claim was not properly raised below.
  Why here: The court describes the G. L. c. 119, § 23 court-appointed investigator's report as the evidentiary spine of the adjudication, showing how judicially ordered investigation feeds the case.
  Quote: “Accordingly, we conclude that parents are entitled to the effective assistance of counsel pursuant to G. L. c. 119, § 29.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1984934/care-protection-of-stephen/
- **Adoption of Georgia**, 433 Mass. 62, 739 N.E.2d 694 (2000)
  Holding: The SJC affirmed termination, holding that the department discharged its duty to strengthen and encourage the family unit where it worked diligently without success and the additional services the mother sought would not have solved her chronic problems, and that a court appointed special advocate appointed as guardian ad litem may testify and submit a report.
  Why here: Georgia authorizes the judge to appoint a CASA as guardian ad litem, an investigative resource the court itself sets in motion.
  Quote: “Mary’s argument that the department failed to fulfil its duty to strengthen and encourage the family unit lacks merit.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6578208/adoption-of-georgia/
- **Care & Protection of Georgette**, 439 Mass. 28, 785 N.E.2d 356 (2003)
  Holding: The SJC affirmed the denial of the children's Mass. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(6) motion for a new trial, holding that the Saferian standard governs ineffective-assistance claims in care and protection proceedings, that counsel's joint representation of siblings created no actual conflict and no prejudice was shown given overwhelming proof of the father's unfitness, and directing that counsel for children follow the 1999 CPCS performance standards on representing a child's expressed preferences.
  Why here: Georgette sets out when a child's appointed counsel should ask the court to appoint a guardian ad litem, the judicially ordered investigative step available when counsel and child diverge.
  Quote: “their positions, based on mature expression, are entitled to weight in custody proceedings (although not determinative)”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6578697/care-protection-of-georgette/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **Guardianship of V.V.**, 470 Mass. 590, 24 N.E.3d 1022 (2015)
  Holding: The SJC held that an indigent parent is entitled to appointed counsel in a private guardianship proceeding concerning her child, reasoning that the parent's liberty interest does not depend on whether the State is a party.
  Why here: V.V. addresses the private, non-agency route by which a child's custody changes hands and holds that the parent's protections travel with it.
  Quote: “There is no reason why an indigent parent whose child is the subject of a guardianship proceeding should receive the benefit of counsel only if the State is involved.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2778096/guardianship-of-vv/
- **Guardianship of K.N.**, 476 Mass. 762, 73 N.E.3d 271 (2017)
  Holding: The SJC held that a guardian who is a de facto parent has no liberty interest supporting a procedural due process right to counsel in a removal proceeding, but that the Probate and Family Court's equitable powers permit a judge to appoint counsel for an indigent guardian where the child's best interests warrant it.
  Why here: K.N. defines the standing and procedural protections of a non-parent caregiver who has assumed the parenting role by private arrangement.
  Quote: “A guardian who is a de facto parent does not have a liberty interest in her relationship with her ward sufficient to warrant the appointment of counsel.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4382785/guardianship-of-kn/
- **Youmans v. Ramos**, 429 Mass. 774, 711 N.E.2d 165 (1999)
  Holding: The SJC affirmed a Probate and Family Court judgment awarding the father custody of his daughter but ordering continuing visitation between the child and the maternal aunt who had been her guardian and de facto parent, holding that the judge's reliance on the child's best interests was appropriate and fell within the court's general equity jurisdiction notwithstanding the absence of any statute authorizing visitation for an aunt or former guardian.
  Why here: Youmans concerns the private guardianship route by which a relative becomes a child's parent in fact, and what protections the child retains when a legal parent later reclaims custody.
  Quote: “The judge's consideration of Tamika's best interests was entirely appropriate.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6577898/youmans-v-ramos/

## INP-07 · Medical Disclosure
- **In the Matter of McCauley**, 409 Mass. 134, 565 N.E.2d 411 (1991)

  Why here: The hospital's petition is the paradigm medical-setting trigger for state protective intervention short of removal.
  Quote: “We conclude that the interests of Elisha and of the State outweigh her parents’ rights to refuse the medical treatment.”

  Why here: The case begins with treating physicians reporting the parents' abandonment of medically indicated treatment, the classic medical-disclosure trigger.
  Quote: “the decision below was consistent with both the best interests of the child and the applicable interests of the State”

## INP-08 · Educational Disclosure
- **Custody of a Minor**, 383 Mass. 595, 421 N.E.2d 63 (1981)
  Holding: The SJC affirmed a care and protection judgment committing a child to the Department of Public Welfare, holding that the absence of an express finding of general parental unfitness did not require reversal where the subsidiary findings were tantamount to unfitness as to this child's welfare, and that the child's wishes were properly given weight.
  Why here: The case originates in a fifth-grade teacher's observations of behavior problems, absenteeism and the child's complaints, which produced the investigation.
  Quote: “The failure of the trial judge to make an express finding of general parental unfitness also does not warrant a reversal in this case.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2230315/custody-of-a-minor/

L. c. 30A asks only whether substantial evidence supports the conclusion that there was reasonable cause to believe the abuse occurred, with particular deference to the hearing officer's credibility determinations.
  Why here: Statements relayed by the child's school guidance counselor were part of the record the hearing officer weighed.
  Quote: “As to facts, where there is substantial evidence to support the findings of the agency, the court will not substitute its own view of the facts.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9433181/kyle-k-v-department-of-children-and-families/
- **Covell v. Department of Social Services**, 439 Mass. 766, 791 N.E.2d 877 (2003)
  Holding: The SJC affirmed the department's decision to "support" an allegation of sexual abuse and to list the plaintiff in its registry of alleged perpetrators, holding that a supported finding requires only reasonable cause to believe the abuse occurred, that the substantial evidence test does not become more stringent because the consequences of the agency decision are severe, and that hearsay bearing indicia of reliability can by itself be substantial evidence.
  Why here: The case begins with a school guidance counsellor's G. L. c. 119, § 51A report of suspected sexual abuse, the paradigm educational-setting disclosure.
  Quote: “Substantial evidence may be based on hearsay alone if that hearsay has "indicia of reliability."”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6578754/covell-v-department-of-social-services/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Care and Protection of Adele**, No. SJC-13601 (Mass. Apr. 23, 2025)
  Holding: The SJC held that although impounded care and protection records carry a strong statutory privacy interest, a journalist showed good cause under Rule 7 of the Uniform Rules on Impoundment Procedure for release of audio recordings of hearings in the case of a child who was placed with her father without a completed ICPC home study and later went missing and died, and the judge abused her discretion in refusing release.
  Why here: Adele is the Massachusetts vehicle by which outside scrutiny, here a documentary journalist, reaches a closed child welfare case and exposes systemic failure.
  Quote: “a person seeking access to impounded materials in a care and protection proceeding faces a high hurdle”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10382710/care-and-protection-of-adele/
- **People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals, Inc. v. Department of Agricultural Resources**, 477 Mass. 280, 76 N.E.3d 227 (2017)
  Holding: The SJC construed the public safety and privacy exemptions to the public records law, holding that the statutes favor disclosure by presuming records are public and placing on the custodian the burden of proving with specificity that an exemption applies.
  Why here: PETA supplies the doctrinal presumption that lets oversight bodies and the public obtain state agency records.
  Quote: “Together, these statutes, and our cases interpreting them, favor disclosure of public records in two primary ways.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4400074/people-for-the-ethical-treatment-of-animals-inc-v-department-of-agr/
- **Care and Protection of M.C.**, 479 Mass. 246, 94 N.E.3d 379 (2018)
  Holding: The SJC held that the good cause standard of Rule 7 of the Uniform Rules on Impoundment Procedure governs requests by the parties or the Commonwealth for access to impounded care and protection records, that the proponent of release bears the burden of establishing good cause, and that a parent's decision to testify and to waive the psychotherapist privilege at the care and protection trial does not waive either at a later criminal trial; it remanded for application of that standard.
  Why here: M.C. I establishes the mechanism by which an outside actor can reach the sealed record of a child welfare case, the precondition for any external review of what the agency did.
  Quote: “a party or the Commonwealth seeking to pierce this veil of privacy should bear the burden of demonstrating good cause for release from impoundment”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4485241/care-and-protection-of-mc/
- **Care and Protection of M.C.**, No. SJC-12652 (Mass. Oct. 28, 2019)
  Holding: On review after remand, the SJC affirmed the Juvenile Court's partial release of impounded care and protection records to the father and the Commonwealth but vacated so much of the order as released the mother's testimony and her psychotherapist's testimony to the Commonwealth, holding that concerns of trial efficiency alone cannot establish good cause where the privilege against self-incrimination and the patient-psychotherapist privilege are both implicated.
  Why here: M.C. II shows the good cause factors actually applied document by document, which is the practical measure of how far an outside reviewer can see into a care and protection file.
  Quote: “This concern, alone, does not serve to tip the balance toward release from impoundment where the mother's Fifth Amendment rights and the patient-psychotherapist privilege are both at play.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4673682/care-and-protection-of-mc/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Adoption of Uday**, No. 16-P-801 (Mass. App. Ct. Feb. 16, 2017)
  Holding: The Appeals Court affirmed a termination decree over an Indian Child Welfare Act challenge, holding that the department satisfied 25 U.S.C. § 1912(a) notice where the three federally recognized Cherokee tribes responded that the child was not an "Indian child," and rejecting the related reasonable-efforts and unfitness claims.
  Why here: Uday is the only Massachusetts appellate decision located that reaches an ICWA issue, and it governs the department's notice obligation when a child may have tribal ancestry.
  Quote: “These responsive letters from the three Cherokee tribes demonstrate that the department in fact did comply with ICWA notice provision.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4349361/adoption-of-uday/
- **Haaland v. Brackeen**, 599 U.S. 255 (2023)
  Holding: The Supreme Court rejected all challenges to the Indian Child Welfare Act, holding that Congress had authority to enact it, that its placement preferences and active-efforts provisions do not violate the anticommandeering doctrine, and that the equal protection and nondelegation claims failed for lack of standing.
  Why here: Brackeen confirms that ICWA's jurisdictional, active-efforts and placement-preference requirements bind Massachusetts state courts in any proceeding involving an Indian child.
  Quote: “we reject all of petitioners’ challenges to the statute, some on the merits and others for lack of standing”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406660/haaland-v-brackeen/

## INP-12 · Emergency / Protective Custody
- **Care & Protection of Robert**, 408 Mass. 52, 556 N.E.2d 993 (1990)
  Holding: The SJC held that the "reasonable cause" standard governs only the initial ex parte emergency hearing under G. L. c. 119, § 24, and that a decision to maintain removal at the seventy-two hour hearing must instead be supported by a fair preponderance of the evidence; the District Court's use of the lower standard was improper and a new hearing was ordered.
  Why here: This is the controlling Massachusetts authority on the burden the Commonwealth must carry to keep a child in protective custody after an emergency taking.
  Quote: “A new seventy-two hour hearing determination must be made in the District Court, based on a “fair preponderance of the evidence.””
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2098870/care-protection-of-robert/
- **Care & Protection of Zita**, 455 Mass. 272, 915 N.E.2d 1067 (2009)
  Holding: The SJC held that although a Juvenile Court judge may act ex parte on an emergency custody petition, it was improper at the ensuing seventy-two hour hearing to rely on the unsworn social worker's petition and on facts the judge knew from earlier proceedings, and it remanded for a prompt new temporary custody hearing.
  Why here: Zita confirms the two-step structure of Massachusetts protective custody: an ex parte emergency order followed by an evidentiary hearing within seventy-two hours.
  Quote: “A judge in the Juvenile Court may conduct an ex parte hearing to determine whether to grant an emergency petition to obtain custody of a child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6580075/care-protection-of-zita/
- **Care & Protection of Lillian**, 445 Mass. 333, 837 N.E.2d 269 (2005)
  Holding: The SJC held that a care and protection petition is legally sufficient if it pleads specific facts, on knowledge or information and belief, falling within one of the four statutory grounds in G. L. c. 119, § 24, and that denial of emergency transfer of custody does not bar the petitioner from proceeding to a temporary custody hearing under § 25.
  Why here: Lillian separates the emergency-custody question from the underlying petition, so a refusal to remove immediately does not end the protective process.
  Quote: “We conclude that a petition is sufficient if it contains allegations asserting specific facts, based on personal knowledge or information and belief, that fall within the statute’s enumerated concerns.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6579233/care-protection-of-lillian/
- **Stanley v. Illinois**, 405 U.S. 645 (1972)
  Holding: The Supreme Court held that a State may not take children from a parent without a hearing on that parent's fitness, and struck down Illinois' presumption that unmarried fathers are unfit.
  Why here: Stanley is the source of the constitutional requirement that a fitness hearing follow any state removal of a child from a parent.
  Quote: “We have concluded that all Illinois parents are constitutionally entitled to a hearing on their fitness before their children are removed from their custody.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/108497/stanley-v-illinois/
- **Hatch v. Department for Children, Youth & Their Families**, 274 F.3d 12 (1st Cir. 2001)
  Holding: The First Circuit affirmed denial of leave to amend as futile, holding that a child protective official who removes a child on an objectively reasonable suspicion of abuse does not violate clearly established law and is entitled to qualified immunity notwithstanding the parent's right to family integrity.
  Why here: Hatch fixes the constitutional threshold, objectively reasonable suspicion, for taking a child into protective custody in the First Circuit.
  Quote: “Because the welfare of the child is paramount, an objectively reasonable suspicion of abuse justifies protective measures.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/775863/richard-h-hatch-jr-v-department-for-children-youth-and-their-families/
- **Care and Protection of Doretta**, 101 Mass. App. Ct. 584 (2022)
  Holding: The Appeals Court affirmed a temporary custody order, holding that social worker affidavits filed with the petition are not categorically inadmissible at the seventy-two hour hearing but are admissible only to the extent permitted by Adoption of Luc, that unpreserved hearsay objections are waived, and that the judge did not deny the father due process by intermittently muting his participation in a remote hearing.
  Why here: Doretta applies the Robert fair-preponderance standard to the continuation of protective custody after emergency removal.
  Quote: “The judge acted within her discretion, and within constitutional bounds, in intermittently limiting the father's participation at the hearing, which took place through an Internet-based video conferencing platform.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10271876/care-and-protection-of-doretta/
- **Care & Protection of Perry**, 438 Mass. 1014, 782 N.E.2d 506 (2003)
  Holding: The SJC dismissed as moot a mother's G. L. c. 211, § 3 appeal from an order treating her non-appearance as a waiver of the seventy-two hour hearing, the Juvenile Court having vacated that order and held the hearing, and stated that the hearing exists to determine whether the child would be in immediate danger of serious abuse or neglect if returned and that continuances sought to delay it for a parent's own benefit are properly denied.
  Why here: Perry states the substantive question the seventy-two hour hearing answers, the question to which Robert's fair-preponderance burden of proof attaches.
  Quote: “whether a child will be in immediate danger of serious abuse or neglect if returned to his parents or custodian”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/6578688/care-protection-of-perry/

## DEC-02 · Accept for Investigation / Assessment
- **Frazier v. Bailey**, 957 F.2d 920 (1st Cir. 1992)
  Holding: The First Circuit affirmed summary judgment for social workers and private child protection agencies sued under § 1983 by a Massachusetts father over their role in a sexual abuse investigation, holding that the familial-integrity right is too fact-dependent and unsettled to have been clearly established and that litigation communications were privileged.
