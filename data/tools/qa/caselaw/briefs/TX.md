## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Perry v. S.N.**, 973 S.W.2d 301 (Tex. 1998)
  Holding: Texas does not recognize a negligence per se claim for failing to report child abuse under Family Code § 261.109; the reporting duty is enforced criminally, not through private tort liability.
  Why here: The controlling Texas answer to the question families ask at this node: the § 261.101 duty to report and the § 261.109 misdemeanor for failing to report create no private cause of action, so a failure to report (or to act on a report) is punished criminally and administratively, not in a civil damages suit.
  Quote: “we hold that it is not appropriate to adopt Family Code section 261.109(a) as establishing a duty and standard of conduct in tort”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2466738/perry-v-sn/
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District**, 15 F.3d 443 (5th Cir. 1994) (en banc)
  Holding: Schoolchildren have a Fourteenth Amendment liberty interest in bodily integrity that a teacher's sexual abuse violates, and school officials may be personally liable under § 1983 for supervisory failures that manifest deliberate indifference to that right; the principal's claim survived summary judgment while the superintendent received qualified immunity.
  Why here: Gives the mandatory-reporter duty of school employees a federal consequence: a principal who learns of abuse and does nothing faces personal § 1983 liability, not merely a state-law misdemeanor.
  Quote: “In short, supervisory liability for deliberate indifference to constitutional violations committed by subordinates was clearly established when the events in this case occurred.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/661734/jane-doe-v-taylor-independent-school-district-mike-caplinger-in-his/
- **Bird v. W.C.W.**, 868 S.W.2d 767 (Tex. 1994)
  Holding: A mental-health professional who examines a child for abuse owes no professional duty of care to a third party — here the accused father — to avoid negligently misdiagnosing the child, and the affidavit she filed in the family court was privileged as a judicial-proceeding communication.
  Why here: The civil-liability half of the mandatory-reporting scheme: a professional reporter owes no duty to the person she accuses, which is what makes the reporting duty in § 261.101 practically costless to obey and costly to question.
  Quote: “We hold that a mental health professional owes no professional duty of care to a third party to not negligently misdiagnose a condition of a patient.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1728574/bird-v-wcw/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **Gates v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 537 F.3d 404 (5th Cir. 2008)
  Holding: The government may not seize a child from his parents absent a court order, parental consent or exigent circumstances, exigency meaning reasonable cause to believe the child is in imminent danger of physical or sexual abuse; an uncorroborated anonymous tip will rarely suffice. Because that law was not clearly established when these events occurred, every individual defendant received qualified immunity and summary judgment was affirmed.
  Why here: Decided on facts where CPS investigators and county deputies acted together; the panel holds that law-enforcement officers may not blindly rely on the agency's removal decision, which is the core risk in a joint referral.
  Quote: “an anonymous tip regarding child abuse will rarely be sufficient to justify the seizure of a child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/62905/gates-v-texas-deparment-of-protective-regulatory-services/
- **Roe v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 299 F.3d 395 (5th Cir. 2002)
  Holding: A CPS caseworker's visual body-cavity search of a child raised a Fourth Amendment claim, and in non-exigent circumstances the worker must obtain a warrant or court order rather than proceed on a parent's silence; a parent's consent to an investigative home interview is not consent to such a search, and consent to entry may be revoked. Because those rights were not clearly established, the worker received qualified immunity.
  Why here: Decided on a CPS investigation launched from a Statewide Intake hotline call and conducted alongside law-enforcement interest; it defines the consent a worker must actually obtain at the door.
  Quote: “In non-exigent circumstances, the worker then has time to obtain a warrant either personally to conduct a visual body cavity search or to have a physician perform it.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/778657/mary-roe-v-texas-department-of-protective-and-regulatory-services/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **In re E.N.C.**, 384 S.W.3d 796 (Tex. 2012)
  Holding: Evidence of a decades-old offense and a deportation, with no service plan ever offered and no evidence of endangering conduct, is legally insufficient to terminate a father's rights.
  Why here: The Department never evaluated the father's means or offered him a plan; the opinion is the Texas authority for the proposition that the agency must engage a parent before condemning him.
  Quote: “We conclude the evidence is legally insufficient and, accordingly, reverse the court of appeals”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/895457/in-the-interest-of-enc-jac-sal-nag-and-cgl/
- **In re C.J.C.**, No. 19-0694 (Tex. June 26, 2020)
  Holding: The constitutional presumption that a fit parent acts in the best interest of the child applies in any proceeding in which a nonparent seeks conservatorship, possession or access over the objection of a fit parent, including a Chapter 156 modification suit where no statutory presumption applies, and a parent does not waive it by having invoked the courts himself.
  Why here: C.J.C. holds that a fit parent who himself invoked the judicial process does not thereby forgo the presumption — the Texas answer to the argument that asking the system for help is evidence of unfitness.
  Quote: “a court must apply the presumption that a fit parent—not the court—determines the best interest of the child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10018442/in-re-cjc-v-the-state-of-texas/
- **Lewelling v. Lewelling**, 796 S.W.2d 164 (Tex. 1990)
  Holding: A non-parent seeking managing conservatorship over a parent must prove that appointing the parent would significantly impair the child's physical health or emotional development; a mother's unemployment, crowded housing, dependence on relatives and history as a victim of spousal abuse are no evidence of such impairment.
  Why here: Authority that a parent's material circumstances — no job, crowded home, living with family — cannot themselves supply the evidence of unfitness that a self-report or request for help often puts in front of the agency.
  Quote: “The fact that Brenda was unemployed at the time of the custody hearing and living in somewhat crowded conditions is no evidence of significant impairment to the child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2456505/lewelling-v-lewelling/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **M.D. ex rel. Stukenberg v. Abbott**, 907 F.3d 237 (5th Cir. 2018)
  Holding: Texas long-term foster children have a substantive due process right to be free from an unreasonable risk of harm, and the State's caseworker-caseload and monitoring failures supported class-wide liability, although the district court's injunction was significantly overbroad.
  Why here: The controlling systemic authority for Texas: it is the Fifth Circuit's framework for caseworker caseloads, placement oversight and monitoring of children in DFPS conservatorship, and — because Suter forecloses private Title IV-E enforcement — it is the mechanism that has actually produced federal oversight of this node in Texas.
  Quote: “we hold that the General Class and the LFC subclass were properly certified”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4545304/m-d-ex-rel-stukenberg-v-abbott/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: Title IV-E's 'reasonable efforts' requirement creates no federal right enforceable by children or parents under 42 U.S.C. § 1983; enforcement runs through HHS and the state-plan process.
  Why here: Determines the enforcement channel for the federal requirement behind this node: the Title IV-E/ASFA command is not privately enforceable, so compliance is policed by HHS, the state plan and the CFSR — not by a parent's or child's lawsuit.
  Quote: “We hold that the Act does not create an enforceable right on behalf of respondents”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Haaland v. Brackeen**, 599 U.S. 255 (2023)
  Holding: Congress had constitutional authority to enact ICWA and its commands to state courts and agencies do not violate the anticommandeering doctrine; the equal-protection and nondelegation challenges failed for lack of standing.
  Why here: This Texas-originated case settles that ICWA validly binds Texas courts and DFPS from the moment an Indian child is identified at intake.
  Quote: “we affirm the judgment of the Court of Appeals regarding Congress’s constitutional authority to enact ICWA”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406660/haaland-v-brackeen/
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield**, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)
  Holding: 'Domicile' under ICWA is a matter of uniform federal law, and twins born off-reservation to parents domiciled on the reservation were themselves domiciled there; the tribal court therefore had exclusive jurisdiction under 25 U.S.C. § 1911(a) and the state adoption decree was vacated.
  Why here: The source of ICWA domicile and exclusive tribal jurisdiction. Because Texas has no state ICWA statute, a Texas court applies this federal rule directly, and parental consent to a state-court adoption cannot defeat it.
  Quote: “In enacting the ICWA Congress confirmed that, in child custody proceedings involving Indian children domiciled on the reservation, tribal jurisdiction was exclusive as to the States.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112241/mississippi-band-of-choctaw-indians-v-holyfield/
- **Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl**, 570 U.S. 637 (2013)
  Holding: ICWA § 1912(f) does not apply where the parent never had custody of the child; § 1912(d)'s active-efforts requirement applies only where terminating the parent's rights would precipitate the breakup of an Indian family; and § 1915(a)'s placement preferences do not bar a non-Indian adoption when no other eligible candidate has sought to adopt.
  Why here: The limiting case a Texas court will be handed in any contested ICWA termination: it narrows §§ 1912(d) and (f) where the Indian parent never had custody, and it is the necessary counterweight to Holyfield at this node.
  Quote: “Consistent with the statutory text, we hold that §1912(d) applies only in cases where an Indian family’s “breakup” would be precipitated by the termination of the parent’s rights.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2959741/adoptive-couple-v-baby-girl/

## OUT-03 · Guardianship / Kinship Permanency
- **In re Derzapf**, 219 S.W.3d 327 (Tex. 2007)
  Holding: A court must presume a fit parent acts in the child's best interest, and a grandparent seeking court-ordered access must prove that denial would significantly impair the child's physical health or emotional well-being.
  Why here: Sets the threshold a relative must clear before a court can grant access or permanency in derogation of a fit parent.
  Quote: “a trial court must presume that a fit parent acts in his or her child’s best interest”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/894839/in-re-derzapf/

  Holding: A fit parent is presumed to act in the child's best interest, and a court may not override that parent's decision about third-party access merely because it would decide differently.
  Why here: Guardianship or non-parent permanency cannot rest on a court's disagreement with a fit parent's judgment; Texas applies Troxel through In re Derzapf.
  Quote: “there is a presumption that fit parents act in the best interests of their children”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1087668/troxel-v-granville/

  Holding: Best interest is determined by a non-exclusive list of factors (the Holley factors), and an absence of evidence on those factors requires denial of termination.

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1512718/holley-v-adams/
- **Smith v. Organization of Foster Families for Equality & Reform**, 431 U.S. 816 (1977)
  Holding: Whatever liberty interest a foster family may have is substantially attenuated when the child is being returned to the natural parents, and the State's existing pre-removal procedures satisfied due process.
  Why here: Frames the outer limit of a caregiver's liberty interest when permanency is being fixed in a non-parent.
  Quote: “the procedures provided by New York State in § 392 and by New York City's SSC Procedure No. 5 are adequate”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/109684/smith-v-organization-of-foster-families-for-equality-reform/
- **In re C.J.C.**, No. 19-0694 (Tex. June 26, 2020)
  Holding: The constitutional presumption that a fit parent acts in the best interest of the child applies in any proceeding in which a nonparent seeks conservatorship, possession or access over the objection of a fit parent, including a Chapter 156 modification suit where no statutory presumption applies, and a parent does not waive it by having invoked the courts himself.
  Why here: Controls any kinship or third-party permanency order entered over a fit parent's objection, including one entered by modification after an earlier order; the nonparent must overcome the fit-parent presumption, not merely win a best-interest weighing.

- **In re Mays-Hooper**, 189 S.W.3d 777 (Tex. 2006)
  Holding: Where there was no evidence the mother was unfit, no evidence the child's health or well-being would suffer if the court deferred to her, and no evidence she meant to exclude the grandmother entirely, a court-ordered grandparent possession order was a clear abuse of discretion under Troxel, and mandamus was conditionally granted.
  Why here: Cited inside In re Derzapf and belongs beside it: a grandparent access or possession order entered over a fit parent's objection, without evidence of unfitness or harm, cannot stand.
  Quote: “We need not sort out all the opinions in Troxel; because the facts here are virtually the same, the judgment must be the same too.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1401714/in-re-mays-hooper/
- **Lewelling v. Lewelling**, 796 S.W.2d 164 (Tex. 1990)
  Holding: A non-parent seeking managing conservatorship over a parent must prove that appointing the parent would significantly impair the child's physical health or emotional development; a mother's unemployment, crowded housing, dependence on relatives and history as a victim of spousal abuse are no evidence of such impairment.
  Why here: Sets the evidentiary burden a relative must carry to take permanent managing conservatorship away from a parent, which is significant impairment, not a comparative best-interest showing.

  Holding: A CPS caseworker's visual body-cavity search of a child raised a Fourth Amendment claim, and in non-exigent circumstances the worker must obtain a warrant or court order rather than proceed on a parent's silence; a parent's consent to an investigative home interview is not consent to such a search, and consent to entry may be revoked.
