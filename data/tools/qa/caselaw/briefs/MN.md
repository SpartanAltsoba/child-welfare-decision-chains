# MN — 50 authorities, verified 2026-09-20

## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Becker v. Mayo Foundation**, 737 N.W.2d 200 (Minn. 2007)
  Holding: The court held that Minnesota's child-abuse reporting act creates no private civil cause of action against a mandatory reporter who fails to report suspected abuse, because the legislature chose to impose only criminal penalties.
  Why here: This is the controlling Minnesota Supreme Court statement of what a mandatory reporter owes and what happens when the duty is breached — criminal exposure, not civil liability.
  Quote: “The plain language of the statute indicates that the legislature chose to impose criminal, but not civil, penalties on mandatory reporters who fail to report.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1705179/becker-v-mayo-foundation/
- **Kuelbs v. Williams**, 609 N.W.2d 10 (Minn. Ct. App. 2000)
  Holding: The court held that Minnesota's Reporting of Maltreatment of Minors Act creates no express or implied civil cause of action for bad-faith reporting or investigation, and that the county defendants' statements were protected by a qualified privilege.
  Why here: It forecloses a civil suit against a mandated reporter even on an allegation that the report was made in bad faith.
  Quote: “We conclude that there is no express or implied civil cause of action under RMMA for bad faith reporting or investigating child maltreatment.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2090111/kuelbs-v-williams/
- **Fosselman v. Commissioner of Human Services**, 612 N.W.2d 456 (Minn. Ct. App. 2000)
  Holding: The court held that maltreatment-based disqualification proceedings against mandated reporters are subject to procedural due process and that making the commissioner's reconsideration the final agency action, with no evidentiary hearing, denied relators due process.
  Why here: Mandated reporters who are found to have failed to report face disqualification, and this case establishes that they are constitutionally entitled to process before that finding sticks.
  Quote: “We conclude that disqualification proceedings are subject to the requirements of procedural due process.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1862579/fosselman-v-commissioner-of-human-services/
- **Valtakis v. Putnam**, 504 N.W.2d 264 (Minn. Ct. App. 1993)
  Holding: Affirming summary judgment, the court held that the Child Abuse Reporting Act imposes no duty between a mandated reporter and the abused child that can support a negligence action, so failure to report gives rise to no civil cause of action.
  Why here: This is the original Minnesota holding that a mandated reporter's failure to report supports no private damages action — the first link in the Valtakis / Kuelbs / Becker line that Kuelbs expressly rests on.
  Quote: “Since CARA created the duty to report, a civil action cannot lie outside the express or clearly implied language of the statute.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2220887/valtakis-v-putnam/
- **State v. Grover**, 437 N.W.2d 60 (Minn. 1989)
  Holding: Reversing the dismissal of the prosecution, the court held that Minnesota's child-abuse reporting statute is neither unconstitutionally vague nor overbroad, and that it reaches a mandated reporter whose failure to report is merely negligent rather than knowing or reckless.
  Why here: It is the Minnesota Supreme Court decision sustaining criminal prosecution of a mandated reporter and construing the 'knows or has reason to believe' trigger, and it is the enforcement mechanism Becker later identified as the only one the legislature provided.
  Quote: “Holding that the statute is not unconstitutionally vague or overbroad, we reverse and remand to the district court for further proceedings.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1856529/state-v-grover/

## INP-02 · Voluntary / Third-Party Report
- **Kuelbs v. Williams**, 609 N.W.2d 10 (Minn. Ct. App. 2000)
  Holding: The court held that Minnesota's Reporting of Maltreatment of Minors Act creates no express or implied civil cause of action for bad-faith reporting or investigation, and that the county defendants' statements were protected by a qualified privilege.
  Why here: A voluntary reporter likewise cannot be sued civilly under the reporting act, which the court read as an immunity provision rather than a liability-creating one.
  Quote: “We conclude that there is no express or implied civil cause of action under RMMA for bad faith reporting or investigating child maltreatment.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2090111/kuelbs-v-williams/

  Why here: It sets the limit of a voluntary reporter's anonymity: confidentiality yields to a court order once the assessment is over.
  Quote: “Guetter is thus entitled to disclosure of the identity of the reporter upon court order, but the statute is silent as to when such an order should be granted.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7926260/guetter-v-brown-county-family-services/
- **Valtakis v. Putnam**, 504 N.W.2d 264 (Minn. Ct. App. 1993)

  Why here: Because the duty to report is purely statutory and no common-law duty preceded it, no reporter — mandated or voluntary — is civilly answerable under the reporting act.

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2220887/valtakis-v-putnam/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **K.D. v. County of Crow Wing**, 434 F.3d 1051 (8th Cir. 2006)
  Holding: The court held that emergency removal of a child without a court order is constitutionally permitted when continued parental custody poses an imminent threat to the child's health or welfare, and affirmed summary judgment for the Minnesota county and officers.
  Why here: The case arose from a law-enforcement encounter in Crow Wing County that led to protective custody of a child.
  Quote: “In cases in which continued parental custody poses an imminent threat to the child's health or welfare, emergency removal of children without a court order is constitutionally permitted.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/792986/kd-a-minor-through-his-mother-michelle-deason-michelle-deason/
- **Riehm v. Engelking**, 538 F.3d 952 (8th Cir. 2008)
  Holding: The court held that a Minnesota county social worker's 72-hour protective hold of a student under an ex parte order was objectively reasonable and that the order was not so lacking in probable cause as to make reliance on it unreasonable, granting qualified immunity.
  Why here: The hold followed a coordinated school, police and county response to a student's writings.
  Quote: “The ex parte order was not so lacking in indicia of probable cause as to render Diercks's reliance on it unreasonable.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1443178/riehm-v-engelking/
- **Heartland Academy Community Church v. Waddle**, 427 F.3d 525 (8th Cir. 2005)
  Holding: The court affirmed injunctive and declaratory relief, holding that the wholesale removal of boarding students on stale ex parte orders or with no order at all, absent exigent circumstances, violated the Fourth Amendment.
  Why here: It addresses a coordinated law-enforcement and child-welfare sweep and the constitutional limits on such joint action.
  Quote: “Waddle effected the wholesale removal of HCA's boarding students with ex parte orders or no orders at all in the absence of exigent circumstances”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/792293/heartland-academy-community-church-v-michael-waddle/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral

  Why here: The report at issue was prepared for the district court, and the case governs correction of court-directed social-services data.
  Quote: “there is substantial evidence in the record to support the Commissioner's decision that the contested report was inaccurate in concluding that the report of abuse by Johnston was substantiated”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1591931/hennepin-county-community-services-department-v-hale/
- **In re Welfare of Child of M.K.**, 805 N.W.2d 856 (Minn. Ct. App. 2011)
  Holding: The court held that parents were entitled to withdraw CHIPS admissions that the county obtained by threatening to withhold needed services, because accepting admissions extracted that way was a manifest injustice.
  Why here: It describes the juvenile court's role when a county routes a family into a CHIPS proceeding to obtain services, and the limits on what the court may accept.
  Quote: “we conclude that a threat to act in a manner that is not in a child's best interests constitutes a manifest injustice”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8280359/in-re-the-welfare-of-mk/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request

  Quote: “because such a transfer of custody is a statutorily approved permanency disposition, we conclude that a parent may voluntarily transfer custody to a relative for good cause”

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **In re Welfare of S.N.R.**, 617 N.W.2d 77 (Minn. Ct. App. 2000)
  Holding: The court held that a tribe's determination that a child is a member or eligible for membership conclusively establishes the child's status as an Indian child for purposes of ICWA's adoptive placement preferences, and affirmed summary judgment.
  Why here: This is the Minnesota rule for the threshold question that triggers ICWA at all: who decides whether the child is an Indian child.
  Quote: “a tribal determination that a child is a member or eligible for membership in that tribe is conclusive evidence that a child is an "Indian child" under section 1915”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2227562/in-re-the-welfare-of-snr/
- **In re Welfare of R.S.**, 805 N.W.2d 44 (Minn. 2011)
  Holding: The court held that neither ICWA nor Minnesota law authorizes transferring pre-adoptive and adoptive placement proceedings for an off-reservation Indian child to tribal court, and reversed the transfer.
  Why here: This is the Minnesota Supreme Court's controlling statement of how far ICWA's tribal-transfer right reaches for an off-reservation Indian child.
  Quote: “with respect to an Indian child not residing or domiciled on the child's tribe's reservation, transfer is limited to foster care placement and termination of parental rights proceedings”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8280343/in-re-the-welfare-of-rs/
- **In re Welfare of the Child of T.T.B.**, 724 N.W.2d 300 (Minn. 2006)
  Holding: The court held there was good cause under ICWA to deny transfer to tribal court because the transfer motions were not made promptly after notice and the proceedings had reached an advanced stage.
  Why here: It supplies Minnesota's good-cause standard for refusing an ICWA transfer, turning on promptness and the stage of the proceedings.
  Quote: “the district court had good cause to deny the motions to transfer jurisdiction of the case to the tribal court”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1754747/in-re-the-welfare-of-the-child-of-ttb/
- **In re Welfare of Children of S.R.K.**, 911 N.W.2d 821 (Minn. 2018)
  Holding: The court held that ICWA and MIFPA require qualified expert witness testimony as part of the court's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt determination that continued parental custody is likely to cause serious emotional or physical damage, and affirmed the terminations.
  Why here: It is the controlling Minnesota Supreme Court construction of ICWA and MIFPA's evidentiary requirements once a case is identified as involving an Indian child.
  Quote: “The statute provides that the court's serious-damage determination must be supported by evidence "including testimony of qualified expert witnesses."”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8283781/in-re/
- **In re Welfare of the Children of S.W.**, 727 N.W.2d 144 (Minn. Ct. App. 2007)
  Holding: The court affirmed termination of parental rights to Indian children, holding the district court did not err in finding that active and reasonable efforts to reunify had failed and in refusing to invalidate the proceedings for earlier ICWA violations.
  Why here: It applies the ICWA and Minnesota Tribal/State Agreement definition of active efforts once a case is identified as involving Indian children.
  Quote: “We conclude that the district court did not err by finding that active and reasonable efforts failed to reunite the family.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1735918/in-re-the-welfare-of-the-children-of-sw/

- Missing (for a later pass): Cracraft v. City of St. Louis Park, 279 N.W.2d 801 (Minn. 1979) — the Minnesota Supreme Court's special-duty framework for municipal tort liability. Radke is decided by applying Cracraft (and overruling Hoppe as irreconcilable with it), so a practitioner pleading a negligent-investigation claim under Radke cannot frame the duty element without it. Belongs at DEC-01, DEC-02 and INP-10.
- Missing (for a later pass): Andrade v. Ellefson, 391 N.W.2d 836 (Minn. 1986) — the Minnesota Supreme Court's decision allowing a negligence action against a county for its conduct in licensing and supervising day care where children were abused. It is the direct precursor Radke relies on, and it is the case that establishes county exposure independent of the reporting statute. Belongs at DEC-02, INP-10, FAIL-04 and PMC-02, which currently has no on-point authority at all.
- Missing (for a later pass): In re Welfare of H.G.B., 306 N.W.2d 821 (Minn. 1981) — the Minnesota Supreme Court's foundational statement that termination requires grave and weighty reasons, that statutory grounds are construed strictly, and that the evidence must address conditions existing at the time of the hearing. It is the ancestor of R.W., S.E.P. and T.R., and OUT-02 currently runs no further back than Santosky (1982) on the federal side and R.W. (2004) on the state side.
