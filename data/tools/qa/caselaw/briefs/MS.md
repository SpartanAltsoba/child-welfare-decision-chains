# MS — 45 authorities, verified 2026-09-20

## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **T.M. v. Noblitt**, 650 So. 2d 1340 (Miss. 1995)
  Holding: The Court reversed the dismissal of a failure-to-report claim against a school principal, holding that while she has qualified immunity for discretionary judgments, once reasonable cause is determined the Section 43-21-353 report is mandatory and non-discretionary.
  Why here: The leading Mississippi case on the mandatory reporter's duty under Section 43-21-353 and the immunity available to the reporter who exercises judgment.
  Quote: “if, after investigation, it was determined there was reasonable cause to support a complaint of suspected child abuse, a mandatory, non-discretionary report to the proper agency is called for”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1750885/tm-v-noblitt/
- **In the Interest of J.T. v. Hinds County Youth Court**, 188 So. 3d 1192 (Miss. 2016)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court's adjudication that a three-year-old had been sexually abused as unsupported by sufficient evidence, and confirmed that the Mississippi Rules of Evidence govern youth court adjudicatory hearings.
  Why here: A child's single statement, reported onward by professionals, is where the chain began, and the Court measured what that report could prove.
  Quote: “except where specifically superseded by a youth-court-specific rule, the Mississippi Rules of Evidence apply with full force and effect”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105448/in-the-interest-of-jt-v-hinds-county-youth-court/

## INP-02 · Voluntary / Third-Party Report

  Why here: A third-party report standing alone does not authorize state action against the child; corroboration is what converts a tip into grounds.
  Quote: “an anonymous tip regarding child abuse will rarely be sufficient to justify the seizure of a child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/62905/gates-v-texas-deparment-of-protective-regulatory-services/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **In the Interest of J.T. v. Hinds County Youth Court**, 188 So. 3d 1192 (Miss. 2016)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court's adjudication that a three-year-old had been sexually abused as unsupported by sufficient evidence, and confirmed that the Mississippi Rules of Evidence govern youth court adjudicatory hearings.
  Why here: The forensic interview at the children's advocacy center and the law-enforcement-linked investigation are assessed for reliability, not accepted on their face.
  Quote: “except where specifically superseded by a youth-court-specific rule, the Mississippi Rules of Evidence apply with full force and effect”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105448/in-the-interest-of-jt-v-hinds-county-youth-court/
- **Gates v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 537 F.3d 404 (5th Cir. 2008)
  Holding: Affirming summary judgment on qualified-immunity grounds, the court held prospectively that the Fourth Amendment governs child-abuse investigations, that the government may not seize a child absent a court order, parental consent or exigent circumstances, and that an uncorroborated anonymous tip will rarely justify seizing a child.
  Why here: Sets the Fifth Circuit rule for investigations conducted jointly by caseworkers and deputies, including entries and child interviews.
  Quote: “an anonymous tip regarding child abuse will rarely be sufficient to justify the seizure of a child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/62905/gates-v-texas-deparment-of-protective-regulatory-services/
- **Roe v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 299 F.3d 395 (5th Cir. 2002)
  Holding: The court held the caseworker's entry was covered by the mother's earlier consent to a home visit and reversed the denial of qualified immunity, while holding that affirmative verbal consent is required for an investigative home entry and that silence or passivity is not consent.
  Why here: Defines what a caseworker arriving with or after a law-enforcement referral may do at the door without a warrant.
  Quote: “Although they still must obtain the parent’s verbal, affirmative consent before conducting an investigative home interview, they need not obtain that consent again when they appear at the parent’s doorstep.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7106786/roe-v-texas-department-of-protective-regulatory-services/

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **In the Interest of N.W.**, 978 So. 2d 649 (Miss. 2008)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court's abuse adjudication and custody order because the father was never served or notified of the hearings and the court did not comply with the Youth Court Act's notice and advice-of-rights requirements.
  Why here: Illustrates how an emergency order and court-driven hearings open a youth court file and what the court must do at the first appearance.
  Quote: “The Hinds County Youth Court failed to comply with the statutory requirements to assure notice to the father for the various hearings affecting the custody of his minor son”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7849191/in-the-interest-of-nw/
- **In the Interest of I.G.**, 467 So. 2d 920 (Miss. 1985)
  Holding: The Court reversed an educational-neglect adjudication, the ensuing neglect adjudication and a contempt citation and returned the child to her parents, holding that the Youth Court Act imposes a mandatory duty on the judge to advise the parties of their right to counsel and to explain the purpose of each adjudicatory hearing, and that noncompliance is reversible error.
  Why here: Shows a compulsory-attendance referral converting into a youth court neglect case, and what the court owes the parents at that first appearance.
  Quote: “The youth court did not inform I.G.'s mother and step-father of their right to counsel, as mandated by our legislature, and for this reason the cause must be reversed.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1686118/in-interest-of-ig/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **In re Custody of M.A.G.**, 859 So. 2d 1001 (Miss. 2003)
  Holding: The Court affirmed an award of custody to third-party custodians over the natural father, holding that the presumption favoring a natural parent is overcome by a clear showing of unfitness.
  Why here: Shows the route by which private caretakers, not the agency, bring a child's situation before a court and ask for authority over the child.
  Quote: “it has been stated many times that this presumption can be overcome by a clear showing that the parent is unfit”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1696726/in-re-custody-of-mag/
- **Davis v. Vaughn**, 126 So. 3d 33 (Miss. 2013)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the award of custody to the natural father over the maternal grandmother and declined to change the established grounds for rebutting the natural-parent presumption.
  Why here: A relative caregiver's petition is measured against the parent's presumptive right, which shapes what a guardian's request for help can achieve.
  Quote: “we find no legally compelling reason to alter or abandon our established standards for rebuttal of the natural-parent presumption”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105232/davis-v-vaughn/
- **In the Matter of the Dissolution of the Marriage of Leverock and Hamby: Pendleton v. Leverock**, 23 So. 3d 424 (Miss. 2009)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the chancellor's refusal to terminate the father's parental rights but reversed the award of custody to him, holding the record established desertion so that the natural-parent presumption was rebutted and custody had to be redetermined through an on-the-record Albright best-interests analysis.
  Why here: The third-party caregivers who had raised the child since infancy petitioned in their own right, the private route by which a caregiver's request for authority over a child reaches a court.
  Quote: “This presumption in favor of a natural parent has long been recognized, and Tony is entitled to it unless there is proof that he has relinquished it.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1614077/in-re-marriage-of-leverock-hamby/

## INP-07 · Medical Disclosure
- **Lauderdale County Department of Human Services v. T.H.G.**, 614 So. 2d 377 (Miss. 1993)
  Holding: The Court declined to create a child-welfare exception to the Rule 503 physician/psychotherapist-patient privilege in a termination proceeding based on the parents' mental illness, and remanded for a final determination on the petition.
  Why here: Controls whether a parent's medical and psychiatric records obtained in treatment can be used by the agency: in Mississippi they remain privileged even when a child's welfare is at stake.
  Quote: “we cannot obliterate the rights of the parents and thus decline to create an exception to Rule 503”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1092021/lauderdale-county-dhs-v-thg/
- **In the Interest of J.T. v. Hinds County Youth Court**, 188 So. 3d 1192 (Miss. 2016)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court's adjudication that a three-year-old had been sexually abused as unsupported by sufficient evidence, and confirmed that the Mississippi Rules of Evidence govern youth court adjudicatory hearings.
  Why here: The children's justice center medical examination report was admitted over hearsay objection, and the Court holds such reports must satisfy the Rules of Evidence.
  Quote: “except where specifically superseded by a youth-court-specific rule, the Mississippi Rules of Evidence apply with full force and effect”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105448/in-the-interest-of-jt-v-hinds-county-youth-court/
- **Roe v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 299 F.3d 395 (5th Cir. 2002)
  Holding: The court held the caseworker's entry was covered by the mother's earlier consent to a home visit and reversed the denial of qualified immunity, while holding that affirmative verbal consent is required for an investigative home entry and that silence or passivity is not consent.
  Why here: Holds that a visual body-cavity search or examination of a child in non-exigent circumstances requires a warrant or valid consent.
  Quote: “Although they still must obtain the parent’s verbal, affirmative consent before conducting an investigative home interview, they need not obtain that consent again when they appear at the parent’s doorstep.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7106786/roe-v-texas-department-of-protective-regulatory-services/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Mississippi Department of Human Services v. D.C.**, No. 2018-IA-00592-SCT (Miss. Aug. 15, 2019)
  Holding: The Court held that Section 43-15-125 immunizes the Department and its officers from claims arising out of its licensing and licensing-related investigation of a foster home, but affirmed the denial of summary judgment on the foster child's remaining Tort Claims Act allegations about his care.
  Why here: Shows how far civil litigation can reach into agency practice, and where statutory immunity blocks scrutiny of licensing decisions.
  Quote: “Section 43-15-125 grants immunity to DHS for its licensing of foster homes.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4648743/mississippi-department-of-human-services-v-dc/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' requirement of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act creates no federally enforceable right and cannot be enforced by private plaintiffs under Section 1983 or an implied right of action.
  Why here: Because private enforcement is unavailable, federal review and the Secretary's plan-approval process are the operative oversight mechanisms.
  Quote: “§ 671(a)(15) does not create a federally enforceable right to “reasonable efforts” under §1983”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **Olivia Y. ex rel. Johnson v. Barbour**, 351 F. Supp. 2d 543 (S.D. Miss. 2004)
  Holding: In the class action challenging Mississippi's child welfare system the court granted in part and denied in part the State officials' motion to dismiss, dismissing the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act claims as conferring no privately enforceable rights, dismissing the equal-protection claims and the protective-services class's due-process claims, but allowing the in-custody children's substantive-due-process claim to constitutionally adequate care to proceed.
  Why here: The systemic challenge to Mississippi's child welfare system, brought for children in and at risk of entering state custody, is the vehicle through which agency-wide caseload, placement and investigation practice is examined in court.
  Quote: “For the reasons given, the court concludes that defendants' motion to dismiss plaintiffs' claims under the AACWA should be granted.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2407808/olivia-y-ex-rel-johnson-v-barbour/

## INP-10 · Agency Escalation (Screening → Investigation)
- **E.K. v. Mississippi Department of Child Protection Services**, 249 So. 3d 377 (Miss. 2018)
  Holding: The Court vacated a youth court's neglect adjudication and rendered judgment for the child, holding the parents' notice and right-to-counsel protections were violated, the petition gave no notice of the circumstances alleged, and a parent's marijuana use without a causal connection to harm is legally insufficient proof of neglect.
  Why here: Escalation from screening to formal adjudication must be matched by proof of actual harm, not by the fact of a positive test.
  Quote: “Elizabeth’s marijuana usage does not negate the requirement of a causal connection between the drug usage and the neglect.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4523332/ek-v-mississippi-department-of-child-protection-services/
- **In the Interest of N.W.**, 978 So. 2d 649 (Miss. 2008)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court's abuse adjudication and custody order because the father was never served or notified of the hearings and the court did not comply with the Youth Court Act's notice and advice-of-rights requirements.
  Why here: When a screening becomes a formal youth court proceeding, the statutory notice and advice-of-rights steps attach immediately.
  Quote: “The Hinds County Youth Court failed to comply with the statutory requirements to assure notice to the father for the various hearings affecting the custody of his minor son”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7849191/in-the-interest-of-nw/
- **In the Interest of I.G.**, 467 So. 2d 920 (Miss. 1985)
  Holding: The Court reversed an educational-neglect adjudication, the ensuing neglect adjudication and a contempt citation and returned the child to her parents, holding that the Youth Court Act imposes a mandatory duty on the judge to advise the parties of their right to counsel and to explain the purpose of each adjudicatory hearing, and that noncompliance is reversible error.
  Why here: Escalation from an educational-neglect petition into a general neglect adjudication must be announced to the parents under Section 43-21-557(1)(d); silent escalation is reversible error.
  Quote: “The youth court did not inform I.G.'s mother and step-father of their right to counsel, as mandated by our legislature, and for this reason the cause must be reversed.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1686118/in-interest-of-ig/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield**, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held that ICWA domicile is defined by federal law, that twins born off the reservation took the reservation domicile of their parents, and that the Choctaw tribal court therefore had exclusive jurisdiction, vacating the Mississippi adoption decree.
  Why here: The controlling authority for Mississippi: a Choctaw child domiciled on the reservation cannot be adopted through state court, however the parents arrange the birth.
  Quote: “In enacting the ICWA Congress confirmed that, in child custody proceedings involving Indian children domiciled on the reservation, tribal jurisdiction was exclusive as to the States.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112241/mississippi-band-of-choctaw-indians-v-holyfield/
- **Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl**, 570 U.S. 637 (2013)
  Holding: The Court held that ICWA Section 1912(d)'s remedial-efforts requirement applies only where termination would precipitate the breakup of an Indian family, and that Section 1912(f) and the Section 1915(a) placement preferences did not bar this adoption by a non-Indian couple.
  Why here: Marks the outer limit of ICWA's active-efforts and placement protections when the Indian parent never had custody of the child.
  Quote: “Consistent with the statutory text, we hold that §1912(d) applies only in cases where an Indian family’s “breakup” would be precipitated by the termination of the parent’s rights.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/931616/adoptive-couple-v-baby-girl/

## INP-12 · Emergency / Protective Custody
- **E.K. v. Mississippi Department of Child Protection Services**, 249 So. 3d 377 (Miss. 2018)
  Holding: The Court vacated a youth court's neglect adjudication and rendered judgment for the child, holding the parents' notice and right-to-counsel protections were violated, the petition gave no notice of the circumstances alleged, and a parent's marijuana use without a causal connection to harm is legally insufficient proof of neglect.
  Why here: Cites Section 43-21-301(3), which bars a probable-cause finding for a custody order based solely on a parent's positive marijuana test.
  Quote: “Elizabeth’s marijuana usage does not negate the requirement of a causal connection between the drug usage and the neglect.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4523332/ek-v-mississippi-department-of-child-protection-services/
- **Gates v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 537 F.3d 404 (5th Cir. 2008)
  Holding: Affirming summary judgment on qualified-immunity grounds, the court held prospectively that the Fourth Amendment governs child-abuse investigations, that the government may not seize a child absent a court order, parental consent or exigent circumstances, and that an uncorroborated anonymous tip will rarely justify seizing a child.
  Why here: Protective custody without a court order requires consent or exigency, defined as reasonable cause to believe the child faces imminent physical or sexual abuse at home.
  Quote: “an anonymous tip regarding child abuse will rarely be sufficient to justify the seizure of a child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/62905/gates-v-texas-deparment-of-protective-regulatory-services/
- **Wernecke v. Garcia**, 591 F.3d 386 (5th Cir. 2009)
  Holding: The court affirmed the denial of qualified immunity to the caseworker who removed two boys not named in the court order and with no evidence of imminent danger, holding that requirement was clearly established before Gates, while reversing as to the search and the supervisor.
  Why here: Confirms that emergency custody of a child not covered by a court order demands independent evidence of imminent danger.
  Quote: “Gates was not required to give social workers fair notice that imminent danger must exist before a child may be removed without a warrant or court order”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1463447/wernecke-v-garcia/
- **Christina Romero v. Amanda Brown**, 937 F.3d 514 (5th Cir. 2019)
  Holding: The court remanded the procedural-due-process claim against the caseworker while rejecting the claims against the officers, holding it is clearly established that a court order or exigency is the predeprivation process due before a child is removed.
  Why here: States the process due before protective custody attaches, in the circuit that governs Mississippi.
  Quote: “It is thus clearly established that a court order or exigency is the predeprivation process that is due when social workers remove a child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4657322/christina-romero-v-amanda-brown/
- **Banks v. Herbrich**, 90 F.4th 407 (5th Cir. 2024)
  Holding: The court affirmed in part the denial of qualified immunity, holding that an unsuitable living situation, without evidence of imminent danger, is not exigency justifying a warrantless removal.
  Why here: The most recent circuit authority separating poor conditions from the imminent danger that authorizes protective custody.
  Quote: “Unsuitable as R.B.’s living situation may have been, it did not constitute exigent circumstances necessitating warrantless removal from his mother.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9457186/banks-v-herbrich/

## DEC-03 · Substantiated / Founded Finding

  Why here: The central Mississippi authority on what a substantiated neglect finding requires - a causal link between parental conduct and harm to the child.
  Quote: “Elizabeth’s marijuana usage does not negate the requirement of a causal connection between the drug usage and the neglect.”

## OUT-02 · Termination of Parental Rights / Adoption
- **Vance v. Lincoln County Department of Public Welfare**, 582 So. 2d 414 (Miss. 1991)
  Holding: The Court affirmed termination of an incarcerated mother's parental rights for substantial erosion of the parent-child relationship, holding that the petitioner must first carry the clear-and-convincing burden on a statutory ground and only then may the child's best interest be weighed.
  Why here: Supplies Mississippi's two-step termination sequence - statutory ground proved by clear and convincing evidence, then best interest - which controls every TPR petition in the state.
  Quote: “Once the clear and convincing burden has been met, the best interest of the child is to be considered.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1790245/vance-v-lincoln-county-dpw/
- **W.A.S. v. A.L.G.**, 949 So. 2d 31 (Miss. 2007)
  Holding: The Court affirmed a chancery court judgment terminating a father's parental rights in an adoption proceeding, applying the two-prong test of a proven statutory ground followed by the child's best interest.
  Why here: States the two-prong structure Mississippi chancellors apply when termination is sought inside a private adoption case.
  Quote: “Once the first prong has been satisfied, the court must consider the second prong: the best interest of the child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7843902/was-v-alg/
- **Blakeney v. McRee**, 188 So. 3d 1154 (Miss. 2016)
  Holding: The Court affirmed a chancery judgment terminating a father's parental rights and granting the adoption petition, holding the chancellor's decision was supported by substantial credible evidence and was not manifestly wrong.
  Why here: Shows the combined termination-and-adoption judgment that produces permanency in Mississippi chancery practice, and the deference it receives on review.
  Quote: “we find that the chancellor’s decision to terminate John’s parental rights was supported by substantial credible evidence and was not manifestly wrong”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105447/blakeney-v-mcree/
- **Santosky v. Kramer**, 455 U.S. 745 (1982)
  Holding: The Court held that the Due Process Clause requires a State to support its allegations by at least clear and convincing evidence before terminating parental rights.
  Why here: The federal constitutional floor beneath Mississippi's clear-and-convincing termination standard, cited by the Mississippi Supreme Court in every leading TPR case.
  Quote: “due process requires that the State support its allegations by at least clear and convincing evidence”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/110676/santosky-v-kramer/
- **Petit v. Holifield**, 443 So. 2d 874 (Miss. 1984)
  Holding: The Court reversed and set aside a decree allowing a stepfather to adopt over the natural father's objection, holding that abandonment, desertion or unfitness must be proved by clear and convincing evidence before the child's best interest may be considered, and that the Holifields had not carried that burden.
  Why here: The origin of Mississippi's two-prong rule for severing parental rights - a statutory ground proved by clear and convincing evidence, and only then the child's best interest - which Vance, W.A.S. and S.R.B.R. each restate by citing this case.
  Quote: “Once that has been established, the best interest of the child is to be considered.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1889050/petit-v-holifield/
- **G.Q.A. v. Harrison County Department of Human Services**, 771 So. 2d 331 (Miss. 2000)
  Holding: The Court affirmed a family court judgment terminating both parents' rights to a severely burned and malnourished child, holding that family court terminations are reviewed under the same clear-and-convincing standard as chancery terminations and that the findings of serial abuse and substantial erosion of the relationship were supported by substantial evidence.
  Why here: The leading Mississippi statement of the termination standard of review, uniform across chancery and family courts, and the authority later cases cite for it.
  Quote: “A Family Court judgment concerning termination of parental rights will be reviewed under the same standard as a Chancery Court judgment, which is clear and convincing evidence.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1744006/gqa-v-harrison-county-dept-of-human-services/

## OUT-03 · Guardianship / Kinship Permanency
- **S.R.B.R. v. Harrison County Department of Human Services**, 798 So. 2d 437 (Miss. 2001)
  Holding: The Court affirmed a family court's termination of parental rights, holding that the petitioner must prove at least one of the grounds enumerated in Section 93-15-103(3) by clear and convincing evidence.
  Why here: The guardian ad litem's weighing of durable legal custody against adoption shows how Mississippi courts choose between guardianship-style permanency and termination.
  Quote: “the petitioner must prove by clear and convincing evidence at least one of the grounds enumerated”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1655438/srbr-v-harrison-county-dhs/
- **In re Custody of M.A.G.**, 859 So. 2d 1001 (Miss. 2003)
  Holding: The Court affirmed an award of custody to third-party custodians over the natural father, holding that the presumption favoring a natural parent is overcome by a clear showing of unfitness.
  Why here: States the unfitness showing required before a third-party custody or guardianship arrangement can become the child's permanent placement.
  Quote: “it has been stated many times that this presumption can be overcome by a clear showing that the parent is unfit”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1696726/in-re-custody-of-mag/
- **Guardianship of B.A.D. v. Finnegan**, 82 So. 3d 608 (Miss. 2012)
  Holding: The Court held the chancery court properly had subject-matter jurisdiction over a guardianship petition after the youth court voluntarily terminated its own jurisdiction under Section 43-21-151(2).
  Why here: Guardianship as a permanency outcome in Mississippi runs through chancery court, which can only act once youth court jurisdiction ends.
  Quote: “The plain language of the Act does allow the youth court to terminate its jurisdiction of a case.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2552144/guardianship-of-bad-v-finnegan/
- **Davis v. Vaughn**, 126 So. 3d 33 (Miss. 2013)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the award of custody to the natural father over the maternal grandmother and declined to change the established grounds for rebutting the natural-parent presumption.
  Why here: Sets the boundary between kinship permanency and the constitutionally protected position of the natural parent.
  Quote: “we find no legally compelling reason to alter or abandon our established standards for rebuttal of the natural-parent presumption”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105232/davis-v-vaughn/
- **In the Interest of S.A.M.**, 826 So. 2d 1266 (Miss. 2002)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the youth court's grant of durable legal custody of a chronically abused child to her long-time foster parents, but reversed the portion of the judgment permanently relieving the Department of Human Services of any further involvement, holding that durable legal custody leaves the parents' residual rights intact and ends only the mandatory annual dispositional reviews, not the youth court's jurisdiction or the Department's ability to monitor.
  Why here: The Supreme Court's construction of durable legal custody under Sections 43-21-609 and 43-21-613, Mississippi's guardianship-style permanency device, which the youth court itself described as similar to a chancery guardianship.
  Quote: “The fact that under durable legal custody the parent retains some form of residual rights and responsibilities is a vital and obvious distinction to termination of parental rights.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1630784/in-re-sam/
- **In the Matter of the Dissolution of the Marriage of Leverock and Hamby: Pendleton v. Leverock**, 23 So. 3d 424 (Miss. 2009)
  Holding: The Court affirmed the chancellor's refusal to terminate the father's parental rights but reversed the award of custody to him, holding the record established desertion so that the natural-parent presumption was rebutted and custody had to be redetermined through an on-the-record Albright best-interests analysis.
  Why here: Third-party permanency becomes available only once the natural-parent presumption is rebutted, and even then custody turns on an on-the-record best-interests analysis rather than on the caregiver's status.
  Quote: “This presumption in favor of a natural parent has long been recognized, and Tony is entitled to it unless there is proof that he has relinquished it.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1614077/in-re-marriage-of-leverock-hamby/

## OUT-04 · Extended Foster Care / Aging Out

  Holding: The Court held the chancery court properly had subject-matter jurisdiction over a guardianship petition after the youth court voluntarily terminated its own jurisdiction under Section 43-21-151(2).
  Why here: Section 43-21-151(2), the provision fixing the outer bound of youth court jurisdiction over a child, is construed here.
  Quote: “The plain language of the Act does allow the youth court to terminate its jurisdiction of a case.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2552144/guardianship-of-bad-v-finnegan/
- **In the Interest of S.A.M.**, 826 So. 2d 1266 (Miss. 2002)

  Why here: Holds that a durable legal custody disposition does not oust the youth court of continuing jurisdiction over the child or foreclose further dispositional review hearings as needed.

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1630784/in-re-sam/

## OUT-05 · Case Dismissal
- **In re V.M.S.**, 938 So. 2d 829 (Miss. 2006)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court order that had terminated a mother's rights on the agency's Rule 60(b) motion, holding the evidence relied upon was not newly discovered and did not merit termination, and remanded on visitation.
  Why here: Where the agency's proof does not meet the statutory grounds, the correct outcome is that the petition fails and the earlier refusal to terminate stands.
  Quote: “We review the termination of parental rights to a minor child applying a “clear and convincing evidence” standard, thus applying a “clearly erroneous/manifest error” standard of review.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7841766/in-re-vms/
- **A.B. v. Lauderdale County Department of Human Services**, 13 So. 3d 1263 (Miss. 2009)
  Holding: The Court reversed a termination of parental rights, holding the department's circumstantial proof of parental drug use and neglect did not amount to clear and convincing evidence.
  Why here: Where proof fails, the case must end in the parent's favor rather than in permanency planning away from the family.
  Quote: “The party seeking termination of parental rights must prove grounds for termination by “clear and convincing evidence.””
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1620468/ab-v-lauderdale-county-department-of-human-services/
- **In the Interest of J.T. v. Hinds County Youth Court**, 188 So. 3d 1192 (Miss. 2016)
  Holding: The Court reversed a youth court's adjudication that a three-year-old had been sexually abused as unsupported by sufficient evidence, and confirmed that the Mississippi Rules of Evidence govern youth court adjudicatory hearings.
  Why here: Insufficient evidence at adjudication requires reversal, not a lesser disposition.
  Quote: “except where specifically superseded by a youth-court-specific rule, the Mississippi Rules of Evidence apply with full force and effect”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5105448/in-the-interest-of-jt-v-hinds-county-youth-court/
- **E.K. v. Mississippi Department of Child Protection Services**, 249 So. 3d 377 (Miss. 2018)
  Holding: The Court vacated a youth court's neglect adjudication and rendered judgment for the child, holding the parents' notice and right-to-counsel protections were violated, the petition gave no notice of the circumstances alleged, and a parent's marijuana use without a causal connection to harm is legally insufficient proof of neglect.
  Why here: The Court rendered judgment for the child rather than remanding, the cleanest form of dismissal after a failed adjudication.
  Quote: “Elizabeth’s marijuana usage does not negate the requirement of a causal connection between the drug usage and the neglect.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4523332/ek-v-mississippi-department-of-child-protection-services/
- **De La Oliva v. Lowndes County Department of Public Welfare**, 423 So. 2d 1328 (Miss. 1982)
  Holding: The Court reversed and rendered a judgment terminating a mother's parental rights and directed that custody of the child be restored to her, holding that the preponderance standard then prescribed by Section 93-15-109 was unconstitutional under Santosky v. Kramer and that the proof was in any event insufficient to establish abandonment or deep-seated antipathy by clear and convincing evidence.
  Why here: Where the department's proof fails, the correct disposition is rendition for the parent and immediate restoration of custody, not a remand giving the agency another attempt.
  Quote: “The chancellor, in making her findings in the case sub judice, clearly relied on an unconstitutional and impermissible standard of proof.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1150445/de-la-oliva-v-lowndes-cty-dept-of-pub-wel/

## OUT-06 · Child Fatality / Serious Injury
- **Mississippi Department of Human Services v. D.C.**, No. 2018-IA-00592-SCT (Miss. Aug. 15, 2019)
  Holding: The Court held that Section 43-15-125 immunizes the Department and its officers from claims arising out of its licensing and licensing-related investigation of a foster home, but affirmed the denial of summary judgment on the foster child's remaining Tort Claims Act allegations about his care.
  Why here: The governing Mississippi authority on agency liability when a child is seriously harmed while in foster care.
  Quote: “Section 43-15-125 grants immunity to DHS for its licensing of foster homes.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4648743/mississippi-department-of-human-services-v-dc/
- **T.M. v. Noblitt**, 650 So. 2d 1340 (Miss. 1995)
  Holding: The Court reversed the dismissal of a failure-to-report claim against a school principal, holding that while she has qualified immunity for discretionary judgments, once reasonable cause is determined the Section 43-21-353 report is mandatory and non-discretionary.
  Why here: A failure-to-report claim is the principal private remedy in Mississippi when a child is harmed after professionals stayed silent.
  Quote: “if, after investigation, it was determined there was reasonable cause to support a complaint of suspected child abuse, a mandatory, non-discretionary report to the proper agency is called for”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1750885/tm-v-noblitt/
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services**, 489 U.S. 189 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held that the Due Process Clause imposes no affirmative constitutional duty on a State to protect a child from private violence, even where the agency knew of the danger and failed to act.
  Why here: Defines what a family can and cannot recover under federal law after a child dies in circumstances the agency was monitoring.
  Quote: “we conclude that a State’s failure to protect an individual against private violence simply does not constitute a violation of the Due Process Clause”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112202/deshaney-v-winnebago-county-department-of-social-services/
- **Olivia Y. ex rel. Johnson v. Barbour**, 351 F. Supp. 2d 543 (S.D. Miss. 2004)
  Holding: In the class action challenging Mississippi's child welfare system the court granted in part and denied in part the State officials' motion to dismiss, dismissing the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act claims as conferring no privately enforceable rights, dismissing the equal-protection claims and the protective-services class's due-process claims, but allowing the in-custody children's substantive-due-process claim to constitutionally adequate care to proceed.
  Why here: Children harmed while in the Division of Family and Children's Services' custody retain a substantive-due-process claim to constitutionally adequate care arising from the State's special relationship with them.
  Quote: “For the reasons given, the court concludes that defendants' motion to dismiss plaintiffs' claims under the AACWA should be granted.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2407808/olivia-y-ex-rel-johnson-v-barbour/

  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10877216/in-the-interest-of-arh-a-minor-redonn-malone-v-jackson-county/

  Holding: The Court reversed an educational-neglect adjudication, the ensuing neglect adjudication and a contempt citation and returned the child to her parents, holding that the Youth Court Act imposes a mandatory duty on the judge to advise the parties of their right to counsel and to explain the purpose of each adjudicatory hearing, and that noncompliance is reversible error.
  Why here: Establishes that noncompliance with the Youth Court Act's notice requirements is itself reversible error, independent of the sufficiency of the evidence.
  Quote: “The youth court did not inform I.G.'s mother and step-father of their right to counsel, as mandated by our legislature, and for this reason the cause must be reversed.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1686118/in-interest-of-ig/

Kramer and that the proof was in any event insufficient to establish abandonment or deep-seated antipathy by clear and convincing evidence.
  Why here: The paradigm Mississippi reversal, cited in Vance and in Petit: a termination decreed on the wrong standard of proof is manifestly wrong and is set aside outright.
  Quote: “The chancellor, in making her findings in the case sub judice, clearly relied on an unconstitutional and impermissible standard of proof.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1150445/de-la-oliva-v-lowndes-cty-dept-of-pub-wel/

## FAIL-02 · Unlawful Removal
- **Gates v. Texas Department of Protective & Regulatory Services**, 537 F.3d 404 (5th Cir. 2008)
  Holding: Affirming summary judgment on qualified-immunity grounds, the court held prospectively that the Fourth Amendment governs child-abuse investigations, that the government may not seize a child absent a court order, parental consent or exigent circumstances, and that an uncorroborated anonymous tip will rarely justify seizing a child.
  Why here: The case Mississippi Section 1983 plaintiffs must satisfy: no court order, no consent and no exigency means an unconstitutional seizure.
  Quote: “an anonymous tip regarding child abuse will rarely be sufficient to justify the seizure of a child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/62905/gates-v-texas-deparment-of-protective-regulatory-services/
- **Wernecke v. Garcia**, 591 F.3d 386 (5th Cir. 2009)
  Holding: The court affirmed the denial of qualified immunity to the caseworker who removed two boys not named in the court order and with no evidence of imminent danger, holding that requirement was clearly established before Gates, while reversing as to the search and the supervisor.
  Why here: Denial of qualified immunity here is the clearest Fifth Circuit example of a removal that crossed the constitutional line.
  Quote: “Gates was not required to give social workers fair notice that imminent danger must exist before a child may be removed without a warrant or court order”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1463447/wernecke-v-garcia/
- **Morris v. Dearborne**, 181 F.3d 657 (5th Cir. 1999)
  Holding: The court held that a teacher who fabricated evidence of sexual abuse, causing a three-year removal and state-initiated termination proceedings, violated the clearly established right to family integrity and was not entitled to qualified immunity.
  Why here: Supplies the substantive-due-process theory, distinct from the Fourth Amendment, for a prolonged wrongful separation.
  Quote: “We therefore hold that Plaintiffs' claims fall squarely within the well established constitutional right to family integrity and to be free of arbitrary, oppressive governmental action.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/18053/morris-v-dearborne/
- **Christina Romero v. Amanda Brown**, 937 F.3d 514 (5th Cir. 2019)
  Holding: The court remanded the procedural-due-process claim against the caseworker while rejecting the claims against the officers, holding it is clearly established that a court order or exigency is the predeprivation process due before a child is removed.
  Why here: Current Fifth Circuit framing of the wrongful-removal claim as procedural due process rather than only Fourth Amendment.
  Quote: “It is thus clearly established that a court order or exigency is the predeprivation process that is due when social workers remove a child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4657322/christina-romero-v-amanda-brown/
- **Banks v. Herbrich**, 90 F.4th 407 (5th Cir. 2024)
  Holding: The court affirmed in part the denial of qualified immunity, holding that an unsuitable living situation, without evidence of imminent danger, is not exigency justifying a warrantless removal.
  Why here: Confirms in 2024 that the Gates standard remains clearly established law for wrongful-removal suits in Mississippi's circuit.
  Quote: “Unsuitable as R.B.’s living situation may have been, it did not constitute exigent circumstances necessitating warrantless removal from his mother.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9457186/banks-v-herbrich/

## FAIL-03 · Federal Compliance Failure
- **In the Interest of A.R.H.**, No. 2023-CT-00420-SCT (Miss. June 18, 2026)
  Holding: Reviewing the statute de novo, the Court reversed a youth court's bypass of reunification, holding that Section 43-21-603(7)(c) requires both one of the three enumerated criteria and a best-interest finding, and that a father's pre-birth criminal history was not conduct to which he subjected the child.
  Why here: Section 43-21-603(7)(c) implements the federal reasonable-efforts condition on Title IV-E funding, so its misapplication is a compliance question as well as an error.
  Quote: “after determining that one of the first three criteria have been met, the youth court must also determine that bypassing reunification is in the best interests of the child”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10877216/in-the-interest-of-arh-a-minor-redonn-malone-v-jackson-county/

  Holding: The Court held that the Due Process Clause imposes no affirmative constitutional duty on a State to protect a child from private violence, even where the agency knew of the danger and failed to act.
  Why here: Failures to meet federal performance expectations are not, by themselves, constitutional violations remediable by the affected family.
  Quote: “we conclude that a State’s failure to protect an individual against private violence simply does not constitute a violation of the Due Process Clause”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112202/deshaney-v-winnebago-county-department-of-social-services/
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' requirement of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act creates no federally enforceable right and cannot be enforced by private plaintiffs under Section 1983 or an implied right of action.
  Why here: The decisive federal authority: a state's failure to make reasonable efforts is remediable administratively, not by suit from the affected families.
  Quote: “§ 671(a)(15) does not create a federally enforceable right to “reasonable efforts” under §1983”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **Olivia Y. ex rel. Johnson v. Barbour**, 351 F. Supp. 2d 543 (S.D. Miss. 2004)

  Why here: Holds that the AACWA's case-plan, case-review, placement and licensing provisions confer no rights enforceable under Section 1983, so Mississippi's federal compliance failures are not litigable by the children they affect.
  Quote: “For the reasons given, the court concludes that defendants' motion to dismiss plaintiffs' claims under the AACWA should be granted.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2407808/olivia-y-ex-rel-johnson-v-barbour/

## FAIL-05 · Discrimination / Retaliation
- **Palmore v. Sidoti**, 466 U.S. 429 (1984)
  Holding: The Court held that removing a child from a parent's custody because of the racial composition of the parent's household violates the Equal Protection Clause.
  Why here: The governing federal rule when a placement or custody decision is driven by race or by anticipated community prejudice.
  Quote: “Private biases may be outside the reach of the law, but the law cannot, directly or indirectly, give them effect.”

- Two doctrinal points worth flagging for anyone using this brief in a Mississippi youth court matter. First, the burden of proof differs by stage: In the Interest of I.G. confirms that a neglect adjudication under Section 43-21-561(3) is decided on a preponderance, while Petit, De La Oliva, Santosky and G.Q.A. require clear and convincing evidence to terminate. Second, In the Interest of S.A.M. holds that durable legal custody ends only the mandatory annual dispositional reviews - the youth court keeps jurisdiction and the Department keeps the ability to monitor - so a disposition order purporting to relieve the agency 'finally' is error.
- Missing (for a later pass): Natural Father v. United Methodist Children's Home, 418 So. 2d 807 (Miss. 1982) (cluster 1701169 on CourtListener) — the decision that actually applied Santosky to Mississippi and held the preponderance standard of Section 93-15-109 unconstitutional in a termination proceeding. De La Oliva quotes it at 811 and rests on it; it is the true origin of the clear-and-convincing rule in this state and belongs at OUT-02 and DEC-03 ahead of, or alongside, De La Oliva.
- Missing (for a later pass): In the Interest of M.R.L., 488 So. 2d 788, 790-91 (Miss. 1986) — the actual source of the limited, non-de-novo youth court standard of review that the brief attributes to In re S.B. S.B. block-quotes M.R.L. verbatim. If the brief wants that standard at OUT-05 it must cite M.R.L. (and In Interest of K.A.R., 441 So. 2d 108, 110 (Miss. 1983), which M.R.L. itself cites), with the burden of proof flagged, rather than quoting a delinquency case's quotation of it.
- Missing (for a later pass): Albright v. Franklin, 437 So. 2d 1003 (Miss. 1983) — the source of the Albright best-interests factors. Leverock reverses and remands expressly 'for an Albright analysis' and the brief quotes that holding, yet the underlying authority is absent. Needed at OUT-03 and ACT-04 wherever a third-party or kinship custody determination turns on best interests.
- Missing (for a later pass): Chism v. Bright, 152 So. 3d 318 (Miss. 2014) (cluster 2759935) — post-Leverock Supreme Court authority on the natural-parent presumption against a third party claiming in loco parentis status. It is the most recent statement of the rule the brief carries with 2009 and 2013 cases, and it is the case the brief should have used instead of Strickland v. Day, which it correctly rejected.
- Missing (for a later pass): In re Adoption of D.N.T., 843 So. 2d 690 (Miss. 2003) (cluster 7817176) — a leading Mississippi Supreme Court decision on termination in the adoption context and the natural-parent presumption. OUT-02 currently runs 1984 (Petit) to 2016 (Blakeney) with nothing from the 2003-2013 window on the private-adoption side.
- Missing (for a later pass): In re T.A.P., 742 So. 2d 1095 (Miss. 1999) — cited in G.Q.A. at 1101 for the best-interest prong of the termination analysis; a natural companion at OUT-02 and absent from the brief.

Section 43-21-353's reporting duty after 2000 — Noblitt (1995) is the only mandatory-reporting case in the brief and INP-01 is left with essentially one authority once the off-node entries are removed. If none exists, that should be stated as an express gap rather than papered over with E.K. and J.T.
