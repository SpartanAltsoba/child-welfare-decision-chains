## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **F.A. v. W.J.F.**, 280 N.J. Super. 570, 656 A.2d 43 (App. Div. 1995)
  Holding: The court held that the immunity in N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.13 for persons who report suspected child abuse must be broadly and liberally construed in favor of the reporter, barring the suit against them.
  Why here: F.A. is the controlling New Jersey construction of the statutory immunity that protects mandated reporters from suit.
  Quote: “We hold that immunity shall be broadly and liberally construed in favor of the reporting person.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1474169/fa-by-pa-v-wjf/
- **L.A. v. New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services**, 429 N.J. Super. 48, 56 A.3d 890 (App. Div. 2012)
  Holding: The court defined the standard governing a physician's statutory duty to report, holding 'reasonable cause to believe' exists when a probable inference from the available information is that the child's condition results from abuse.
  Why here: L.A. gives New Jersey's operative definition of the 'reasonable cause to believe' that triggers the duty to report.
  Quote: “The inference need not be the "most probable," but it must be more than speculation or suspicion.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7404636/la-v-new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services/

## INP-02 · Voluntary / Third-Party Report
- **F.A. v. W.J.F.**, 280 N.J. Super. 570, 656 A.2d 43 (App. Div. 1995)
  Holding: The court held that the immunity in N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.13 for persons who report suspected child abuse must be broadly and liberally construed in favor of the reporter, barring the suit against them.
  Why here: F.A. applies N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.13 immunity to any person who reports, not only designated professionals.
  Quote: “We hold that immunity shall be broadly and liberally construed in favor of the reporting person.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1474169/fa-by-pa-v-wjf/
- **L.A. v. New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services**, 429 N.J. Super. 48, 56 A.3d 890 (App. Div. 2012)
  Holding: The court defined the standard governing a physician's statutory duty to report, holding 'reasonable cause to believe' exists when a probable inference from the available information is that the child's condition results from abuse.
  Why here: L.A. explains that the reporting threshold is deliberately low so that potential cases reach the Division for investigation.
  Quote: “The inference need not be the "most probable," but it must be more than speculation or suspicion.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7404636/la-v-new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services/
- **Croft v. Westmoreland County Children and Youth Services**, 103 F.3d 1123 (3d Cir. 1997)
  Holding: The court reversed summary judgment for the caseworker, holding that forcing a father from his home on the basis of an uncorroborated anonymous tip violated the family's constitutional right to integrity because there were no objectively reasonable grounds to suspect abuse.
  Why here: Croft addresses the weight that may be given to an uncorroborated third-party report.
  Quote: “Even considered together, minor inconsistencies which provide no affirmative evidence of sexual abuse cannot alone establish the objectively reasonable grounds necessary to remove a family member from the family unit.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/732428/croft-v-westmoreland-county-children-and-youth-services/

## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral
- **State v. P.Z.**, 152 N.J. 86, 703 A.2d 901 (1997)
  Holding: The Court held that a father's statement to a DYFS caseworker during a Title 9 investigation was voluntary and not the product of custodial interrogation, so it was admissible in his criminal prosecution.
  Why here: P.Z. governs the coordination between child-protection caseworkers and prosecutors in a joint abuse investigation.
  Quote: “We conclude that defendant's statement to Kobran was not coerced in violation of his Fifth Amendment privilege against self-incrimination.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7406276/state-v-pz/

  Why here: Michaels governs joint law-enforcement and child-protection forensic interviewing of child witnesses.
  Quote: “If a child's recollection of events has been molded by an interrogation, that influence undermines the reliability of the child's responses as an accurate recollection of actual events.”

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **New Jersey Department of Children & Families v. I.S.**, 214 N.J. 8, 66 A.3d 1271 (2013)
  Holding: The Court held that a Title 9 action must be dismissed when no abuse or neglect is proved, but that jurisdiction may continue where the Division also proceeds under Title 30 to secure needed services for the child.
  Why here: I.S. sets the limits on what a court may order the Division to do when a case is referred for assessment.
  Quote: “when the Division brings its complaint also under the authority granted to it under Title 30, the court's jurisdiction may continue”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7409667/new-jersey-department-of-children-families-v-is/
- **New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services v. C.H.**, 428 N.J. Super. 40, 50 A.3d 93 (App. Div. 2012)
  Holding: The court held the trial judge's sua sponte dismissal of the abuse-or-neglect complaint mid-hearing violated due process and remanded, while affirming the dismissal that followed a noticed motion.
  Why here: C.H. describes the court's role and its limits once a child-protection matter is before it.
  Quote: “the court's sua sponte dismissal of the abuse or neglect complaint against C.H. was a due process violation necessitating a remand”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7404606/new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services-v-ch/

## INP-08 · Educational Disclosure
- **S.C. v. New Jersey Department of Children and Families**, No. A-57-18 (N.J. May 27, 2020)
  Holding: The Court upheld the Department's 'not established' investigative category but required improved notice, a credible-evidence basis, and an informal opportunity to respond, while rejecting any right to a contested OAL hearing.
  Why here: S.C. arises from an investigation of alleged corporal punishment reported through school and child-protection channels.
  Quote: “We reject that due process considerations require the Department to conduct an adjudicative contested case proceeding either internally or at the Office of Administrative Law for a "not established" finding.”

- **State v. Michaels**, 136 N.J. 299, 642 A.2d 1372 (1994)
  Holding: The Court held that coercive or suggestive interview techniques can so taint a child's statements that a pretrial hearing on reliability is required before the child's statements or testimony may be admitted.
  Why here: Michaels arose from disclosures in a day-care setting and shows how adult questioning can shape a child's report.
  Quote: “If a child's recollection of events has been molded by an interrogation, that influence undermines the reliability of the child's responses as an accurate recollection of actual events.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2009700/state-v-michaels/
- **In re Allegations of Physical Abuse at Blackacre Academy**, 304 N.J. Super. 168, 698 A.2d 1275 (App. Div. 1997)
  Holding: The court upheld the Institutional Abuse Investigation Unit's substantiated finding, holding the unit could report its results to the Department of Education and the affected parents and was not required to hold a hearing first.
  Why here: Blackacre governs investigations of abuse allegations against staff in school and institutional settings.
  Quote: “the IAIU was not required to conduct a formal or informal hearing before placing appellants' names in the Central Registry”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7402785/in-re-allegations-of-physical-abuse-at-blackacre-academy/
- **New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services v. P.W.R.**, 205 N.J. 17, 11 A.3d 844 (2011)
  Holding: The Court reversed the abuse-or-neglect finding, holding the record inadequate to establish a Title 9 violation and that an occasional slap to a teenager's face as discipline, causing no bruising or marks, is not 'excessive corporal punishment' under N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.21(c)(4)(b).
  Why here: P.W.R. arose from a report routed through school and family channels about slapping and is the substantive standard governing such school-sourced discipline referrals.
  Quote: “Limiting state involvement only to interference with excessive corporal punishment requires the exercise of judgment by reviewing courts before a finding of physical abuse is entered against a parent.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2423138/new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services-v-pwr/
- **New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services v. A.R.G.**, 179 N.J. 264, 845 A.2d 106 (2004)
  Holding: The Court affirmed as modified and remanded for de novo reconsideration, holding that 'aggravated circumstances' excusing the Division from reasonable reunification efforts under N.J.S.A. 30:4C-11.3 is a fact-specific inquiry and that a parent must first receive notice of the underlying facts and theory the State will rely on.
  Why here: A.R.G. begins with an anonymous call to a school nurse who verified the child's bruises and reported to DYFS, illustrating the school-based intake pathway.
  Quote: “Moreover, any circumstances that increase the severity of the abuse or neglect, or add to its injurious consequences, equates to "aggravated circumstances."”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1890630/new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services-v-arg/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **In re Guardianship of J.O.**, 327 N.J. Super. 304, 743 A.2d 341 (App. Div. 2000)
  Holding: The court affirmed termination, holding that ICWA's notice obligation was not triggered on this record and that no remedial program could have cured the father's sexual abuse of the children.
  Why here: J.O. is the published New Jersey decision on when ICWA notice and its heightened standards apply in a state child-welfare case.
  Quote: “The notice obligation is triggered when the court "knows or has reason to know that an Indian Child is involved" in custody proceedings.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1435852/in-re-guardianship-of-jo/
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield**, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held that 'domicile' under ICWA is a matter of uniform federal law and that the tribal court had exclusive jurisdiction over the adoption of children domiciled on the reservation.
  Why here: Holyfield is the Supreme Court's controlling decision on when ICWA strips a state court of jurisdiction over an Indian child.
  Quote: “In enacting the ICWA Congress confirmed that, in child custody proceedings involving Indian children domiciled on the reservation, tribal jurisdiction was exclusive as to the States.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112241/mississippi-band-of-choctaw-indians-v-holyfield/
- **Haaland v. Brackeen**, 599 U.S. 255 (2023)
  Holding: The Court affirmed Congress's constitutional authority to enact the Indian Child Welfare Act and reversed on the anticommandeering claims, vacating and remanding the equal-protection and nondelegation claims with instructions to dismiss for lack of standing.
  Why here: Brackeen is the controlling federal backdrop for any ICWA analysis today, confirming that ICWA validly binds state courts and agencies handling Indian child custody proceedings.
  Quote: “But the bottom line is that we reject all of petitioners' challenges to the statute, some on the merits and others for lack of standing.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406660/haaland-v-brackeen/

- **In re Guardianship of J.O.**, 327 N.J. Super. 304, 743 A.2d 341 (App. Div. 2000)
  Holding: The court affirmed termination, holding that ICWA's notice obligation was not triggered on this record and that no remedial program could have cured the father's sexual abuse of the children.

## PMC-04 · Fatality Review Commission
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services**, 489 U.S. 189 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held the Due Process Clause imposed no constitutional duty on the county to protect a child from his father's violence, even though the agency knew of the danger, absent a custodial or other special relationship.
  Why here: DeShaney explains why fatality accountability depends on review commissions and state law rather than federal constitutional suits.
  Quote: “the Due Process Clauses generally confer no affirmative right to governmental aid, even where such aid may be necessary to secure life, liberty, or property interests”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112202/deshaney-v-winnebago-cty-dept-of-social-servs/
- **New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services v. A.W.**, 103 N.J. 591, 512 A.2d 438 (1986)
  Holding: Reversing termination, the Court set out the four-part best-interests standard for terminating parental rights under N.J.S.A. 30:4C-15(c) and held the Division must prove it by clear and convincing evidence.
  Why here: A.W. illustrates judicial fatality review of agency records, criticizing illegible and inadequate Division case records.
  Quote: “The child's health and development have been or will be seriously impaired by the parental relationship.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1927603/new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services-v-aw/
- **L.A. v. New Jersey Division of Youth & Family Services**, 429 N.J. Super. 48, 56 A.3d 890 (App. Div. 2012)
  Holding: The court defined the standard governing a physician's statutory duty to report, holding 'reasonable cause to believe' exists when a probable inference from the available information is that the child's condition results from abuse.
  Why here: L.A. is a judicial post-mortem on missed reporting opportunities of the kind fatality reviews examine.
  Quote: “The inference need not be the "most probable," but it must be more than speculation or suspicion.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7404636/la-v-new-jersey-division-of-youth-family-services/
