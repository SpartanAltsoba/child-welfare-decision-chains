## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **Division of Family Services v. James**, 28 A.3d 480 (Del. Fam. Ct. 2009)
  Holding: The Family Court denied the Division's motion to be relieved of offering reunification services to a mother whose rights to a sibling had been involuntarily terminated, holding that the decision whether reasonable efforts are required rests with the court rather than the agency, and ordering reunification to remain the permanency goal.

  Holding: The Court reversed the termination of a young mother's parental rights and returned custody of her son to her, holding that requiring an unrepresented minor to sign a voluntary placement agreement as the price of housing assistance, followed by an ex parte custody order entered without the notice the rules required, fell below minimum due process, and that the agencies' failure to provide meaningful case plans or make reasonable efforts to prevent removal and reunify the family independently vitiated the judgment.

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl**, 570 U.S. 637 (2013)
  Holding: The Supreme Court reversed, holding that ICWA section 1912(f) does not bar termination where the parent never had custody, that section 1912(d)'s active-efforts requirement applies only where termination would precipitate the breakup of an existing Indian family, and that section 1915(a)'s placement preferences do not apply when no preferred party has sought to adopt.
  Why here: The governing federal construction of ICWA's active-efforts and placement-preference provisions, which supplies the standard for Delaware because no published Delaware ICWA decision was located.
  Quote: “Consistent with the statutory text, we hold that §1912(d) applies only in cases where an Indian family's "breakup" would be precipitated by the termination of the parent's rights.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/931616/adoptive-couple-v-baby-girl/
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield**, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)
  Holding: Reversing the Mississippi Supreme Court, the Court held that "domicile" in ICWA is a uniform federal standard rather than a matter of state law, that twins born off-reservation to reservation-domiciled parents were domiciled on the reservation, and that the tribal court therefore had exclusive jurisdiction under 25 U.S.C. section 1911(a), so the state adoption decree had to be vacated.
  Why here: The controlling federal decision on when ICWA strips a state court of jurisdiction altogether in favor of the tribal court, which Delaware courts must apply because Delaware has produced no ICWA case law of its own.
  Quote: “Tribal jurisdiction under § 1911(a) was not meant to be defeated by the actions of individual members of the tribe”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112241/mississippi-band-of-choctaw-indians-v-holyfield/

- Missing (for a later pass): Division of Family Services v. T.W., 854 A.2d 844 (Del. Fam. Ct. 2004) — a published Delaware Family Court decision adjudicating a Petition for Substantiation and placement of both parents on the Child Protection Registry alongside a dependency/neglect petition. It directly fills DEC-03, which currently has no Delaware authority at all, and it refutes note 5's assertion that a Child Protection Registry / substantiation search returned zero Delaware results. See also Division of Family Services v. S.P., 120 A.3d 26 (Del. Fam. Ct. 2015).
- Missing (for a later pass): Newmark v. Williams, 588 A.2d 1108 (Del. 1991) — the Delaware Supreme Court's leading decision on the State's power to take custody and compel a child's medical treatment over the parents' objection, balancing parental autonomy, the child's interest and the proposed treatment's efficacy. ACT-06 and INP-07 currently rest on Hunt (end-of-life de-escalation) plus a records-contempt case; Newmark is the binding Delaware authority on court-ordered medical intervention short of that.
- Missing (for a later pass): Daber v. Division of Child Protective Services, 470 A.2d 723 (Del. 1983) — the decision Burns, Hanks and Stevens all cite for the proposition that termination must be in the child's best interests and that the 13 Del. C. sec. 722(a) factors apply to termination proceedings. OUT-02 cites the cases that quote Daber without citing Daber itself.
- Missing (for a later pass): Patricia A.F. v. James R.F., 451 A.2d 830 (Del. 1982) (en banc) — the Delaware decision establishing that a parent's rights may be terminated only on a showing by clear and convincing evidence (overruling In re Five Minor Children in part). It is the Delaware root of what the brief cites Santosky and Powell for at OUT-02 and DEC-05, and Burns and Hanks both cite it by name.
- Missing (for a later pass): Betty J.B. v. Division of Social Services, 460 A.2d 528 (Del. 1983) — the source of Delaware's appellate standard of review in child-welfare appeals (findings that are 'the product of an orderly and logical deductive process' will not be disturbed), which the brief attributes to Harrison dicta at FAIL-01. Hanks cites Betty J.B. for exactly that standard.
- Missing (for a later pass): Blessing v. Freestone, 520 U.S. 329 (1997) — the Supreme Court's framework for when a Spending Clause child-welfare plan requirement is privately enforceable under section 1983, and the necessary modern companion to Suter at FAIL-03 and PMC-01; Suter alone overstates the bar without Blessing's three-part test.
- Missing (for a later pass): M.L.B. v. S.L.J., 519 U.S. 102 (1996) — holding that a State may not condition appellate review of a parental-termination decree on the parent's ability to pay record preparation fees. It is the federal access-to-review authority missing from DEC-05 and FAIL-01, and the natural companion to Lassiter and Brown in a brief written for families.
