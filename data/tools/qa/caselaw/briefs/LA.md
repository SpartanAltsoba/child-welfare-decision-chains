## INP-01 · Mandatory Report
- **Todd v. State Through Department of Social Services, Office of Community Services**, 699 So. 2d 35 (La. 1997)
  Holding: The Louisiana Supreme Court reversed judgments against a caseworker and the State for a child's suicide after an abuse investigation, holding the manner of investigation is discretionary and that the mother failed to prove the suicide was reasonably foreseeable or causally linked to the caseworker's conduct.
  Why here: Sets out La. Ch.C. arts. 611 and 612, the immunity and priority-setting provisions that frame every mandated report.
  Quote: “The manner in which the investigation is conducted is one of discretion, unless the investigation is so incomplete that it could not be found to be thorough.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1723601/todd-v-state-through-dept-of-social-services/
- **State ex rel. L.B.**, 986 So. 2d 62 (La. 2008)
  Holding: The Louisiana Supreme Court reversed the juvenile court's refusal to find the infant a child in need of care, holding that expert medical evidence of repeated non-accidental trauma established reasonable grounds and that the agency need only prove the petition's allegations by a preponderance.
  Why here: Arose from a verified complaint supported by treating and staff physician reports, the classic mandated medical report.

## INP-04 · Court-Ordered Referral
- **State ex rel. L.C.B.**, 805 So. 2d 159 (La. 2002)
  Holding: The Louisiana Supreme Court held the juvenile court exceeded its authority by ordering the department to move a child adjudicated in need of care into a certified foster home, because La. Ch.C. art. 672(A) gives the department sole authority over placement, with judicial control exercised through approval and review of the case plan.
  Why here: Marks the outer limit of a judge's power to direct agency action in a dependency case.
  Quote: “the court was without authority to order the child's movement to a certified foster home”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7806485/state-ex-rel-lcb/
- **State ex rel. K.K.**, 243 So. 3d 1155 (La. Ct. App. 2d Cir. 2017)
  Holding: The court of appeal affirmed the denial of the mother's motion to modify a disposition placing the child with her adult half-brother and affirmed the appointment of the half-brother as guardian, finding no manifest error in the conclusion that guardianship was the most appropriate permanent plan.
  Why here: Shows the juvenile court's continuing authority to revisit disposition on its own motion or on any party's motion.
  Quote: “The trial court may modify a judgment of disposition on its own motion or on the motion of the district attorney, the department, the child or his/her parents.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7677880/state-ex-rel-kk/

## INP-05 · Self-Report / Guardian Request
- **State ex rel. A.T.**, 936 So. 2d 79 (La. 2006)
  Holding: The Louisiana Supreme Court affirmed reversal of a termination judgment, holding that where reunification is the permanent plan the department must make reasonable efforts to help the parent remove the impediments to reunification - here to obtain suitable and affordable housing - before seeking termination under La. Ch.C. art. 1015(5).
  Why here: The case began when the mother herself told the agency she could no longer care for her children, the paradigm guardian-request intake.
  Quote: “the duty to make reasonable efforts to remove the impediments to reunification does not necessarily stop once the children are removed from the parent's custody”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7840865/state-ex-rel-at/
- **State ex rel. P.B.**, 154 So. 3d 806 (La. Ct. App. 2d Cir. 2014)
  Holding: The court of appeal affirmed the change of the child's permanent plan to adoption after three adjudications, nine placements and nearly five years in foster care, holding the court must select the plan most appropriate and in the child's best interest and that the department had exceeded reasonable efforts at reunification.
  Why here: The parents repeatedly sought and received services over five years without achieving stability, the outer limit of voluntary engagement.
  Quote: “The court is required to determine the permanent plan for the child that is most appropriate and in the best interest of the child.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5004936/state-ex-rel-pb/

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the reasonable-efforts requirement of 42 U.S.C. section 671(a)(15) confers no privately enforceable right under section 1983 and creates no implied private cause of action, leaving enforcement to the Secretary's funding-review machinery.
  Why here: Explains why federal oversight of state child-welfare performance runs through administrative plan review rather than private suit.
  Quote: “the "reasonable efforts" language does not unambiguously confer an enforceable right upon the Act's beneficiaries”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **New Orleans Bulldog Society v. Louisiana Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals**, 222 So. 3d 679 (La. 2017)
  Holding: The Louisiana Supreme Court held that a private nonprofit performing a governmental function under a cooperative endeavor agreement and paid with public money is an instrumentality of the municipality subject to the Public Records Law, and that contractual reporting requirements cannot substitute for public-records access.
  Why here: Means privatized child-welfare contractors performing state functions can be compelled to open their records to oversight.
  Quote: “the requirement for access to public records cannot be circumscribed by contract”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/4388621/new-orleans-bulldog-society-v-louisiana-society-for-the-prevention-of/
- **Health and Hospital Corporation of Marion Cty. v. Talevski**, 599 U.S. 166 (2023)
  Holding: The Court held that section 1983 is presumptively available to enforce individual rights unambiguously conferred by Spending Clause legislation unless the statute's own remedial scheme forecloses it, and that the FNHRA provisions at issue create such enforceable rights.
  Why here: It confirms that federal oversight of a spending-program state agency runs through administrative machinery unless the statute unambiguously confers an individual right.
  Quote: “In that respect, our precedent sets a demanding bar: Statutory provisions must unambiguously confer individual federal rights”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9404968/health-and-hospital-corporation-of-marion-cty-v-talevski/
- **Gonzaga University v. Doe**, 536 U.S. 273 (2002)
  Holding: The Court held that FERPA's nondisclosure provisions confer no individual rights enforceable under section 1983, and that nothing less than an unambiguously conferred right will support a section 1983 action to enforce a federal spending statute.
  Why here: It explains why statutes with an aggregate, funding-directed focus are enforced by federal administrative review rather than by the individuals they benefit.
  Quote: “We now reject the notion that our cases permit anything short of an unambiguously conferred right to support a cause of action brought under § 1983”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/121157/gonzaga-university-v-doe/

## INP-11 · Federal / Tribal Jurisdiction Trigger
- **Adoptive Couple v. Baby Girl**, 570 U.S. 637 (2013)
  Holding: The Court held that ICWA section 1912(f) does not bar termination where the Indian parent never had custody, and that section 1912(d)'s remedial-services requirement applies only where the termination would precipitate the breakup of an Indian family.
  Why here: Governs how a Louisiana court must apply ICWA's heightened standards once an Indian child is identified, and narrows when active-efforts proof is required.
  Quote: “we hold that §1912(d) applies only in cases where an Indian family's "breakup" would be precipitated by the termination of the parent's rights”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/931616/adoptive-couple-v-baby-girl/
- **Hampton v. J.A.L.**, 658 So. 2d 331 (La. Ct. App. 2d Cir. 1995)
  Holding: Applying the judicially created existing-Indian-family exception, the court of appeal held ICWA inapplicable to the adoption of a child who had never lived in an Indian environment, reversed the portion of the judgment allowing the Indian mother to withdraw her consent, and left custody with the non-Indian adoptive couple.
  Why here: Is the only published Louisiana appellate decision retrieved applying ICWA, and it must be read against 25 C.F.R. 23.103(c), which rejects the existing-Indian-family exception it applies.
  Quote: “In sum, we find that the adoption of A.S.L. will not cause the breakup of an existing Indian family or removal of a child from an Indian environment.”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/1138518/hampton-v-jal/
- **Haaland v. Brackeen**, 599 U.S. 255 (2023)
  Holding: The Court upheld the Indian Child Welfare Act against Article I and anticommandeering challenges and vacated the equal-protection and nondelegation rulings for want of standing, leaving ICWA and its placement preferences fully in force in state child-welfare proceedings.
  Why here: This is the current Supreme Court decision settling ICWA's constitutional footing, so a Louisiana court identifying an Indian child must apply the Act and its placement preferences without regard to the constitutional objections rejected here.
  Quote: “the bottom line is that we reject all of petitioners' challenges to the statute, some on the merits and others for lack of standing”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9406660/haaland-v-brackeen/
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield**, 490 U.S. 30 (1989)
  Holding: The Court held that domicile under ICWA section 1911(a) is a matter of uniform federal law, that twins born off-reservation to reservation-domiciled parents were domiciled on the reservation, and that the tribal court therefore had exclusive jurisdiction, so the state adoption decree was vacated.
  Why here: It establishes the federal domicile rule and exclusive tribal jurisdiction that a Louisiana court must apply at the moment an Indian child is identified, and it cannot be displaced by state-law notions of abandonment or residence.
  Quote: “We therefore think it beyond dispute that Congress intended a uniform federal law of domicile for the ICWA”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112241/mississippi-band-of-choctaw-indians-v-holyfield/

- Added Wooley v. City of Baton Rouge, 211 F.3d 913 (5th Cir. 2000), for INP-03, INP-12, DEC-04 and FAIL-02: it is the Fifth Circuit's Louisiana child-seizure decision, holding that absent a warrant or probable cause officials must have some evidence of danger before taking a child, and that La. Ch.C. arts. 621 and 736 did not authorize the taking where no imminent threat existed. It closes the hole flagged in the prior pass and corrects the earlier note.
- Added Lassiter v. Department of Social Servs. of Durham Cty., 452 U.S. 18 (1981), for OUT-05 and FAIL-01: it is the controlling federal decision on appointed counsel for indigent parents in termination proceedings and supplies the case-by-case due-process test that State ex rel. D.A.G. and La. Ch.C. art. 1016 operate within. It was not mapped at OUT-02 because that node is already at the six-case ceiling and Lassiter's contribution is procedural rather than evidentiary.
- Added Haaland v. Brackeen, 599 U.S. 255 (2023), for INP-11: it is the Supreme Court's current ICWA decision, rejecting the Article I and anticommandeering challenges and vacating the equal-protection and nondelegation rulings for lack of standing, so ICWA and its placement preferences remain fully binding on Louisiana courts.
- Added Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians v. Holyfield, 490 U.S. 30 (1989), for INP-11 and FAIL-05: it supplies ICWA's purposes, the uniform federal law of domicile, and exclusive tribal jurisdiction under 25 U.S.C. 1911(a), and it is the authority that most directly undercuts the existing-Indian-family exception applied in Hampton v. J.A.L. It should be cited with 25 C.F.R. 23.103(c) wherever Hampton appears.
- Added Health and Hospital Corporation of Marion Cty. v. Talevski, 599 U.S. 166 (2023), for FAIL-03, INP-09 and PMC-01: it is the modern framework governing when a Spending Clause statute creates a right enforceable under section 1983, and it is the case that must be applied before Suter v. Artist M. is deployed as the last word on federal child-welfare enforcement.
- Added Gonzaga University v. Doe, 536 U.S. 273 (2002), for FAIL-03 and INP-09: it states the rights-creating test - nothing short of an unambiguously conferred right will support a section 1983 action - that Talevski applies and that governs whether a state's federal child-welfare compliance failure is litigable by the children it affects.
- Added State ex rel. J.M., 837 So. 2d 1247 (La. 2003), for OUT-02 and OUT-01: it is the Louisiana Supreme Court's leading statement of the child's competing interest in permanency and of the standard governing involuntary termination, it is quoted in State in the Interest of C.F., and it holds that permanent placement does not include leaving children indefinitely in foster care.
- Added State in the Interest of S.M., 719 So. 2d 445 (La. 1998), for OUT-01: it holds that reunification cannot be approved as the permanent plan without record evidence of the parent's actual reformation, and it is the decision State ex rel. P.B. relies on for the child's right to a stable permanent home. Its best-interest language also bears on OUT-02, but that node is at the six-case ceiling.
- Added In re Adoption of B.G.S., 556 So. 2d 545 (La. 1990), for OUT-02 and OUT-05: it is the Louisiana Supreme Court's foundational decision on notice, the opportunity to be heard, and parental rights in the surrender/adoption context, holding La. R.S. 9:422.8 unconstitutional as applied to an unwed father with a protected interest. It is the actual source of the due-process rule the brief previously attributed only to In re A.J.F.
- Added Title Research Corp. v. Rausch, 450 So. 2d 933 (La. 1984), for PMC-05, PMC-03, PMC-06 and FAIL-06: it is the foundational Louisiana Public Records Law decision holding that access is a fundamental constitutional right, that the provision is construed liberally, and that any doubt is resolved in favor of the public. Landis v. Moreau cites it as the source of the rule the brief quotes Landis for.
- Added Wernecke v. Garcia, 591 F.3d 386 (5th Cir. 2009), for ACT-06, DEC-04, FAIL-02 and INP-07: it is a medical-neglect case in which the State took custody of a child with Hodgkin's disease so refused treatment could be given, it adapts the Gates exigency factors to a child-endangerment investigation, and it holds that Roe and Wooley had already clearly established the imminent-danger requirement.
- Added Parham v. J. R., 442 U.S. 584 (1979), for ACT-06 and ACT-03: it is the controlling federal standard for authorizing immediate psychiatric or psychological care for a child, including a child who is a ward of the State, requiring an independent inquiry by a neutral factfinder and periodic review but not a formal adversarial hearing.
