## INP-03 · Law-Enforcement Referral

  Why here: The Idaho rule officers rely on when a welfare call escalates into entry and a child-protection referral.

  Why here: Controls Ninth Circuit law when police, rather than a caseworker, take protective custody of a child on an uninvestigated third-party tip.

## INP-09 · Systemic Audit / Oversight Trigger
- **Suter v. Artist M.**, 503 U.S. 347 (1992)
  Holding: The Court held that the 'reasonable efforts' provision of the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act, 42 U.S.C. § 671(a)(15), creates no right enforceable by private plaintiffs, either directly under the Act or through 42 U.S.C. § 1983.
  Why here: Explains why systemic child-welfare deficiencies are addressed through federal administrative oversight rather than private suit.
  Quote: “We hold that the Act does not create an enforceable right on behalf of respondents”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/112712/suter-v-artist-m/
- **Blessing v. Freestone**, 520 U.S. 329 (1997)
  Holding: The Court held that a state's obligation to operate a federally funded program in 'substantial compliance' with its plan is not intended to benefit individual beneficiaries and creates no enforceable right, and that courts must examine each statutory provision separately rather than take a blanket approach.
  Why here: Systemic performance shortfalls identified by federal audit are addressed by the funding agency, not by individual suit.
  Quote: “The Court of Appeals erred not only in finding that individuals have an enforceable right to substantial compliance”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/118101/blessing-v-freestone/

  Why here: Idaho's foundational holding that ICWA is triggered by the child's status, not by the family's living arrangements.

  Why here: Holds that ICWA's consent formalities in 25 U.S.C. § 1913(a) displace state law whenever they give the parent greater protection, per § 1921.
  Quote: “We hold that none of the consents to terminate parental rights complied with ICWA's statutory formalities, and that the order of June 1990 terminating the mother's parental rights is invalid”
  https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5341698/doe-v-roe/

  Why here: The controlling Idaho authority on the burden of proof for ICWA active efforts once a case is identified as involving an Indian child.

  Why here: Allocates the burden in an Idaho public-records suit: the withholding agency must show cause and prove the records fit a narrowly construed exemption.

  Why here: The foundational Idaho Public Records Act decision establishing that all public records are presumed open and that exemptions are narrowly construed.
  Quote: “In deciding that the administrative review must be disclosed, we are also influenced by the presumption that all public records are open unless expressly provided otherwise by statute”
